# Classroom activity proposals — image and media pathways

2026-10-20. Durations are suggestions for selecting an activity within an
existing session. They are not an added timetable, official hour allocation
or new assessment weight. Two labs can feed an existing portfolio entry;
the remaining activities are optional preparation or transfer exercises.

## Shared evidence record

Each group uses one row per claim: case ID; exact source URL and date;
image/version or video timecode actually viewed; observable decision;
documented finding; interpretation; strongest counter-reading; proposed
change; remaining uncertainty. Mark unknown consent, pay or reception as
unknown. No number is assigned to a person's dignity.

## B1 — prepare an inquiry

### A1. Observation, interpretation, verdict — 20 minutes

Pairs receive a short text-only case and its primary decision. Sort six
statements into observable feature, source finding, inference or proposal;
then challenge one ambiguous placement. Output: a corrected annotation
table with one weak inference crossed out and explained. Image students
focus on framing; media students focus on caption, audience and placement.
Feedback: can another reader retrace the evidence without sharing the
student's taste? This is a classification exercise, not a vote on morality.

### A2. Read the dissent before the judgement — 25 minutes

Use the revised FKA twigs ruling in the shared guide. Assign the advertiser's
response, the regulator's reasoning and the represented person's agency as
separate documentary questions. Each group writes a claim and a sentence
that would make them revise it. Output: a 150-word position with two source
locators. No role requires a student to perform an identity or disclose a
personal experience. Feedback: are objectification and placement separated?

## B2 — exactly two portfolio-bound labs

### Lab I. Change the image decision — 45–60 minutes

**Fit:** CD I images/references and CD II retouching, avatar or portfolio work.
Choose a licensed/self-made adult portrait, garment image or nonfigurative
layout, or use an equivalent text-only shot plan. Annotate one decision
about crop, gaze, pose, scale, context or retouching. Develop two revisions
that preserve the communication brief but change how agency is represented;
implement one and preserve the original. Deliver the before/after or two
shot plans, the source record, an edit log and a 120-word explanation of
what the revision cannot establish. Feedback: the proposed mechanism must
be visible and argued; more clothing, more faces or a smiling subject is
not automatically a better solution. **Provenance:** method transfer from
the visual discourse study; effectiveness untested.

### Lab M. Change the circulation decision — 45–60 minutes

**Fit:** NM digital communication/advertising and CD II video/web portfolio.
Use a guide case as the analytical reference and a fictional brand brief
for production. Draw advertiser → publisher/platform → audience → response,
adding the represented person and the people who supply labour. Change
one mechanism: an unsupported promise, a patronising narration, a pressure
to buy, an uncredited participation request or a placement choice. Produce
a six-frame storyboard or a three-post plan with captions, a bounded
claim and an accessible alternative. Deliver the original/revised flow,
source record, two alternatives, chosen version and a reasoned success
criterion beyond engagement. No public posting is required. Feedback:
does the change address the mechanism rather than replace the slogan?
**Provenance:** GD16 production/communication linkage, Abidin visibility
labour and UNEP guidance; no measured impact claimed.

## B3 — individual reasoning and transfer

### A3. The production brief outside the picture — 30 minutes

Adapt GD16. A fictional textile company must choose between two briefs
with incomplete information about wages, materials and a sustainability
claim. Students identify what must be verified, write one supportable
message and refuse one unsupported promise. Output: a one-page decision
record and three procurement questions. Feedback: a respectful visual is
not treated as evidence of fair production. No invented wage, audit or
certification data. Extension: teams compare their conflicting priorities.

### A4. Voiceover without the hierarchy — 30 minutes

After reading the primary research on the chopsticks controversy, students
write two short voiceover/storyboard alternatives for a fictional cultural
collaboration. One keeps the commercial objective; another changes the
brief itself. Output: two versions and a note explaining who retains
authority and who would need to be consulted. Do not imitate an accent,
ask students to speak for a whole culture or re-enact the offending joke.
Feedback: can they name what is learned from the case without assuming
their own version is endorsed by the people represented?

### A5. A claim the audience can check — 25 minutes

Use the ACM commitments as documentary background. Rewrite a fictional
vague environmental label using only a supplied evidence card. If the
evidence does not support a benefit, withdraw the label. Output: the
revised claim, its visible qualifier and a link/locator to the relevant
evidence. Feedback: can the viewer tell the product-level claim from a
company-level aspiration? The source is a Dutch case, not a substitute for
checking the applicable law of every market.

### A6. One image, two readings — 25 minutes

Students compare a composition-based reading with a historically situated
reading. Use an instructor-made diagram or a licensed adult image rather
than copying a sensitive advertisement. Output: two annotations and one
question neither reading can answer from the image. Feedback: does the
historical lens add an evidenced connection rather than assign a culture
or an identity from appearance? Browne supplies a critical historical
frame, not a universal diagnostic key.

### A7. Engagement is not the same as a reason — 20 minutes

Students receive a fictional dashboard with high views, purchases,
complaints and incomplete provenance. Write what each measure can and
cannot establish, then propose a nonintrusive way to learn about one
missing issue. Output: a metric/claim table. Feedback: reactions and
commercial outcomes are not treated as measures of dignity. No real
student analytics, sentiment classification or sensitive data is collected.

### A8. Exit: revise your own claim — 10 minutes

Individually write a 100-word judgement, underline the source-backed part,
mark the interpretation and state one fact that would change the judgement.
Output: a revisable claim, not a declaration of correct values. Feedback:
an explicit uncertainty and a serious counter-reading can strengthen the
work. This is suitable as an ungraded exit ticket or existing portfolio note.

## Formative feedback

| Dimension | Evidence sought | Revision prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source discipline | Finding and inference clearly distinguished | What source would let a sceptical reader check this? |
| Agency and difference | Specific mechanism; no ranking of persons | Whose choice, history or capacity to refuse remains invisible? |
| Editorial craft | Formal/media change linked to the mechanism | What exactly changed, and why should it matter? |
| Accountability | Process record, counter-reading and limits | What does your proposal still fail to establish? |

Course AI declaration applies to any submitted portfolio entry; declaring no
AI use is valid. Record material help with text, translation, imagery or
editing. All proposed timings, prompts, feedback criteria and safeguards
are editorial adaptations rather than empirically validated procedures.
