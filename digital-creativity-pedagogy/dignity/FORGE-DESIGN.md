## Backward design

**Desired result:** students can defend and revise an ethical interpretation
of a fashion communication decision without substituting taste, outrage,
similarity or a brand's intention for evidence.

**Acceptable evidence:** a short annotated source record, a claim with a
counter-reading, two revision options, one implemented image/media revision,
and a process note specifying what the intervention cannot establish.

This adopts the design discipline required by curriculum-forger; it does not
publish a new claim that this particular guide is an effective intervention.

## B1 / B2 / B3

| Surface | Bloom process × knowledge | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 concepts and worked distinction | Analyse × conceptual | Separate observation, interpretation and normative judgement |
| B2 image/media labs, exactly two | Create × procedural | Produce and document an editorial revision under a real brief |
| B3 individual transfer/problem | Evaluate × metacognitive | Defend a judgement, identify its weak point and revise it after new evidence |

The two B2 labs are contextualised portfolio work. B3 is individual reasoning,
not another group production task. The menu contains optional variants;
students are not assigned all variants by the existence of this guide.

## Inquiry and load

5E is used as an organising analogy: engage with a documented decision;
explore its exact artefact; explain competing readings; elaborate through a
revision; evaluate the claim and its limits. No efficacy claim is attached.

Intrinsic load: reading an image and a regulator/research text together.
Extraneous load to reduce: long legal prose, unstable videos, mixed source
versions and simultaneous demands to learn a new editing package. Provide a
short text-only case and one worked annotation before asking for production.
Germane work sought: the student's explicit connection between evidence,
formal choice and counterargument. No additional software is required.

## Critical moment detection

Apparatus: framing/cropping/retouching and publishing/calls to action.

- Framing selects who acts and who is looked at: representation and agency.
- Retouching and templates can impose a bodily norm: power and difference.
- Posting and engagement requests redistribute labour and attention:
  political economy, consent and platform governance.
- A sustainability label can abbreviate a complex supply chain into an
  untestable promise: epistemic authority and accountability.
- A critique performed as a compulsory confession can reproduce the
  hierarchy it challenges: classroom power and the right to refuse.

B1 frames those relations. B2 constrains the redesign to a documented
decision and requires two alternatives. B3 asks for a judgement that survives
counterevidence rather than one that matches the teacher's preferences.

## Facilitation decisions

No personal dignity stories required; no ranking, diagnosing or comparison of
students' bodies; no recording of sensitive experiences; no compulsory public
posting. A text-only documentary case or fictional brand brief is equivalent.
These are explicit course-design choices, including departures from the
supplied facilitator's sharing instruction, not purported statutory rules.
