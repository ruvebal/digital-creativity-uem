# Critical field/subfield layer

This is the reusable critical-thinking overlay for the 15 CD I/CD II session
prompts. It makes critical work concrete: a field tension, a situated question,
one verified perspective, and an open living prompt that can change as the
class encounters new evidence.

## Shared method

For each session, ask:

1. What does this practice make visible, and what does it hide?
2. Who controls the tools, data, image, body, archive, platform, or audience?
3. Who benefits, who absorbs cost or risk, and whose knowledge is missing?
4. What evidence would make us revise the first interpretation?

Do not reduce the exercise to “technology is good/bad.” Name the relation being
redistributed: attention, authorship, labour, resources, access, identity,
visibility, or authority. One student contribution should be a counterexample
or absent perspective, not only agreement with the teacher.

## Unit lenses and living prompts

| Unit | Critical field lens | Open living prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I.1 | An image is never only a file; venue, audience, and circulation distribute authority. | When the same image travels between editorial, campaign, archive, and classroom, who gets to define its meaning? |
| I.2 | Tool fluency can privilege the student with better hardware, software access, or prior training. | When does a “clean” digital line describe craft, and when does it hide a particular tool, labour, or default aesthetic? |
| I.3 | Colour is technical and cultural; displays, profiles, lighting, and contrast can include or exclude viewers. | Whose perception is treated as the default when a palette is called accurate, beautiful, or accessible? |
| I.4 | Effects can clarify material evidence or manufacture a more desirable body, garment, or world. | At what point does transformation become a claim that requires disclosure, consent, or refusal? |
| I.5 | Spatial representation is shaped by conventions, software defaults, and whose bodies/forms are used as examples. | Which forms become easy to model, and which remain difficult or absent in our spatial vocabulary? |
| I.6 | Dematerialisation may reduce samples while relocating energy, labour, and overproduction pressures. | What exactly has been reduced when a physical sample becomes a digital one, and what has merely moved elsewhere? |
| I.7 | Reference boards can turn archives and cultural knowledge into uncredited extraction. | How can a reference remain useful while preserving the source’s context, agency, and right not to be reused? |
| I.8 | Motion attracts attention but can also accelerate consumption, hide labour, or exclude people sensitive to movement. | Who is the rhythm designed for, and what does the loop make us overlook? |
| I.9 | Still-life composition turns objects, materials, and value into a hierarchy of attention. | Which objects become desirable or disposable through the frame, and who benefits from that arrangement? |
| II.1 | Retouching distributes authority over bodies, garments, truth claims, and professional appearance. | Who has the right to decide that an image needs correction, and what must remain visible after the edit? |
| II.2 | Avatars can expand identity play while reproducing body, cultural, biometric, or ownership defaults. | What kind of agency does the avatar give its maker, and which identities does the system make difficult to imagine? |
| II.3 | Digital experience design choreographs attention and can confuse engagement with learning or value. | What does the visitor gain from the interaction, and what responsibility is shifted onto the visitor? |
| II.4 | Fashion film packages identity, desire, labour, and brand power through selection and rhythm. | Which realities are edited out so that the brand’s desired world feels coherent? |
| II.5 | A portfolio is both self-presentation and evidence; platforms reward some forms of visibility over others. | What should a portfolio prove beyond polish, and who decides which proof counts? |
| II.6 | Immersive novelty can increase attention while shifting privacy, access, energy, and interpretive work to audiences. | When does an added layer deepen relation to the garment, and when does it merely make the viewer perform the technology? |

## Verified quotation bank

Use one short passage, then state its boundary. These are page-verified and
evaluator-safe in the current Ahmes matrix:

- **Volume / sustainability:** “digital tools alone cannot overcome fashion’s
  systemic drivers of overproduction and rapid trend turnover” (Coats 2026, 8).
  This supports a critical discussion of relocated costs; it is not proof of a
  general sustainability effect or of this lesson sequence.
- **Authorship / student voice:** “The goal is to ensure GenAI strengthens, not
  weakens, student voice and design identity” (Anwar 2025, 9). This supports an
  authorship guardrail; it does not validate a particular avatar or portfolio
  method.
- **Human–AI relation:** Rizzi describes GenAI as a tool that can “augment human
  perception and creativity” (Rizzi 2025, 10). This supports a complement—not
  replacement—frame; it does not establish that generated output is original
  or educationally effective.

If a stronger critical source is found through Athanor, resolve the complete
Ahmes node and require `evaluator_safe=yes` before adding it to this bank. The
current algorithmic-bias and digital-utopianism candidates are retained as
discovery leads or private gaps when their resolver metadata is not safe; they
must not become public author-date citations.

## Living-prompt record

End every session with three instructor notes:

```yaml
prompt_status: open
current_question: "..."
evidence_added: "source, observation, process trace, or counterexample"
absent_perspective: "..."
next_revisit: "unit/session milestone"
```

This is a pedagogical trace, not a student research dataset. Do not collect,
retain, or analyse personal responses beyond the ordinary teaching need unless
the separate DPO and consent gates are complete.
