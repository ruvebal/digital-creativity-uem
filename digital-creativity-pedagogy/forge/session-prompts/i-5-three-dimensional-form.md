## Context & learning goals

Move from silhouette and plane to axis, proportion, section, and volume. Students
will translate a 2D reference into three views, identify where a representation
loses spatial information, and create a simple digital form study without
confusing software fluency with form understanding.

## Session A: From plane to volume (2 hours)

| Time | Teacher move |
| --- | --- |
| 00–10 | Rotate a simple object while hiding one view; ask what the missing view must contain. |
| 10–30 | Explain axis, proportion, silhouette, plane, section, perspective, orthographic views. |
| 30–45 | Screen a rights-cleared 2D-to-3D fashion or object-form demonstration; annotate spatial cues. |
| 45–55 | Compare an aligned three-view drawing with a contradictory one. |
| 55–65 | Demo a 2D reference → blockout → three-view check, narrating each spatial decision. |
| 65–100 | Studio sprint: sketch → primitive volume → simple digital 3D (lesson B2 deliverable). |
| 100–113 | Individual B3, no AI: follow lesson page — projection fold, mock-up error, draped pattern. |
| 113–120 | Gallery walk and one “spatial evidence” note per team. |

## Screening & analysis

- **Video:** process demonstration with a clear start/end; ask students to
  mark when the representation becomes testable from another view.
- **Stills:** orthographic exemplar, ambiguous drawing, and fashion-form
  reference; focus on evidence, not polish.
- **Graphic:** `reference → axis → silhouette → planes → views → blockout`,
  with a tactile paper fallback.

## Studio sprint

Deliver the lesson B2 sequence: sketch, primitive-volume study, simple digital 3D
representation, piece ID, process folder, and one-paragraph 2D-read reflection.
Optional stretch: three annotated orthographic views if time allows.

## Individual problem

Follow the lesson page B3 items (authoritative for assessment):

1. **Diagnostic, no AI:** given three 2D projections, identify which stated 3D volume they could not fold into and why.
2. Name the specific inconsistency in a rendered mock-up with a visible form error.
3. Without any tool, describe how a flat pattern piece becomes a rounded draped form — at least two things that change.

## Follow-up

## Critical field lens & open living prompt

**Field tension:** spatial conventions and software defaults make some bodies,
materials, and forms easier to represent than others.

**Critical perspective:** What does the chosen view make legible, and which form
is being treated as the unspoken standard?

**Open living prompt:** Which forms become easy to model, and which remain
difficult or absent in our spatial vocabulary?

**Bring forward:** one missing view, atypical form, or changed convention.
