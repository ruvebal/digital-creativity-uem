## Context & learning goals

Use the still life as a controlled laboratory for object relation, light, edge,
material, and hierarchy. Students will compose a small digital still life,
explain three visual priorities, test an alternate crop, and retain a process
record rather than submitting only a polished image.

## Session A: Arrange, light, frame (2 hours)

| Time | Teacher move |
| --- | --- |
| 00–10 | Rearrange three objects live; ask when the composition became intentional. |
| 10–30 | Explain hierarchy, negative space, scale, overlap, value, material, and focal path. |
| 30–45 | Screen a licensed tabletop/photography process video; students map object-light-camera decisions. |
| 45–55 | Compare a balanced exemplar with a visually noisy counterexample. |
| 55–65 | Demo capture or digital build, crop variants, and an object/credit ledger. |
| 65–100 | Studio sprint: make one still life (lesson B2) with genuine I.1–I.4 cross-references. |
| 100–113 | Individual B3, no AI: follow lesson page — too-heavy diagnostic, hand-written balance note, synthesis check. |
| 113–120 | Silent gallery walk and one evidence-based critique per student. |

## Screening & analysis

- **Video:** tabletop lighting, compositing, or still-life process clip; note
  where material evidence is created rather than merely described.
- **Stills:** hierarchy exemplar, cluttered counterexample, and tactile/material
  close-up with clear attribution.
- **Graphic:** value/scale/overlap triangle with `object → light → frame → viewer`
  and an accessible checklist.

## Studio sprint

Submit a still life, process note with balance decisions and genuine I.1–I.4
cross-references, object/asset credits, and a 100-word rationale for hierarchy,
material, and audience. Offer a verbal description and high-contrast version for the critique.

## Critical field lens & open living prompt

**Field tension:** still-life composition turns objects, materials, and value
into a hierarchy of attention and desire.

**Critical perspective:** Which objects become desirable or disposable through
the frame, and who benefits from that arrangement?

**Open living prompt:** What does the still life make valuable, and what labour
or material history disappears behind its calm surface?

**Bring forward:** one object history, material consequence, or alternate framing.
