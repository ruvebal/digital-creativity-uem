## Context & learning goals

Separate spectacle, hologram-like display, and augmented reality as experience
choices. Students will map a viewer interaction, prototype one AR layer with a
non-AR fallback, assess audience burden and access, and avoid claiming that
engagement equals learning or purchase.

## Session A: Layer, trigger, response (2 hours)

| Time | Teacher move |
| --- | --- |
| 00–10 | Show an object with and without a digital layer; ask what the layer earns. |
| 10–30 | Explain trigger, tracking, overlay, spatial relation, audience action,
  hologram-like display, privacy, and fallback. |
| 30–45 | Screen a licensed fashion-AR or display demonstration; students chart trigger, layer, and response. |
| 45–55 | Compare a useful layer with a spectacle-only counterexample. |
| 55–65 | Demo a phone/marker or screen prototype and a static storyboard alternative. |
| 65–100 | Studio sprint: teams make an AR interaction card and low-fidelity prototype. |
| 100–113 | Individual B3, no AI: diagnose a failed trigger, inaccessible interaction, or unjustified layer. |
| 113–120 | Peer test with observation only; delete participant data after critique. |

## Screening & analysis

- **Video:** fashion-AR audience demonstration; annotate trigger, overlay,
  object relation, action, and response rather than novelty.
- **Stills:** object-only, overlay-on-object, and cluttered counterexample;
  provide captions and a non-AR view.
- **Graphic:** interaction loop `object → trigger → layer → viewer action →
  response → fallback`, with privacy/accessibility checkpoints.

## Studio sprint

Produce one interaction card, storyboard/prototype, asset/provenance ledger,
privacy note, and non-AR/static fallback. If the prototype uses a person,
obtain permission; no public showroom release is implied.

## Individual problem

1. **Diagnostic, no AI:** decide whether the failure is trigger, tracking,
   overlay, instruction, access, or audience fit.
2. Distinguish “more engagement” from a claim about learning or buying.
3. Redesign the interaction for a viewer without a compatible device.
