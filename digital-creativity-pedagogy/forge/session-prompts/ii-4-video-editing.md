## Context & learning goals

Make the complete production path visible: brief, storyboard, capture, edit,
feedback, export. Students will identify a fashion-film purpose, assemble a
short sequence, use a continuity or rhythm decision intentionally, and submit
an accessible, licensed, reproducible edit package.

## Session A: Brief to export (2 hours)

| Time | Teacher move |
| --- | --- |
| 00–10 | Play the same three shots in two orders; ask how meaning and garment attention change. |
| 10–30 | Explain fashion film, branded content, shot purpose, continuity, rhythm, audio bridge, and export. |
| 30–45 | Screen a legal fashion-film excerpt; students map shot, sound, pace, and audience. |
| 45–55 | Compare a purposeful cut with a continuity/rhythm failure; diagnose before fixing. |
| 55–65 | Demo a six-shot storyboard → timeline → captioned export. |
| 65–100 | Studio sprint: teams make a 20–30-second fashion-film rough cut from licensed/original clips. |
| 100–113 | Individual B3, no AI: choose the missing shot or edit to solve a stated communication problem. |
| 113–120 | Screening circle and one specific next-cut note per team. |

## Screening & analysis

- **Video:** a short fashion-film excerpt with canonical source and captions;
  annotate shot purpose, brand function, rhythm, and what is not shown.
- **Stills:** storyboard frame, strong establishing/detail pair, and a
  counterexample whose cuts hide the garment.
- **Graphic:** `brief → storyboard → capture → edit → critique → export` with
  audio/caption and rights checkpoints.

## Studio sprint

Create a 20–30-second rough cut with six shots, a storyboard, edit decision
list, captions/transcript, credits, and export settings. Use only original or
licence-cleared media. Include a silent/static fallback for the critique.

## Individual problem

1. **Diagnostic, no AI:** select a wide, medium, detail, or reaction shot to
   solve the described audience problem.
2. Diagnose whether a confusing cut is caused by screen direction, rhythm, missing context, or sound.
3. Write the one-line human verification needed before publishing the export.

## Follow-up

Submit rough cut, project file, storyboard, edit decision list, caption file,
and rights ledger. The final film is not public by default; exhibition requires
separate permission.
