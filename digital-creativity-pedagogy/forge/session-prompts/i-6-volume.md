## Context & learning goals

Use a small analogue/digital loop to make volume inspectable. Students will
compare a physical mock-up and its digital representation, identify material
and spatial mismatches, make one controlled iteration, and explain what the
screen cannot prove by itself.

## Session A: Build, scan, question (2 hours)

| Time | Teacher move |
| --- | --- |
| 00–10 | Place a folded fabric form beside its screen image; ask which evidence is missing from either. |
| 10–30 | Explain drape, tension, fold, void, scale, gravity, camera, and lighting as volume variables. |
| 30–45 | Screen a hybrid garment-prototyping video; students identify analogue-to-digital translations. |
| 45–55 | Compare a convincing volume with a polished but physically impossible one. |
| 55–65 | Demo one paper/muslin mock-up and its digital blockout, including a check-back loop. |
| 65–100 | Studio sprint: build a small volume study, capture it, and make one digital revision (lesson B2). |
| 100–113 | Individual B3, no AI: follow lesson page — hybrid step diagnosis, flat-render limit, draping consequence. |
| 113–120 | Exit ticket: name one claim the physical model supports and one the screen supports. |

## Screening & analysis

- **Video:** hybrid prototyping/process clip; pause at the analogue/digital
  handoff and ask what information is gained or lost.
- **Stills:** material exemplar, digital-only counterexample, and a close-up
  showing fold/void evidence.
- **Graphic:** a two-way loop `mock-up ↔ capture ↔ digital volume ↔ critique`,
  with labels for gravity, material, scale, and camera.

## Studio sprint

Deliver a 15–20 cm volume study or equivalent low-tech form, two documented
views, a digital interpretation, and a one-change revision note. Accessibility:
provide tactile description, large labels, and a static fallback.

## Individual problem

Follow the lesson page B3 items (authoritative for assessment):

1. **Diagnostic, no AI:** given a described hybrid workflow, identify which step was probably analogue vs digital and why.
2. Name what a flat digital render cannot show about volume that a physical or rotating 3D view can.
3. Without any tool, describe how draping changes as volume increases — at least one structural consequence.

## Critical field lens & open living prompt

**Field tension:** digital prototyping may reduce physical samples while moving
energy, labour, expertise, and overproduction pressures elsewhere.

**Verified perspective:** Coats (2026, 8) reports student scepticism about broader sustainability — supports critical discussion of relocated costs, not a general sustainability or learning-effect claim.

**Open living prompt:** What exactly has been reduced when a physical sample
becomes a digital one, and what has merely moved elsewhere?

**Bring forward:** one material, labour, energy, or production consequence.
