## Context & learning goals

Frame the image as a practice with an audience, venue, intention, and chain of
decisions. By the end, students can classify an image by practice, identify
its artistic/commercial boundary, annotate one production choice, and explain
why the file format alone does not describe the image.

## Session A: The image before the tool (2 hours)

| Time | Teacher move |
| --- | --- |
| 00–10 | Show the same image with three captions: editorial, campaign, archive. Ask what changed. |
| 10–30 | Mini-lecture: editorial, advertising, portrait, documentary; audience, venue, intent, circulation. |
| 30–45 | Screen the prospected image-practice video; pause twice for students to mark framing and implied audience. |
| 45–55 | Compare two stills: “What does the caption make visible or hide?” |
| 55–65 | Demonstrate a four-field image annotation: practice / audience / decision / boundary. |
| 65–100 | Studio sprint: teams classify 3–5 licensed images and write one boundary sentence per image. |
| 100–113 | Individual B3 diagnostic, without AI: classify one unseen image description and justify it. |
| 113–120 | Exit ticket: “A digital image is more than ___ because ___.” Assign the annotation folder. |

## Screening & analysis

- **Video:** short fashion-image context or archive clip; ask students to notice
  who controls the frame and who is presumed to look.
- **Stills:** one editorial exemplar, one commercial exemplar, and one ambiguous
  image; classify each as practice, not as “good” or “bad.”
- **Graphic:** a four-node diagram `maker → venue → audience → circulation`,
  with a movable artistic/commercial threshold.

## Studio sprint

Produce an annotated set of 3–5 images. Every item needs a piece ID, creator,
canonical source, licence/permission note, practice label, and one sentence
about the artistic/commercial boundary. Include a text-only annotation sheet
for accessibility.

## Individual problem

1. **Diagnostic, no AI:** classify a described image and justify the label in two sentences.
2. Explain why "JPEG" answers a technical question but not a practice question.
3. Given an ambiguous artistic/commercial placement, write the one-sentence boundary judgement by hand — no tool or AI.

## Follow-up

Submit the annotated set and a 100-word reflection naming one image decision
that would change if the venue changed. Do not collect the work as research
data or publish it without the repository consent gates.

## Critical field lens & open living prompt

**Field tension:** circulation gives an image new audiences and meanings while
often hiding who selected, framed, or benefits from it.

**Critical perspective:** Whose caption, venue, and gaze become authoritative,
and which maker or subject remains absent?

**Open living prompt:** As one image travels between editorial, campaign,
archive, and classroom, who gets to define its meaning, and what evidence would
make us revise that judgement?

**Bring forward:** one changed caption, source context, or counterexample.
