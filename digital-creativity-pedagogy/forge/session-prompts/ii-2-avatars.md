> **Central question:** Who is represented by an avatar, and who gets to decide what that representation means?

## Context & learning goals

Treat an avatar as a designed point of view with identity, body, garment, and
agency implications. Students will separate avatar components, document source
and authorship, make one representation choice explicit, and test the design
from another viewer's perspective. VTON is out of scope.

## Session A: Body, viewpoint, agency (2 hours)

| Time | Teacher move |
| --- | --- |
| 00–10 | Compare two avatars wearing the same garment; ask what the body/viewpoint changes. |
| 10–30 | Explain identity, embodiment, proxy, pose, garment, camera, agency, and consent. |
| 30–45 | Screen a legal avatar/embodiment or character-design clip; annotate viewpoint and agency. |
| 45–55 | Audit a respectful exemplar and a stereotype/counterexample without speculating about a real person. |
| 55–65 | Demo a component map and an authorship/material declaration. |
| 65–100 | Studio sprint: create a non-identifying fashion avatar and a viewpoint variation. |
| 100–113 | Individual B3, no AI: diagnostic on narrow defaults; identity-intent note; VTON boundary. |
| 113–120 | Peer feedback: "What does this avatar choose, not merely display?" |

## Screening & analysis

- **Video:** avatar design or embodiment process with a transcript; pause at
  decisions about body, pose, gaze, and clothing.
- **Stills:** authored avatar exemplar, ambiguous representation, and a
  counterexample; avoid unlicensed likenesses.
- **Graphic:** component map `identity fiction / body / pose / garment / camera /
  voice`, with consent and provenance checkpoints.

## Studio sprint

Create one avatar, one viewpoint variation, a component/provenance map, and a
100-word authorship statement. Use fictional or self-authored inputs. No public
showroom or research reuse follows automatically.

## Individual problem

1. **Diagnostic, no AI:** given an avatar brief where every trait defaults to one narrow body type, name the representational problem and how you would correct it.
2. Write the identity-intent note (body type, skin tone, styling, and why) for a described avatar concept — without using any generator.
3. **No-AI, declared:** in two sentences, explain why this unit's avatar is not a VTON/try-on simulation.

piece-specific permission first.

## Critical field lens & open living prompt

**Field tension:** avatars can expand identity play while reproducing body,
cultural, biometric, or ownership defaults.

**Critical perspective (C2):** Avatar libraries are not neutral representations
of the body — available categories, skin tones, and features condition which
identities can be recognised.

**Critical perspective (C3):** Contemporary image-generation systems can
reproduce intersectional gender and racial inequalities; tools are not
representationally neutral infrastructure.

**Open living prompt:** What kind of agency does the avatar give its maker, and
which identities does the system make difficult to imagine?

**Bring forward:** one default audit, absent identity, or human decision checkpoint.
