# I.4 · Effects — enrichment

## Forge thesis

An effect is a transformation with a before, a purpose, and an accountable
trace. The technical families are adjustment, compositing, masking, blend,
distortion, and synthesis; the critical question is what the transformation
claims about a body, garment, place, or reality.

## Strong routes from refreshed discovery

- **Human–AI process:** Rizzi and Bertola support a process ledger: source,
  prompt or operation, selection, refusal, and final change.
- **Image-synthesis ethics:** distinguish “can be generated” from “should be
  shown, attributed, or used.”
- **Decolonial archive:** Rodriguez Schon and Valle-Noronha’s route through
  Bogotá and Spain keeps reconstruction from becoming a purely software issue:
  it can reactivate histories, or flatten and appropriate them.
- **Cultural checks:** algorithmic bias, appropriation, and environmental cost
  are debate axes, not a ban on tools.

## Critical / Ibero-American / Spain lens

Compare a retouched commercial image with a digitally reconstructed cultural
object or archive record. Identify the before, intervention, authority to
intervene, and disclosure needed. Spain and Iberoamerica are situated cases,
not a single cultural category.

## Open living prompt

**At what point does transformation become a claim about a person, garment,
heritage, or reality—and who has the right to make that claim?**

## Slideshow spine

1. Every effect has a before.  2. Non-destructive layers preserve agency.  3.
A filter is an argument, not a button.  4. Generative pixels change authorship.
5. Reconstruction can reveal and erase history.  6. Lab: disclose the
transformation and keep the trace.
