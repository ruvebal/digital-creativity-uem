# I.3 · Colour and bitmap images — enrichment

## Forge thesis

Colour is sampled, encoded, displayed, named, and interpreted. The studio task
is therefore to make a palette while keeping the chain of decisions visible.

## Strong routes from refreshed discovery

- **Fashion palette computation:** Nobile, Noris, Kalbaska, and Cantoni’s review
  places palette extraction and colour forecasting inside digital-fashion
  research. Ask what is lost when a runway image becomes a ranked palette.
- **Teaching route:** Balasubramanian gives a concrete fashion-design activity
  using HEX/Pantone specifications and a moodboard. Use it as a comparative
  precedent, not proof of a validated UEM sequence.
- **Critical colour:** CIELAB and skin-reflectance records make the “neutral”
  viewer visible. Ask whose skin, display, lighting, and contrast are treated
  as default.
- **Computational route:** colour forecasting, texture generation, and visual
  retrieval map the field; the exercise remains a bounded bitmap comparison.

## Critical / Ibero-American / Spain lens

Compare an algorithmic palette with a situated reading from a Spanish or
Ibero-American fashion/image archive. The question is which histories,
materials, bodies, and display conditions disappear when colour is normalised.

## Open living prompt

**When a palette is called accurate, accessible, or fashionable, who is the
default viewer—and what changes when another body, display, or cultural code
enters the room?**

## Slideshow spine

1. Colour is a relation, not a swatch.  2. A bitmap is sampled colour with
limits.  3. A palette can be computed and still needs interpretation.  4. The
display participates in the image.  5. “Neutral” colour has a history.  6. Lab:
preserve the palette decision and its alternate.
