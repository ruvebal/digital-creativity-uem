## Official course contract

The course covers 150 hours and 6 ECTS. The presencial formative activities
are: lectures 10 h; practical application seminars 20 h; problem solving 8 h;
research and projects 20 h; workshop/laboratory activities 14 h; independent
work 56 h; debates and colloquia 8 h; academic tutoring 12 h; and in-person
assessment tests 2 h. The pedagogical session plan allocates the 80 contact
hours across lectures, seminars, problem solving, research/projects,
workshop/laboratory activity and debate. Independent work, tutoring and tests
remain course-level allocations rather than invented weekly dates.

Official evaluation instruments are: in-person assessment tests 55%;
case/problem 15%; research and projects 20%; and laboratory/workshop practice
notebook 10%.

The four percentages above are the official course weights. The D1 local
rubric (presentation 70%, deliverable support 20%, peer debate 10%) describes
how that activity is read internally; it does not change the 15%/10%/20%/55%
institutional distribution. Campus Virtual briefs and rubrics remain the
reference for submission format and any announced deadline.

Official contents: Introduction to digital images; 2D digital technology:
drawing tools; colour and bitmap images; effects; fundamentals and
representation of three-dimensional form; volume; compositional sources and
references in fashion design; creating animated digital images; and digital
still lifes.

Official competencies served: CON1, HAB9, HAB10, COMP8 and COMP9.

## Assessment activities

### D1 Live fashion-image analysis — Research and projects (10%)

- **Method:** analyse one fashion image related to a Madrid exhibition piece
  with the eight-step Master Lecture method.
- **Format:** live, navigable slideshow; maximum 15 minutes including questions
  and feedback.
- **Submit:** selected image and source/permission record, eight-step analysis
  card, editable and exported slideshow, process trail, and final or
  penultimate authorship / AI declaration slide.
- **Local rubric:** presentation 70%, deliverable support 20% and peer debate
  10%; this does not create an additional course percentage.

### D2 Transposition — Research and projects (10%)

- **Brief:** complete the same bilingual transposition brief as ACT3.
- **Outcome:** transform a song, film, poem, literary work, theatre piece or
  other work from another language into a cartel; an image series is acceptable
  when it functions as the cartel proposal.
- **Make visible:** composition, colour, hierarchy and typography as decisions.
- **Submit:** source and credits, concept rationale, reference lineage,
  sketches/tests, editable file, final export and process trail.

### D3 Atrium — Case/problem (15%)

- **Theme:** “What is fashion for you?”
- **Develop:** technical script, audience, event sequence, visual and technical
  communication, roles/credits and accessibility decisions.
- **Submit:** script, production or rehearsal evidence, final communication
  artefacts and live-defence materials.

### Activity 4 — D4 Final Digital Portfolio Notebook — practice notebook (10%)

- **Format:** one navigable digital portfolio notebook.
- **Include:** at least one substantive exercise entry for every Lab
  time/lesson, evidence of all nine Digital Creativity I contents, and D1–D3.
- **Document:** dated process traces, feedback and revisions, final synthesis,
  process inventory, source/credit record, technical/accessibility check, and
  authorship, AI and material declaration for every piece.
- **Purpose:** the portfolio is evidence for the final exam as well as the
  practice-notebook instrument.

### Activity 5 — D5 Exam — in-person assessment tests (55%)

- **Format:** critical demonstration using the portfolio as evidence.
- **Explain and defend:** technical choices, formal decisions, references,
  revisions, feedback and authorship.
- **Standard:** connect the response to the official contents rather than
  merely recalling software terms.
- **Timing:** official assessment period; no institutional exam date is
  invented here.

## Transversal master sessions

These are teaching methods and critique sessions, not additional official
CONTENIDOS unit IDs:

- Fashion image analysis: morphology first, then evidence-based reading of
  meaning, power, audience and circulation.
- Image morphology: dot, line, plane, shape, form, colour, composition and
  perspective as a shared formal vocabulary.
- Dance, Cinema and Fashion cycle: movement, costume, camera, edit,
  reference, appropriation and evidence.
- What Does a Garment Do When the Body Moves?; Who Shapes a Dance: the Costume
  or the Camera?; and Who Owns a Movement When It Travels?: three connected
  sessions for embodied viewing, camera framing and responsible reference.

## Portfolio and AI requirements

Every portfolio entry, exercise, project or exam response includes a piece ID,
final artefact, editable/source files, process trace, references/credits and an
AI declaration. The [AI usage declaration](https://ruvebal.github.io/digital-creativity-uem/ai-declaration/)
does not prohibit AI; it requires transparent documentation of **Tools used**,
**Purpose of use**, **Human contribution** and **Source material**. “No AI tools
used” is valid. Transparency over prohibition and human authorship remain the
central terms: AI may assist, but it cannot replace core creative decisions,
technical understanding, critical judgement or final responsibility. This also
covers concept development, technical assistance, media enhancement and
language support. Traditional citations, collaboration credit, material
permissions and accessibility standards still apply.

Images of classmates or other identifiable people require permission; the
draft research consent forms in the repository are not live consent
instruments and cannot be used until professor and DPO / ethics approval.
Instructor-side files: `digital-creativity-pedagogy/forge/AI-DECLARATION-LAW.mdc`,
`digital-creativity-pedagogy/AI-EVIDENCE-HARNESS.md`, and the draft English
consent form in `digital-creativity-pedagogy/consent/CONSENT-FORM-EN.md`.

## Academic integrity

Students must be the authors of their work. Plagiarism, in whole or in part,
and fraudulent means in assessment are very serious offences under the current
Universidad Europea student disciplinary regulations. The course AI rule is
additional to, and does not replace, academic integrity requirements.
