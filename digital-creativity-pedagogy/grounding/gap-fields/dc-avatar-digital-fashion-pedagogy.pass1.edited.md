Research landscape map — Avatar and digital human making pedagogy for fashion education without virtual try-on

Field snapshot: 2024–2026 · emphasis on higher education, fashion/design pedagogy, avatar authorship rather than consumer VTON

Scope boundary. This map treats an avatar/digital human as a designed or authored representational body used for garment creation, fashion communication, virtual environments, animation, performance, or identity work. It distinguishes that from virtual try-on (VTON), whose central research problem is transferring or visualising a specified garment on a target person for fit, retail, or e-commerce. That distinction is supported by current VTON reviews and technical literature. 
MDPI

A major finding across the map is that fashion HE research has substantially stronger evidence for teaching 3D garment prototyping on avatars than for teaching avatar/digital-human construction as a craft in itself. Papachristou & Zolota-Tatsi (2024), Kumar, Sinha & Pai (2025), Conlon et al. (2024), and Galada & Baytar (2025) provide direct educational evidence around 3D garments, virtual bodies and simulation; comparable validated curricula for modelling, morphology, rigging, characterization and identity-conscious avatar authorship remain difficult to locate. 

## 1. Avatar creation pedagogy in fashion higher education

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — direct HE evidence currently approaches avatars primarily through 3D virtual garment design rather than standalone character modelling. Papachristou & Zolota-Tatsi's Encouraging 3D Virtual Design in Fashion Education: Best Practices reports a twelve-week third-year fashion intervention incorporating 2D CAD and 3D clothing-design tools; students developed and communicated fashion collections through 3D virtual prototyping. Papachristou, E. & Zolota-Tatsi, N. (2024), ITC-ICEE proceedings, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-48933-4_43. 

[ESTABLISHED] A second direct HE strand uses digital pattern making and virtual garment simulation to develop spatial and garment-construction competence. Kumar, Sinha & Pai studied undergraduate fashion-design students and specifically frame 3D visualisation competency as part of the learning problem. Kumar, M., Sinha, S. & Pai, S. (2025), Exploring the Practice of Digital Pattern Making and Virtual Garment Prototyping by Fashion Design Students, ICoRD 2025, DOI 10.1007/978-981-96-7312-4_16. 

[ESTABLISHED] Fashion-business education also contains explicit avatar manipulation. Conlon et al.'s university/industry masterclass includes selecting and modifying a 3D avatar for rendering and virtual-catwalk presentation, making it one of the clearer published precedents for avatar handling within fashion education rather than merely mentioning avatars conceptually. Conlon et al. (2024), Developing digital skills: a fashion business masterclass in virtual 3D prototyping with Style3D, International Journal of Fashion Design, Technology and Education.

The project describes learning-by-doing combined with critical reflection rather than software instruction alone. Kolding School of Design (2025), Me You Us: Exploring Digital Tools for a More Sustainable and Inclusive Fashion Education.

## 3. Teaching virtual garments on avatars in design schools

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — this is the best-established pedagogical subsection of the whole field. Current HE studies teach the relation among 2D pattern, 3D garment, fabric simulation and avatar rather than avatar creation in isolation. Papachristou & Zolota-Tatsi (2024) and Kumar, Sinha & Pai (2025) provide the clearest recent direct evidence. 

[ESTABLISHED] Physical/digital iteration remains pedagogically important rather than obsolete. Arnevik's Design School Kolding model explicitly moves from digital pattern/garment on an avatar to physical toile fitting and then corrections back into the digital pattern. Christel Arnevik, The (R)evolution of Virtual Prototyping: Implementing CLO3D in an Artistic Fashion Education (2023). 
User Content

[ESTABLISHED] Assessment research is beginning to separate visualisation from actual fit reasoning. Galada & Baytar developed and validated a simulation-based instrument for measuring students' ability to read 3D garment misfit and select corrective changes to 2D patterns. Galada, A. & Baytar, F. (2025), Developing an instrument with simulations to measure 3D to 2D fit correction skills in fashion design. 

[EMERGING] 2026 work continues to use parametric avatars as experimental bodies in fashion-design learning. A recent AUTEX study digitises an existing pattern, modifies it for a specific body form and virtually sews it on a parametric avatar before comparison with a physical garment; the authors explicitly discuss application in fashion-school education. 

[ESTABLISHED] Canonical foundation: 3D virtual prototyping predates the current metaverse/avatar discourse and remains technically anchored in virtual garment simulation, fabric behaviour, pattern-avatar relations and physical/digital comparison. Papachristou & Bilalis (2017), for example, treated 3D prototyping as a sustainability and product-development technology well before contemporary generative-avatar hype; DOI 10.1080/19397038.2017.1348563. 

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: there is still no clearly validated progression from avatar-as-fitting-mannequin → avatar-as-authored communicative character in the HE literature located here. The literature supports the former substantially better.

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology: body–pattern relationship, virtual prototyping, virtual garment simulation, digital pattern making, fit correction and 3D-to-2D translation are durable terms for this pedagogical cluster. 

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: simulations are not treated in the stronger studies as transparent substitutes for physical fitting. Both the recent AUTEX comparison and earlier pedagogical work identify body measurement, material behaviour and translation between virtual and physical garments as sources of error or required expertise. 

## 4. Anthropomorphism, embodiment and identity frameworks for fashion avatars

[ESTABLISHED] Canonical distinction: anthropomorphism is a narrower construct than avatar identity. Epley, Waytz & Cacioppo define it as attributing human-like properties, motivations or intentions to nonhuman agents and theorise it through elicited agent knowledge, effectance and sociality. Epley, N., Waytz, A. & Cacioppo, J.T. (2007), On Seeing Human: A Three-Factor Theory of Anthropomorphism, Psychological Review 114(4), DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.114.4.864. 
PubMed

[ESTABLISHED] Avatar research therefore has a more precise vocabulary for fashion-relevant identity questions: self-representation, self-presentation, self-avatar, embodiment, self-presence and avatar customization address the user's relationship to a digital body more directly than anthropomorphism alone. 
ScienceDirect

[ESTABLISHED] The Proteus Effect remains a canonical reference because avatar appearance can affect behaviour. Yee & Bailenson's work showed behavioural consequences of transformed self-representation, with subsequent literature continuing to test and theorise the effect. Yee & Bailenson (2007/2009); see also Coesel et al. (2024), A theoretical review of the Proteus effect. 
Sage Journals

[ESTABLISHED] Anthropomorphic appearance is not a neutral variable. Nowak & Rauh found avatar anthropomorphism, gendered appearance, attractiveness and perceived credibility to be interrelated; their results also caution against treating anthropomorphism as the sole explanatory dimension. Nowak, K.L. & Rauh, C. (2005), The Influence of the Avatar on Online Perceptions of Anthropomorphism, Androgyny, Credibility, Homophily, and Attraction. 
OUP Academic

[EMERGING] Recent fashion research increasingly treats digital fashion as a performative component of virtual selfhood rather than merely clothing rendered on a body. Kabalska's 2026 work explicitly examines digital fashion's functions and performers in the metaverse through self-related theoretical frameworks. 
Taylor & Francis Online

[EMERGING] Recent scholarship also destabilises a simple body/avatar boundary. Craig's Dressing the technological body treats fashioning avatars and other technological bodies as cultural and aesthetic meaning-making, while Lange's ethnographic digital-human-twin work argues that avatar practices raise unresolved questions about bodies, technological extension and everyday self-representation. 

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: these identity and embodiment theories explain why avatar form matters, but I found no evidence that they validate a particular fashion-modelling syllabus. They should therefore be classified as conceptual foundations, not pedagogical proof.

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: specialists increasingly resist reducing avatar identity to “make the character more human-looking.” Current work separates physical resemblance, behavioural correspondence, identity linkage, experiential self-presence and social meaning. 
Sage Journals

## 6. Assessing avatar literacy as craft rather than asset download

[ESTABLISHED] Direct precedent exists for assessing underlying 3D reasoning instead of polished artefacts. Galada & Baytar's validated instrument measures students' capacity to diagnose visible 3D fit problems and map them back to appropriate 2D pattern corrections rather than scoring only the rendered garment. 

[ESTABLISHED] The strongest educational 3D-fashion studies similarly emphasise process competence—spatial visualisation, pattern development, iterative simulation and problem-solving—not mere production of a final rendered image. 

[ESTABLISHED] This matters particularly because contemporary avatar systems increasingly provide high-level presets. MetaHuman Creator can begin from preset humans and provides fully rigged characters; CLO likewise provides editable avatar systems. The ability to produce a credible character therefore no longer demonstrates that a learner understands morphology, topology, joints or deformation. 
Epic Games Developers
MetaHuman

[EMERGING] The asset-versus-authorship problem is becoming more acute as interoperability and marketplaces expand. MetaHuman-compatible humans, clothing and hair assets can now be obtained through Fab and further edited in the MetaHuman ecosystem. 
Epic Games Developers

[UNVERIFIED] Major blank: I found no validated avatar-literacy assessment instrument for fashion HE equivalent to Galada & Baytar's fit-correction measure. Specifically absent from the located literature are validated rubrics distinguishing downloaded/preset assembly from authored morphology, rigging, body representation, motion preparation or deliberate identity decisions.

[UNVERIFIED] Major blank: I also found no established empirical threshold for how much modelling or rigging a fashion student must perform personally before an avatar can defensibly count as “student-authored.” No verifiable source found.

[EMERGING] Sharper terminology: procedural knowledge, 3D spatial literacy, fit-correction skill, digital craft, character-authoring literacy and asset provenance are potentially relevant. Of these, fit-correction and spatial competence are empirically grounded in fashion education; “character-authoring literacy” as a settled fashion-education construct remains [UNVERIFIED]. 

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: output-only assessment becomes increasingly weak evidence of competence as preset characters, automated posing and ready-rigged digital humans become easier to deploy. This is a technological inference from documented tooling, not yet a validated fashion-pedagogy finding.

## 7. AI-generated avatars, authorship and fashion-studio education

[EMERGING] State of the art — GenAI is now an explicit fashion-education research topic, but avatar-specific pedagogy remains a subset rather than the dominant focus. Balasubramanian (2026) frames GenAI integration into fashion curriculum through TPACK, while recent studio-oriented reviews organise the debate around knowledge construction, learner identity/authorship, teaching practice and studio ethics. 
Frontiers

[EMERGING] Fashion-specific empirical work reports both creative opportunity and critical/ethical requirements. A 2025 fashion-design course study reports enhanced creativity, critical thinking, cultural awareness and teamwork when GenAI was integrated with cultural-diversity work. 
Emerald Publishing

[EMERGING] GenAI is also entering 3D fashion-modelling pedagogy directly. Chan's 2025 IASDR paper specifically investigates GenAI in 3D-modelling education within fashion design, making it unusually close to the present field even though it is not limited to avatar creation. 

[EMERGING] Authorship is not only pedagogical but legal. Niyompatama's 2025 copyright analysis addresses originality, pre-existing fashion works and generative systems, showing that fashion GenAI authorship questions cannot be reduced to prompt ownership. Niyompatama, M.J. (2025), Generative AI in fashion design creation: a copyright analysis, Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice. 
OUP Academic

[EMERGING] AI-generated educational avatars already have an evidence base outside fashion. Vallis et al. studied student perceptions of AI-generated avatars in a postgraduate subject and explicitly situate them as objects for critical reflection on algorithms, postdigitality and ethics rather than treating acceptance as self-evidently beneficial. Vallis et al. (2024), Student Perceptions of AI-Generated Avatars in Teaching. 

[EMERGING] Named active fashion-education locus: London College of Fashion's Fashion AI Research, Enterprise and Education Hub (FAIREE) explicitly researches ethical AI across fashion design, education and enterprise. 

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: I found no validated fashion-studio assessment model specifically determining authorship when the student's digital human is substantially generated by text/image-to-avatar systems. Existing fashion GenAI literature treats authorship broadly but does not yet settle avatar-specific attribution.

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: the literature does not support treating AI adoption as automatically pedagogically progressive. Current fashion-education work explicitly foregrounds ethics, learner identity and authorship, while legal scholarship highlights unresolved relationships to underlying works and originality.

## 8. Inclusive body representation and educational avatar libraries

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — avatar inclusion is a genuine research field, but most rigorous evidence comes from HCI/social-VR rather than fashion classrooms. Ribeiro et al.'s 2024 study of 133 participants identifies continuing gaps in inclusive avatar self-representation. Ribeiro et al. (2024), Towards Inclusive Avatars: A Study on Self-Representation in Virtual Environments. 
ResearchGate

[ESTABLISHED] Disability representation is particularly well documented as an inadequacy of existing avatar systems. Park et al. found that people with physical disabilities have heterogeneous preferences about disability representation rather than universally preferring either idealisation or realism. 
PubMed Central (PMC)

[EMERGING] The evidence became more operational in 2025. Zhang et al.'s work derived avatar-design guidelines through literature review and interviews with 60 disabled participants, covering appearance, body dynamics, assistive technologies, avatar surroundings and customization control; the guidelines were subsequently evaluated with VR practitioners. Zhang et al. (2025), Inclusive Avatar Guidelines for People with Disabilities. 

[EMERGING] Invisible disability further complicates “representative” avatar design. Gualano et al. found contextual and individual variation in whether participants wanted disabilities disclosed through embodied avatars and identified patterns including non-disclosure and situational disclosure. 

[EMERGING] Morphological inclusion remains technically underdeveloped. Yamada, Gois & Takahashi (2026) identify scarcity of diverse datasets and inadequate animation support for limb differences and non-normative anatomies as continuing technical problems. 

[EMERGING] Fashion education is beginning to connect scanning with body diversity directly. Kolding's 2025 “Me You Us” project used body and environmental scanning and explicitly asked students to consider diverse bodies and contexts through co-design. 

[ESTABLISHED] Canonical technical caveat: even widely used parametric-body systems encode bounded statistical body spaces. The STAR authors explicitly identified limitations in SMPL's representation of human shape variation and expanded training data to improve generalisation. Osman, Bolkart & Black (2020), STAR: Sparse Trained Articulated Human Body Regressor. 

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: I found no published audit of the body-type, disability, racialised morphology, age or gender-expression coverage of avatar libraries actually supplied to fashion students across HE institutions. This is a significant empirical blank, not evidence that curricula are necessarily deficient.

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology: inclusive avatar design, disability representation, self-representation, body diversity, morphological diversity and embodied identity are more precise than simply “inclusive avatars.” These are primarily durable conceptual concerns even though the technical implementations are rapidly changing. 

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: “more avatar options” does not automatically equal meaningful inclusion. Current research stresses user control, contextual disclosure, behaviour/body dynamics and assistive technology—not merely a larger menu of skin tones or body presets.

## 10. Boundary between avatar pedagogy and virtual try-on research

[ESTABLISHED] The clearest boundary is the research objective. Image-based VTON seeks to generate a representation of a target person wearing a specified garment; contemporary systems typically preserve person identity and garment characteristics while performing garment transfer. 

[ESTABLISHED] Consumer VTO research is consequently dominated by retail outcomes and adoption questions. Chen et al.'s 2024 systematic review synthesised 69 studies addressing consumer behaviour, purchasing decisions, adoption and technological gratification in fashion VTO. Chen et al. (2024), Virtual Try-On Systems in Fashion Consumption, Applied Sciences 14(24), 11839. 
MDPI

[ESTABLISHED] Avatar/digital-human making has a different centre: body representation, shape, character identity, mesh/skeleton structure, embodiment, animation and communication remain meaningful even when no consumer “tries on” anything. SMPL and contemporary MetaHuman pipelines demonstrate this technically; self-representation research demonstrates it conceptually. 
ACM Digital Library
Epic Games Developers

[ESTABLISHED] 3D virtual garment education sits between those territories but is not automatically VTON. A student sewing and simulating a designed garment on a configurable avatar in CLO is performing virtual prototyping/design; VTON ordinarily introduces the distinct objective of visualising a chosen garment on a particular target wearer for try-on, purchase or fit evaluation. 

[EMERGING] The boundary is becoming technically harder to maintain. Recent 3D/4D VTON systems explicitly reconstruct or animate avatars and model dynamic garments, as in AvatarVTON (2025), while the 2026 review of 3D clothing modelling describes pipelines comprising garment generation, avatar reconstruction and body–garment transformation. 

[ESTABLISHED] Nevertheless, overlap of infrastructure does not collapse the disciplinary distinction. The same body models, garment assets or simulation engines may support fashion design, animation, virtual performance and VTON, while the research questions, evaluation metrics and users remain different. This distinction is visible when fashion-education studies are compared with VTON reviews. 
MDPI

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology for the non-VTON teaching space: 3D virtual prototyping, digital garment simulation, avatar authoring, digital-human creation, character pipeline, virtual fashion communication, embodied avatar and digital identity. The first two are durable and established in fashion education; avatar authoring/digital-human creation are emerging as pedagogical labels; character pipeline and embodiment are durable in adjacent technical/HCI disciplines. 
CONNECT Help Center

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: I found no accepted disciplinary taxonomy stating exactly where fashion-education “avatar literacy” ends and VTON begins. The distinction above is supported by the different research objectives in the literatures, but it is not yet a formal curricular boundary standard.

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: importing VTON research wholesale into an avatar-making curriculum risks conflating consumer visualisation/fit prediction with creative digital-human authorship and communication. The dominant VTON literature evaluates garment-transfer fidelity, identity preservation, fit, adoption or shopping behaviour rather than student competence in constructing digital bodies.
