Digital creativity curriculum gap harvest — fashion image, volume, avatar, AR, video and portfolio

Scope convention. [HE] = evidence from higher-education teaching or curriculum research; [PRACTITIONER] = professional/industry practice rather than demonstrated pedagogy; [PLATFORM] = vendor/platform evidence. VTON is treated only as adjacent literature and is not included as a pedagogical target.

## 1. Teaching 3D form and volume in fashion digital creativity education

[ESTABLISHED][HE] 2024–2026 pedagogy exists, but the strongest fashion-specific evidence concerns garment/pattern construction rather than general visual-spatial “form and volume” literacy. Hardaker & Penfold’s Towards digital transformation of fashion and textiles education: a strategic pedagogy documents a De Montfort University sequence in which first-year students learn manual pattern cutting first and then use digitised 2D blocks and CLO3D avatars in a CAD studio; analogue methods continue alongside digital ones in later years. Hardaker & Penfold, 2025, IFFTI Annual Proceedings, vol. 4, pp. 1118–1131. 

[EMERGING][HE] A second identifiable strand treats immersive 3D sketching/design as a design-development environment rather than simply garment CAD. Hardaker & Penfold report a Level-5 university project using Gravity Sketch and Meta Quest, with initial tool-acquisition weeks followed by a six-week adidas Originals brief, VR tutorials and final 3D presentations. This is unusually concrete teaching-sequence evidence, although it is one institutional case rather than a settled pedagogy. 

[EMERGING][HE] Digital Fashion Australia is an identifiable educator community around this problem. Madeline Taylor’s peer-reviewed IFFTI paper reports educators from Australian fashion, textile and costume programmes comparing CLO3D curriculum designs and explicitly identifies a need for communities of practice and shared teaching resources. Taylor, Digital Fashion: Reflections on Pedagogy from key Australian Fashion Institutions, 2026, IFFTI Annual Proceedings 4, pp. 139–153. 

[EMERGING][HE] Recent experimental work on AI-supported 3D design reports effects on learning and creativity, but it should not be conflated with a validated general sequence for teaching spatial fundamentals. Drakou et al., The Impact of AI-Driven 3D Design on Learning, Creativity..., 2024, ACM, DOI 10.1145/3688671.3688794. 
ACM Digital Library

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational terminology: draping, pattern cutting/patternmaking, form development, silhouette, volume, spatial design and 3D garment visualisation. Physical draping remains a load-bearing analogue foundation against which virtual draping is understood; Laurence King’s Advanced Creative Draping is representative of that continuing pedagogical canon. 

[EMERGING] Sharper frontier terminology: virtual prototyping, 3D garment simulation, digital pattern cutting, immersive sketching and hybrid analogue–digital studio. These are currently more precise than the broad phrase “teaching 3D fashion”. Hardaker & Penfold explicitly frame their implementation as hybrid rather than replacement-oriented. 

[EMERGING] Field-flagged blank: transfer from software operation to durable spatial reasoning remains insufficiently evidenced. Taylor describes CLO3D pedagogy as sufficiently new that participating educators were still establishing baseline course-design approaches, while Hardaker & Penfold assess an institutional digital-transformation strategy rather than isolate gains in spatial cognition. 

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: recent fashion-education work resists a simple “digital replaces physical” narrative. Both the De Montfort case and the Australian educator study explicitly retain traditional studio knowledge alongside virtual tools. 

## 2. 2D drawing, colour, bitmap structure and effects pedagogy in fashion schools

[UNVERIFIED-GAP][HE] I found no strong 2024–2026 peer-reviewed fashion-HE sequence that jointly theorises bitmap structure, resolution, layer/compositing logic, colour and digital effects as a coherent fashion-communication literacy. Current fashion-school curricula demonstrably teach digital image production, but the public evidence is usually course-description rather than evaluated pedagogy; London College of Fashion’s Fashion Photography BA, for example, explicitly includes digital image production and manipulation. 

[ESTABLISHED][HE] Fashion drawing itself remains a visible educational foundation. Current specialist teaching continues to combine line, proportion, colour, collage and traditional/digital rendering rather than treating generative imaging as a replacement for representational literacy; ESDI and Istituto Marangoni provide contemporary curriculum examples. These are programme sources, not efficacy studies. 
ESDI

[EMERGING][HE] There is some recent fashion-specific research on composition and colour teaching. Lisohor’s 2025 study explicitly investigates methodological approaches for integrating compositional and colour knowledge in fashion-design training, but this is a much narrower literature than the current GenAI-fashion corpus. 
Reicst

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational terminology: fashion illustration, visual representation, figure drawing, rendering, colour theory, composition, image manipulation and digital image production are more durable terms than software-specific “Photoshop skills”. Contemporary fashion-illustration literature still frames anatomy/proportion, balance, movement and mixed traditional/digital media as fundamentals. 
Bloomsbury Publishing

[EMERGING] Sharper gap term: digital image literacy for fashion better captures resolution, raster/vector distinction, layers, masks, compositing and colour-management knowledge than “digital drawing” alone; however, I found no 2024–2026 fashion-education study establishing this as a standard named construct. [UNVERIFIED-GAP]

[EMERGING] GenAI intensifies this gap because fashion educators now report AI support at ideation, refinement, prototype and evaluation stages, yet An & Park explicitly state that fashion-education research has concentrated more on tool capability than systematic instructional roles. Hyosun An & Minjung Park, AI tools and fashion design education: instructor perspectives on student challenges and design process tool support, 2026, Fashion and Textiles, 13:3. 

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: a tool-first account of digital drawing can conceal durable visual competencies. Recent fashion-AI literature explicitly warns about originality, authorship and preservation of hands-on/tactile learning; the 2025 systematic review treats learner identity/authorship and studio ethics as core dimensions, not peripheral issues. Anwar, Raza & Marzouk, 2025, Generative AI in Studio-Based Fashion Education, DOI 10.65136/ejbm.v10i4.24.

## 3. Fashion animation and digital still-life studio pedagogy

[UNVERIFIED-GAP][HE] Fashion-specific higher-education research on digital still-life pedagogy is conspicuously thin. I did not find a 2024–2026 peer-reviewed sequence analysing how students learn digital product staging, object composition, material/light control and post-production as a distinct fashion-communication studio practice.

[EMERGING][HE] Moving-image production is more visible than digital still-life. Current fashion photography programmes combine still photography, film, editing and immersive media; Falmouth explicitly lists fashion film, specialist editing and high-end retouching in its degree, but this is curriculum evidence rather than research on learning outcomes. 
Falmouth University

[ESTABLISHED] A load-bearing conceptual base for the moving-image side is fashion film as a distinct visual-communication form. Nick Rees-Roberts’ Fashion Film: Art and Advertising in the Digital Age analyses fashion moving image across advertising, spectacle, online magazines and streamed fashion culture; Bloomsbury, 2018. 
Bloomsbury Publishing

[ESTABLISHED] Film, Fashion & Consumption provides an established peer-reviewed interdisciplinary venue linking film, fashion, design and visual culture, showing that fashion moving-image scholarship is mature even where explicit pedagogy remains sparse. 
Intellect Books

[EMERGING] Sharper durable terms: fashion image-making, still-life photography, product photography, art direction, set design, lighting, compositing, fashion film. Frontier terms: CG product visualisation, synthetic product imagery and AI image-to-video. The latter are active production categories, but a stable higher-education pedagogy was not verified.

[PRACTITIONER][EMERGING] AI-generated fashion video can now be produced from static imagery in institutional/industry demonstrations, but such examples are stimuli rather than controlled instructional research. Bennett University published one such 2025 production case. 
Bennett University

[UNVERIFIED-GAP] No robust 2024–2026 evidence was found comparing physical still-life staging vs 3D-rendered staging vs generative synthetic staging as pedagogical conditions for fashion-communication students.

## 4. Fashion photographic retouching curriculum in higher education

[ESTABLISHED][HE] Retouching is definitely present in fashion-photography curricula, but public evidence is dominated by curriculum specification rather than research on how retouching competence should be sequenced or assessed. Falmouth includes high-end retouching and specialist editing, while LCF explicitly describes digital image production/manipulation as a degree competency. 
Falmouth University

[PRACTITIONER] Contemporary training commonly teaches non-destructive workflows, skin/hair retouching, colour grading and print/web preparation in Photoshop/Lightroom. City Lit’s current beauty/fashion retouching course documents that professional-practice bundle, but it is not a peer-reviewed HE study. 
City Lit

[UNVERIFIED-GAP][HE] I found no convincing 2024–2026 fashion-HE study evaluating whether students should learn retouching through frequency separation, dodge-and-burn, raw-development, colour-management or other particular production sequences. The techniques are professionally established; the pedagogical comparative evidence is not.

[ESTABLISHED] Durable terminology: non-destructive editing, raw processing, colour grading, masking, compositing, retouching ethics and image manipulation. These are conceptually more durable than any one Adobe interface; current university programmes already frame the outcome as image-making/manipulation rather than simply application mastery. 

[EMERGING] Generative editing makes authorship, provenance and representational truthfulness inseparable from retouching literacy. Fashion-specific GenAI research currently discusses originality, ethical responsibility and authorship far more explicitly than conventional retouching pedagogy does. 
ejbm.apu.edu.my

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: fashion-image pedagogy cannot be treated as neutral technical polish. Contemporary fashion education is also being challenged over whose bodies, cultures and visual standards its curricula reproduce; critiques of Eurocentric/white fashion curricula remain relevant to both image selection and manipulation practices. 
Vogue

[UNVERIFIED-GAP] A fashion-HE evidence base joining retouching craft + disclosure/provenance + body-image ethics + synthetic-image editing as one assessable competence was not verified.

## 5. Avatar and digital-human making pedagogy, excluding virtual try-on

[EMERGING][HE] Fashion education does use avatars extensively, but the best-established use is as a digital mannequin within virtual garment development, not as a separately theorised discipline of digital-human authorship. Hardaker & Penfold document students using a standard virtual size-12 female avatar as the CLO3D analogue of the physical mannequin. 

[EMERGING][HE] Digital-fashion courses also use avatars as destinations for self-expression and digital garments. The V&A’s 2025 Digital Fashion Course gave participants technical instruction and 3D-artist mentoring to produce digital garments around self-portraiture; this is structured education but not university-level outcome research. 
Victoria and Albert Museum

[UNVERIFIED-GAP][HE] I found very little 2024–2026 fashion-HE evidence on teaching students to author the avatar itself—body morphology, rig, skin/materials, identity representation, animation and persona construction—independently of garment fitting or VTON.

[EMERGING][HE] A 2025 ITAA proceedings contribution proposes AI avatars as a pedagogical mechanism, including scalable mentoring, but that concerns avatars as instructional agents rather than fashion students learning digital-human production. It is therefore adjacent, not direct evidence. 
Iowa State Digital Press

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology: digital human, virtual human, avatar embodiment, character creation, body representation, rigging and digital identity. Digital human and embodiment are more academically precise than simply “avatar” when the research question concerns representation rather than a garment-display dummy.

[EMERGING] Representation is a substantive controversy. Industry deployment of AI fashion models has produced criticism that synthetic “diversity” may substitute for hiring and paying actual underrepresented models; the debate also exposes dataset bias and unequal access to digital-fashion training. This is industry/social critique, not evidence for a particular classroom method. 
Vogue Business

[UNVERIFIED-GAP] Fashion-specific teaching research on avatar body diversity, disability, race, gender presentation, stylisation versus realism and student authorship remains insufficiently verified.

[EMERGING] VTON and algorithmic garment fitting form a large adjacent technical literature, including controllable 3D garment generation and human/garment reconstruction, but these should not be mistaken for avatar-making pedagogy. 

## 6. Teaching digital fashion experiences and virtual-show staging

[EMERGING][HE] Concrete curricular examples now exist. Hardaker & Penfold report an immersive VR footwear-design brief with software induction, client brief, VR mentoring and final virtual presentation; this demonstrates a teachable sequence for spatial presentation/design even though it is not explicitly a runway course. 

[EMERGING][RESEARCH] Virtual-show production itself is becoming technically formalised. A 2025 ACM paper, Research on the Production Method of Virtual Fashion Show, proposes a multi-software workflow based on physical-fashion-show planning. It is design/production research, not demonstrated HE pedagogy. DOI 10.1145/3745133.3745170. 
ACM Digital Library

[PRACTITIONER][EMERGING] Digital and hybrid fashion shows are established industry communication phenomena, but the literature frequently evaluates experience, access or designer expression rather than learning sequences. A 2024 study on digital/metaverse runways is representative of this experience-oriented literature. 
ResearchGate

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology: virtual fashion show, digital runway, immersive fashion experience, virtual exhibition, spatial storytelling, experience design and fashion spectacle. These distinguish communication/staging from garment CAD.

[EMERGING] FACTUM is a relevant scholarly network for the communication side of this space: the 2025 proceedings explicitly position fashion communication as interdisciplinary research spanning media, communication practices and fashion-related social values. 
ResearchGate

[UNVERIFIED-GAP][HE] I found no mature comparative evidence establishing how to teach camera choreography, avatar blocking, spatial sound, virtual lighting, interaction design and audience journey as an integrated higher-education virtual-fashion-show sequence.

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: the technological novelty of a “metaverse runway” should not itself be treated as learning evidence. Existing publications include production-method papers and audience/designer studies, but those study different constructs from student learning. 
ACM Digital Library

## 7. Fashion video editing education for brand communication

[ESTABLISHED][HE] Fashion communication degrees demonstrably teach video production, post-production and visual storytelling. Istituto Marangoni’s current Fashion Communication & Image programme explicitly names professional video production/post-production and strategic visual storytelling as competencies. This establishes curricular presence, not effectiveness. 
Istituto Marangoni

[EMERGING][HE] Amsterdam University of Applied Sciences provides a more sequence-like example: its 2024–25 Fashion and Visual Culture programme includes a Film Project in which students make their own fashion films through workshops linked to film-analysis theory. 
Exchange Course Catalogue

[ESTABLISHED][HE] Graduate fashion-film programmes also combine capture/editing, research methodology, teamwork and portfolio development; Paris College of Art’s MA Fashion Film & Photography is one current example. Again, this is programme evidence rather than comparative pedagogical research. 
Paris College of Art

[ESTABLISHED] Rees-Roberts’ Fashion Film: Art and Advertising in the Digital Age remains a canonical conceptual source because it frames moving fashion imagery as communication, promotion and spectacle rather than merely technical video production. Bloomsbury, 2018. 
Bloomsbury Publishing

[ESTABLISHED] Durable terminology: fashion film, moving image, montage/editing, visual narrative, art direction, brand storytelling, sound-image relation. Frontier terminology: AI video, image-to-video, synthetic cinematography.

[UNVERIFIED-GAP][HE] I found no robust 2024–2026 fashion-specific comparison of editing pedagogies—e.g. continuity versus associative montage, short-form/social editing versus longer fashion film, or manual editing versus AI-assisted generative/video-remix workflows.

[PRACTITIONER][EMERGING] AI image-to-video fashion production is already occurring, but current evidence is closer to production experimentation than tested pedagogy. 
Bennett University

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: “brand communication” and “fashion film” are overlapping but not identical. The peer-reviewed Film, Fashion & Consumption field explicitly encompasses cultural, historical and critical questions beyond marketing effectiveness. 
Intellect Books

## 8. Fashion student portfolio web UX, accessibility and hiring outcomes

[ESTABLISHED][HE] The e-portfolio has a substantial general HE evidence base as a mechanism for collecting, reflecting on and presenting learning. Farrell’s historical analysis traces its shift from artists’ portfolios to assessment and employability instruments; From Portafoglio to Eportfolio, 2020, Journal of Interactive Media in Education, DOI 10.5334/jime.574. 
JIME

[ESTABLISHED][HE] A 2024 review confirms continued higher-education research interest in e-portfolio implementation, while earlier empirical work links professional e-portfolios to reflection and employability preparation. These findings are HE-general rather than fashion-specific. 
Journals Library Publishing

[EMERGING][HE] Fashion-specific evidence is beginning to appear: a 2026 study titled Communication Challenges in Fashion Portfolio Presentation explicitly concludes that fashion programmes need structured communication and focused pedagogical strategies. 
ebpj.e-iph.co.uk

[PRACTITIONER][HE] Fashion schools visibly use online graduate portfolios as public-facing professional artefacts; Regent’s 2025 graduate portfolio platform is one current institutional example. This demonstrates practice, not causal hiring outcomes. 
Regent's University London

[UNVERIFIED-GAP] I found no robust 2024–2026 fashion-specific study connecting portfolio-site UX quality or WCAG accessibility to employment/hiring outcomes. Claims that an accessible or highly usable portfolio causes improved fashion employment should therefore remain unverified.

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology: e-portfolio, professional portfolio, digital portfolio, reflective portfolio, portfolio presentation, employability signalling. For the interface itself, portfolio UX, information architecture and web accessibility are precise adjacent HCI terms, but they are not yet strongly integrated into fashion-portfolio research.

[ESTABLISHED] Art/design e-portfolio work predates the current fashion-specific gap: the UK Higher Education Academy studied e-portfolios in Art, Design and Media as tools for learner skills and employability. 
Advance HE UK

[EMERGING] Current professional guidance increasingly asks portfolios to expose process, decisions and problem-solving rather than polished outcomes alone; that trend aligns with broader e-portfolio reflection research, though current hiring guidance itself should be treated as practitioner evidence. 
Master CAWEB

[UNVERIFIED-GAP] Fashion-specific evidence for responsive design, semantic HTML, keyboard accessibility, alt-text quality, motion accessibility or portfolio performance as assessed disciplinary learning outcomes was not found.

## 9. Holograms and augmented-reality pedagogy in fashion communication

[EMERGING][HE] AR has some fashion-education research precedent: El-Nahass et al. studied augmented reality in fashion/textile design education and earlier work experimentally tested AR-supported fashion-design skills with tertiary students. The evidence predates the 2024–2026 frontier but establishes that this is not an entirely new pedagogical domain. 
IDJ

[EMERGING][HE] Current higher-education AR evidence outside fashion is much larger. Li et al.’s 2025 systematic review treats AR as an established HE technology with a substantial research base, but transfer of those findings to fashion communication requires caution. 
MDPI

[EMERGING][HE] Current fashion schools are incorporating AR/VR into broader digital-fashion programmes; ELIA’s 2024 Digital Fashion Education initiative explicitly covers 3D prototyping, VR, AR and AI in Higher Arts Education. This is field-development evidence rather than a controlled classroom evaluation. 
Elia Artschools

[PRACTITIONER][EMERGING] “Hologram” fashion education is much less mature. Loughborough announced use of Proto-style holographic telepresence, including plans involving fashion students and immersive-show expertise, but this concerns holograms as a teaching/presence medium, not students learning holographic fashion communication. 
The Guardian

[UNVERIFIED-GAP] I found no robust 2024–2026 peer-reviewed HE sequence for students designing holographic fashion communication—capture, volumetric illusion, spatial staging, interaction, narrative and accessibility—as a coherent fashion subject.

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology: augmented reality, mixed reality, spatial computing, immersive media, AR activation and virtual exhibition. “Hologram” should be used cautiously because many commercial systems marketed that way are forms of telepresence or projected display rather than true holography. The Loughborough/Proto case illustrates this terminology problem. 
The Guardian

[EMERGING] Critique: novelty and engagement should not be treated as synonymous with learning. Current AR-in-HE reviews continue to discuss implementation challenges as well as participatory benefits, so fashion-specific claims need direct evaluation rather than extrapolation from enthusiasm. 
MDPI

[ADJACENT][PRACTITIONER] AR try-on/VTON is a large nearby application area but is outside this harvest; its commercial prevalence does not establish a pedagogy for authoring fashion-communication AR.

## 10. Generative-AI authorship in studio-based fashion education and assessment

[ESTABLISHED][HE] This is one of the best-evidenced 2024–2026 areas in the harvest. Anwar, Raza & Marzouk’s 2025 systematic review synthesises 28 sources and organises the field around knowledge construction, learner identity/authorship, teaching practice, and studio culture/ethics. DOI 10.65136/ejbm.v10i4.24. 
ejbm.apu.edu.my

[EMERGING][HE] An & Park’s 2026 instructor study adds fashion-specific empirical evidence from ten university instructors in South Korea and the US. It maps AI support onto problem definition, ideation/refinement, prototype development and evaluation while stressing learner autonomy and human–AI collaboration. 

[EMERGING][HE] Hardaker & Penfold provide a concrete fashion-textiles case in which staff and students co-created rules of engagement for Midjourney as a visualisation tool; four students incorporated generated imagery into collection development, with questionnaires and focus groups used for evaluation. 

[EMERGING][HE] Balasubramanian’s 2026 work explicitly proposes curriculum integration through a TPACK framing, indicating movement from ad-hoc experimentation toward named instructional frameworks. 
Frontiers

[ESTABLISHED][ADJACENT-HE] General higher-education assessment work has moved away from a simple ban/allow binary. Furze, Perkins, Roe & MacVaugh’s 2024 AI Assessment Scale pilot provides graded levels of acceptable GenAI involvement and focuses assessment design on human contribution and critical thinking. 

[EMERGING][PRACTITIONER] Fashion schools are correspondingly shifting attention from final polish toward process and critical judgement; reporting from London College of Fashion describes assessment changes intended to make the student’s reasoning around AI more visible. This is current field reporting rather than a peer-reviewed efficacy study. 
Vogue

[ESTABLISHED] Durable terminology: authorship, creative agency, process evidence, studio critique, reflective practice, assessment validity. Frontier terms: human–AI co-creation, AI literacy, prompt provenance, synthetic media disclosure and AI-assisted studio practice.

[EMERGING] Field-flagged open problems are explicit: Anwar et al. call for longitudinal, empirical and cross-cultural validation of impacts on learning and authorship; An & Park call for student-perspective studies and classroom evaluation of specific tools. 
ejbm.apu.edu.my

[ESTABLISHED] Critiques are substantive rather than merely anti-technology: originality, copyright/appropriation, dataset ethics, equity of access, personal design voice, cognitive dependence and the displacement of tactile/studio knowledge recur across recent design-education literature. 
ejbm.apu.edu.my

[EMERGING] The fashion-specific evidence still does not justify treating prompting skill as equivalent to visual/design mastery; recent instructor research instead places AI within a larger design process including planning, technical construction, communication and evaluation. 

## 11. Fashion composition references and visual-research pedagogy

[ESTABLISHED] Moodboards remain a recognised design-research device, not merely an informal inspiration collage. Research describes them as tools for organising references, inspiration and decision-making, with composition affecting how viewers read them. 
ResearchGate

[EMERGING][HE] Silvia’s 2025 Enhancing Futures Driven Design Education Through Moodboards develops explicit guidelines for moodboard composition as an educational output of metadesign/trend research, positioning moodboards as tools for visualising research findings and complexity rather than decorative assemblages. Journal of Futures Studies, 2025. 
Journal of Futures Studies

[EMERGING][HE] AI-mediated moodboarding has now entered fashion vocational curriculum research; Nazli’s 2025 work explicitly examines AI-based moodboards in fashion TVET. The evidence is emerging and should not be treated as a settled replacement for manual visual research. 
seminars.id

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper durable terminology: visual research, design research, reference gathering, moodboard, visual analysis, composition, trend research, precedent/reference analysis. Metadesign and design futures are more specialised adjacent terms rather than synonyms for all fashion reference work. 
Journal of Futures Studies

[EMERGING] A useful distinction now visible in the literature is between moodboard-as-inspiration and moodboard-as-research synthesis; Silvia’s work explicitly argues for the latter as a way of encoding research findings, values and behavioural patterns. 
Journal of Futures Studies

[UNVERIFIED-GAP][HE] I found no strong 2024–2026 fashion-HE evidence establishing a common teaching sequence for source criticism, image provenance, citation, cultural contextualisation and formal composition of visual references.

[UNVERIFIED-GAP] Nor did I find robust comparative evidence showing whether AI-generated reference boards improve or diminish students’ ability to locate, contextualise and transform primary visual sources.

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: visual-reference pedagogy carries a canon problem as well as a composition problem. Longstanding critiques of Western/white fashion curricula show why “reference literacy” cannot be reduced to locating aesthetically compatible imagery; source selection itself structures whose histories and bodies become normative. 
Vogue

## 12. CLO-class virtual-garment education versus interface-layer teaching

[ESTABLISHED][HE] CLO-class education is now a genuine fashion-pedagogy research cluster. Hardaker & Penfold document curricular integration across years; Taylor documents a multi-institution Australian educator community specifically examining teaching and learning with CLO3D. 

[ESTABLISHED][HE] Conlon et al.’s Developing digital skills: a fashion business masterclass in 3D garment simulation provides an earlier but still relevant empirical bridge between university students, industry facilitation and virtual-prototyping skill development. Published 2024 in International Journal of Fashion Design, Technology and Education. 
Taylor & Francis Online

[EMERGING][HE] The dominant pedagogical model is currently hybrid: students transfer pattern-cutting and garment-construction knowledge into digital simulation instead of learning the software as an isolated graphics application. Hardaker & Penfold explicitly sequence manual foundations before mandatory first-year CLO3D and retain analogue tuition later. 

[EMERGING][HE] Taylor’s Australian study describes CLO3D curriculum design as sufficiently immature that educators are still sharing baseline practices, identifying common problems and developing a community of practice; this makes the field active but not standardised. 

[PLATFORM] CLO3D itself should therefore be classified as a platform/toolchain, not the underlying disciplinary construct. Durable constructs include pattern logic, drape, silhouette, material behaviour, fit interpretation, spatial reasoning and iterative prototyping; software-specific UI knowledge is comparatively volatile. The distinction is visible in curricula that teach analogue pattern fundamentals before the software layer. 

[UNVERIFIED-GAP][HE] The interface-layer question remains substantially less researched: I found no equivalent fashion-education literature asking whether students explicitly learn the transferable interaction model—scene hierarchy, property panels, transforms, cameras, materials, simulation controls, asset pipelines—so that competence transfers from CLO-class software to Blender, Unity, Unreal, web 3D or other interfaces.

[EMERGING] Sharper terminology for that blank: 3D interaction literacy, digital workflow literacy, software-independent procedural knowledge, interface transfer and cross-platform digital competence. These are useful adjacent constructs, but I could not verify a settled fashion-education term for this exact phenomenon. [UNVERIFIED-GAP]

[ESTABLISHED] Critique: industry adoption pressure does not by itself establish educational value. Hardaker & Penfold explicitly frame curricular digital transformation around staff capability, infrastructure, traditional competencies and pedagogical design, while Taylor stresses shared resources and educator communities rather than software acquisition alone. 

[EMERGING] Equity is also unresolved: current CLO3D educator discussions explicitly include access and implementation challenges, while wider digital-fashion reporting documents financial barriers to specialist 3D training. 
ePrints Soton

[ADJACENT][EMERGING] Research on automatic 3D garment generation, AR/VR sketch-to-garment systems and VTON is advancing much faster technically than the pedagogical evidence. Zang et al.’s From Air to Wear (2025) and Fashion-3DLR (2026) are examples of frontier systems research; they should be treated as stimuli/adjacent research, not proof that corresponding classroom sequences exist. 

Gap-harvest reading of the map: the strongest current HE evidence clusters are CLO/3D garment workflow, hybrid analogue–digital curriculum design, and GenAI studio authorship/assessment. AR has an educational literature but little fashion-communication-specific sequence evidence. Fashion film/video is clearly taught but much less frequently studied pedagogically. The clearest unresolved curriculum-research blanks are digital image fundamentals as an integrated literacy; digital still-life; fashion-specific retouching pedagogy; authored digital humans beyond garment fitting; virtual-show staging as a complete production pedagogy; fashion portfolio web UX/accessibility linked to hiring; student-authored holographic communication; and cross-platform/interface-transfer literacy from CLO-class environments. Those gap classifications follow the asymmetry between the identifiable fashion-education studies above and the areas for which searches returned mainly programme descriptions, practitioner cases or adjacent technical studies. 
ejbm.apu.edu.my
