Research landscape map · Teaching digital fashion experiences — virtual shows, spaces, and brand staging in higher education

Coverage: literature and documented HE practice checked through 16 August 2026.
Evidence rule: [PRACTITIONER] and [SECONDARY] mark non-peer-reviewed industry/institutional evidence. Phenomenon/consumer studies are used only to establish the object of teaching or transferable design constructs, not as proof of a complete pedagogy.

## 1. Digital fashion experience design education in higher education

[ESTABLISHED] The most directly relevant pedagogical literature does not yet constitute a mature subfield called “digital fashion experience design education.” Instead, it sits across digital-fashion pedagogy, 3D/virtual product development, fashion/game-design hybridity, immersive commerce and broader fashion-technology curriculum transformation. Tepe & Koohnavard explicitly studied how HE fashion practice might create fashion-related experiences in digital worlds, while Conlon & Gallery examined an industry-facilitated virtual-3D masterclass as a curriculum intervention. Tepe, J. & Koohnavard, S., “Fashion and game design as hybrid practices: approaches in education to creating fashion-related experiences in digital worlds,” International Journal of Fashion Design, Technology and Education 16(1), 2023 [online 2022], DOI 10.1080/17543266.2022.2103591; Conlon, J. & Gallery, C., “Developing digital skills: a fashion business masterclass in virtual 3D prototyping with Style3D,” IJFDTE 17(1), 2024, DOI 10.1080/17543266.2023.2247425. 
Taylor & Francis Online

[ESTABLISHED] Tepe & Koohnavard remain the clearest canonical bridge between fashion design education and experience-making in digital worlds: their industry interviews found that meaningful digital-fashion outcomes require understanding the technical and socio-dynamic properties of digital worlds rather than simply reproducing physical garments or physical-fashion conventions digitally. 
ResearchGate

[EMERGING] Recent HE work is broadening from single-tool competence toward institution-level digital-transformation pedagogy. De Montfort University's School of Fashion and Textiles reports a co-created “Digital Manifesto” spanning practice-based courses, emphasizing staff/student/industry co-creation and interdisciplinary digital competence; its authors explicitly identify accessibility, scalability and long-term impact assessment as unresolved areas. Hardaker et al., “Towards digital transformation of fashion and textiles education: a strategic pedagogy,” IFFTI Annual Proceedings, vol. 4, 2025. 

[EMERGING] A second trajectory replaces software demonstration with short, situated industry studios/masterclasses. Conlon & Gallery's Style3D intervention is explicitly framed as informing future fashion curriculum rather than merely evaluating software. 
Taylor & Francis Online

[EMERGING] Named active centres visible in this intersection include the Department of Design, University of Borås (Tepe/Koohnavard and current digital-tools work), Fashion Business School/Centre for Fashion Business, London College of Fashion around Bethan Alexander's experiential/immersive retail research, Manchester Fashion Institute, and De Montfort University School of Fashion and Textiles. University of Borås launched a 2025–2026 “Digital tools for textile and fashion” project explicitly spanning research, education and industry; Alexander's LCF profile identifies experiential/sensory retail and innovative fashion formats as an active research area. 
Manchester Fashion Institute
Högskolan i Borås

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: I found no 2024–2026 peer-reviewed comparative study establishing a standard HE studio sequence specifically for digital fashion experience design from concept → spatial worldbuilding → interaction → audience testing → public staging. Existing evidence covers components of that chain rather than a settled pedagogy. The strategic-pedagogy literature itself calls for further refinement and long-term evaluation. 

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: digital fashion is durable but broad; fashion-related experiences in digital worlds, immersive fashion experience, virtual fashion experience, immersive retailing, XR fashion and digital fashion experience are more precise depending on object. Recent historical work stresses that digital fashion encompasses virtual garments, game skins, AR filters, digital twins/phygital fashion and AI-mediated forms rather than a single technology. Boughlala et al., “Tracing the History of Digital Fashion,” 2025, DOI 10.1177/0887302X241283504. 

## 2. Teaching virtual fashion shows and staged brand environments

[ESTABLISHED] The virtual fashion show now has a substantial phenomenon/UX literature that can ground production teaching, particularly around presence, immersion, interaction, spatiality and aesthetic experience. Kim's VR-fashion-show study distinguishes cognitive presence, sensible immersion, emotional immersion and aesthetic interaction as components of virtual fashion experience. Se Jin Kim, “Virtual fashion experiences in virtual reality fashion show spaces,” Frontiers in Psychology 14, 2023, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1276856. 
Frontiers

[ESTABLISHED] Interaction is not merely decorative in this literature. Ahn, Bae & Kim designed an interactive VR fashion show manipulating elements such as viewpoint, clothing, effects and environment specifically to investigate UX. “User Experience of a Digital Fashion Show: Exploring the Effectiveness of Interactivity in Virtual Reality,” Applied Sciences 13(4), 2558, 2023, DOI 10.3390/app13042558. 
ScholarWorks

[EMERGING] More recent work shifts attention from “is VR engaging?” toward spatial dramaturgy and imaginary brand space. Cho et al.'s 2025 luxury-fashion-show study examines how the imaginary space of a VR fashion show affects perceived benefits, risks and brand word-of-mouth; this is phenomenon evidence, not evidence of a teaching sequence. 
Yonsei University

[EMERGING] Manchester Fashion Institute supplies a recent HE/industry production case: in February 2026 its students participated in an AI-driven, immersive, shoppable runway combining physical models, virtual avatars, interactive purchasing and production roles including content creation, dressing and backstage work. This documents situated event-production learning, although it is institutional reporting rather than controlled pedagogical research. [PRACTITIONER] Manchester Fashion Institute, “Fashion students and graduates collaborate with THG Studios and Topshop…,” 6 March 2026. 
Manchester Fashion Institute

[ESTABLISHED] A load-bearing antecedent is experiential/event design rather than fashion software alone: digital fashion shows combine choreography, scenography, spectatorship, temporality, media direction and brand narrative. Industry commentary from production specialists has long argued that virtual experiences should not simply imitate a physical runway; digital media require their own experiential logic. [SECONDARY] Vogue, “Physical? Digital? Virtual? The Masterminds of Fashion Show Production Weigh In on Runway's Future,” 2020. 
Vogue

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: I found no validated 2024–2026 fashion-education rubric for judging student virtual fashion shows across staging, spatial storytelling, interaction, garment legibility, audience experience and technical reliability.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: virtual fashion show is durable and reasonably precise; digital runway, immersive runway, virtual catwalk and phygital fashion show describe implementations. Scenography, experience design, brand experience and spatial storytelling are more durable conceptual vocabularies than platform-specific “metaverse show.”

## 3. Curriculum for immersive fashion retail and exhibition experiences

[ESTABLISHED] Immersive-fashion teaching sits at the intersection of two better-developed literatures: experiential/sensory retailing and museum/exhibition/object-based learning. At London College of Fashion, Bethan Alexander's research programme explicitly covers experiential and sensory retailing, customer experience, retail technologies and innovative retail formats. 

[EMERGING] Alexander et al.'s recent fashion-metaverse work analyzes customer experience across cognitive, affective, behavioural and social dimensions and identifies both motivations and pain points in branded immersive spaces. That makes customer experience/CX a stronger research vocabulary for brand-space evaluation than undifferentiated “immersion.” Alexander et al., “The Role of Immersive Spaces on the Customer Experience: An Exploration of Fashion Metaverses,” Psychology & Marketing 42(2), 2025, pp. 539–553, DOI 10.1002/mar.22140. 
Wiley Online Library

[ESTABLISHED] Fashion exhibition education already has direct student evidence. Smith-Glaviana studied 62 university students viewing a narrated digital fashion exhibition; the tour supported object-based learning, and the study identifies multiple garment views, close-ups, time for observation/reflection and multisensory engagement as design considerations. “University Students' Experience of a Digital Fashion Exhibition: Engagement, Embodiment, and Object-Based Learning,” Clothing and Textiles Research Journal 43(1), 2025, DOI 10.1177/0887302X231161641. 
Sage Journals

[ESTABLISHED] That same study also prevents an overly celebratory digital-only interpretation: earlier online object-based learning was better for some silhouette/form observations but weaker for students wanting construction and fabric detail; hybrid physical/digital access was therefore recommended in the underlying literature. 
Sage Journals

[EMERGING] A 2025 University of Manchester flexible-learning pilot exposed students to SEAMM for 3D environments, virtual try-on, interactive journeys and cross-sector gamification, explicitly coupling experience design with inclusion, algorithmic personalisation, privacy, accessibility and interoperability. This is documented pedagogical practice, but not a peer-reviewed fashion-school effectiveness trial. [PRACTITIONER/HE REPORT] Flexible Learning Pilot 2025. 
StaffNet

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: robust comparative evidence is missing on whether students learn fashion spatial communication better through head-mounted VR, browser-based 3D, projection-based installations, game engines or hybrid physical/digital environments.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: experiential retailing, immersive retailing, fashion exhibition design, digital exhibition, brand experience, customer experience/CX and object-based learning are durable. Metaverse retail is frontier/context-dependent.

## 4. Transferring event-design literacy to digital fashion spaces

[ESTABLISHED] The literature supports treating digital fashion space as an experience system rather than a garment viewer. Tepe & Koohnavard specifically warn against reproducing physical-fashion outputs inside digital worlds and argue that digital-world socio-dynamics and technical conditions must inform design practice. DOI 10.1080/17543266.2022.2103591. 
ResearchGate

[ESTABLISHED] Transferable constructs documented in fashion-show research include presence, immersion, interactivity, point of view, environmental effects and aesthetic interaction. Kim 2023 and Ahn, Bae & Kim 2023 provide fashion-specific empirical grounding for these constructs. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1276856; DOI 10.3390/app13042558. 
Frontiers

[EMERGING] The conceptual centre of gravity is shifting from “virtual replica” toward spatially authored experience. Recent fashion-metaverse CX research treats environmental dimensions themselves as determinants of user response, while immersive retail research similarly examines the store/environment as an experience generator. 
Wiley Online Library

[EMERGING] Current practice is also hybrid rather than exclusively virtual. Recent fashion-industry cases combine shared physical installations, large-scale projection, smart mirrors, VR and conventional retail; Vogue Business reported this movement away from purely headset/at-home “metaverse” experiences toward physically anchored immersive encounters in 2024. [SECONDARY] 
Vogue Business

[EMERGING] Manchester Fashion Institute's 2026 Topshop collaboration demonstrates another form of transfer: students were exposed not only to garment presentation but also to backstage production, live audience, interactive commerce, content creation and AI-mediated visual systems. [PRACTITIONER] 
Manchester Fashion Institute

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: I found no peer-reviewed fashion-education study isolating whether explicit prior teaching in event production/scenography improves students' digital-world design outcomes.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: scenography, spatial dramaturgy, experience design, event design, spatial storytelling and experiential branding are durable transfer terms; worldbuilding is increasingly useful but broader and currently fast-moving in AI/game-engine discourse.

## 5. Assessing student digital-experience prototypes in fashion schools

[ESTABLISHED] Assessment is one of the weakest evidenced areas in the specific field. Existing fashion-digital pedagogy studies more often evaluate learner confidence, perceptions, skill acquisition or curriculum interventions than validated assessment instruments for complete immersive experience prototypes. Conlon & Gallery evaluate a virtual-product-development masterclass; Balasubramanian evaluates student perceptions and confidence after GenAI curriculum exposure. 
Research Explorer

[EMERGING] Balasubramanian's 2026 fashion-design curriculum study used a six-week studio module with direct instruction, hands-on experimentation and iterative feedback, followed by mixed-method analysis of student responses. It provides a current example of process-oriented evaluation in digitally augmented fashion studio teaching, although the output was a digital capsule collection rather than an immersive show/space. Frontiers in Education 11:1824003, DOI 10.3389/feduc.2026.1824003. 
Frontiers

[ESTABLISHED] Earlier 3D fashion pedagogy has used structured instructional-design frameworks rather than ad-hoc software teaching. Research on remote 3D virtual prototyping describes a flipped product-development course designed and evaluated within an ADDIE framework. 
Sage Journals

[EMERGING] A 2025 apparel study developing a simulation-based instrument for measuring 3D-to-2D spatial ability illustrates increasing attention to specific measurable digital-fashion competences, but this remains narrower than assessing an authored spatial brand experience. 

[ESTABLISHED] The adjacent immersive-media field warns that assessment terminology itself is unstable: a 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of cinematic VR found inconsistent use of presence, immersion and narrative engagement, irregular questionnaire selection and incomplete reporting. This methodological warning is directly relevant when fashion schools borrow immersive-UX metrics. Zhang et al., “Exploring Viewing Modalities in Cinematic Virtual Reality…,” 2024. 

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: no verifiable fashion-specific validated instrument was found for jointly scoring spatial coherence, fashion communication, interaction, accessibility, narrative/brand fit, audience experience and technical execution in a student-produced digital-fashion environment.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: authentic assessment, prototype evaluation, user-experience evaluation, design critique, practice-based assessment and constructive alignment are durable adjacent educational terms; no fashion-specific “virtual-show assessment” vocabulary appears settled.

## 6. “Metaverse fashion” as a cautious academic teaching category

[ESTABLISHED] “Metaverse” should be treated as a historically situated umbrella rather than the stable name of the whole field. Recent digital-fashion historiography locates metaverse/Web3 alongside older virtual worlds, games, AR, digital fashion, NFTs and newer AI rather than treating it as an inevitable destination. Boughlala et al., “Tracing the History of Digital Fashion,” 2025, DOI 10.1177/0887302X241283504. 

[ESTABLISHED] Dwivedi et al.'s large multidisciplinary 2022 synthesis is the canonical reference for both the potential and contested definition of the metaverse; the later fashion literature inherits those conceptual ambiguities rather than resolving them. “Metaverse beyond the hype: Multidisciplinary perspectives…,” International Journal of Information Management, 2022. 
ScienceDirect

[EMERGING] The 2024–2026 fashion literature increasingly studies specific immersive spaces, virtual worlds, gaming environments, XR and digital-fashion ecosystems rather than assuming one interoperable metaverse. Alexander's immersive-spaces research and the 2026 Research Agenda for the Advancement of Digital Fashion exemplify this broader framing. 
Wiley Online Library

[EMERGING] Recent industry discourse explicitly describes a post-hype recalibration after the NFT/crypto/metaverse boom, with activity migrating toward games, mixed reality, physical–digital products and utility rather than disappearing. This is industry evidence, not pedagogical evidence. [SECONDARY] Vogue Business, “Is digital fashion still in fashion?”, 4 June 2024. 
Vogue

[ESTABLISHED] Metaverse Fashion Week is therefore a legitimate case-study object, but not evidence that “metaverse production” is a settled graduate competency. Recent scholarship still asks whether fashion-week implementations represent durable strategy or costly fad. “Metaverse Fashion Week: A Costly Fad or a Future Worth Exploring?”, 2025. 

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: no 2024–2026 fashion-education consensus document was found that defines “metaverse literacy” as a discrete HE learning outcome.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: virtual worlds, immersive environments, XR, social virtual worlds, game worlds, spatial computing and digital fashion ecosystems are generally sharper. Durable: virtual worlds/XR/immersive environments. Frontier: metaverse, spatial computing, Web3 fashion.

## 7. AI-assisted worldbuilding for fashion experiences and authorship

[EMERGING] Direct empirical fashion-pedagogy evidence on GenAI now exists. Balasubramanian's 2026 study embeds Adobe Firefly, Photoshop and Illustrator in a six-week senior-undergraduate fashion module structured through TPACK, active learning, experimentation and iterative feedback. Students reported increased confidence but also tensions around creativity, efficiency, authenticity and future adoption. DOI 10.3389/feduc.2026.1824003. 
Frontiers

[EMERGING] That paper explicitly identifies limited empirical research on GenAI in creative, studio-based fashion disciplines, particularly end-to-end design processes, as a research gap. This caution applies even more strongly to AI-assisted spatial/worldbuilding pedagogy. 
Frontiers

[EMERGING] Drakou et al. provide another recent fashion-education contribution on AI-driven 3D design, linking AI/3D workflows to learning, creativity and sustainability. “Transforming Fashion Education: The Impact of AI-Driven 3D Design on Learning, Creativity, and Sustainability,” SETN 2024, DOI 10.1145/3688671.3688794. 
ResearchGate

[EMERGING] The University of Manchester 2025 pilot explicitly surfaced a student distinction between “design by AI” and “design with AI,” with creative students questioning aesthetic authorship and control; its immersive-commerce component also coupled automated systems with 3D-space creation and user journeys. [HE REPORT] 
StaffNet

[EMERGING] At the technical frontier, generative systems increasingly create garments, textures and other 3D assets from textual or sketch input, but these computer-graphics results demonstrate tool capability, not educational effectiveness or authorship competence. He et al., “DressCode: Autoregressively Sewing and Generating Garments from Text Guidance,” 2024; Zang et al., “From Air to Wear…,” 2025. 

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: no peer-reviewed fashion-education study was found that tests an AI-assisted worldbuilding sequence covering environment generation, spatial narrative, fashion assets, interaction design, provenance and final audience experience.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: AI-assisted design, human–AI co-creation, co-creative AI, generative design and AI literacy are more durable than tool-specific “prompt engineering.” Text-to-3D, world models, AI worldbuilding and agentic creative pipelines remain fast-moving frontier terms.

[ESTABLISHED] Controversy: current fashion pedagogy cannot reduce authorship to output ownership alone; empirical student work already raises authenticity and human-replacement concerns, while the wider fashion sector is confronting disputes over synthetic models and AI-derived likenesses. Balasubramanian 2026 supplies the pedagogical evidence; recent model/likeness disputes supply industry context. 
Frontiers

## 8. Accessibility of digital fashion experiences for diverse audiences

[ESTABLISHED] Immersion does not automatically increase accessibility. Disability/XR scholarship finds that virtual participation can improve access for some people while excluding others through embodied interaction requirements, inaccessible interfaces and overlooked disability-specific harms. Aragon et al., “Expanding Accessibility in Immersive Virtual Spaces: A Comprehensive Approach for All Disabilities,” 2024. 

[ESTABLISHED] Earlier metaverse-accessibility research similarly argues that disabled users and creators need to participate actively in platform design and that standards and regulation remain incomplete. Radanliev, De Roure, Novitzky & Sluganovic, “Accessibility and Inclusiveness of New Information and Communication Technologies for Disabled Users and Content Creators in the Metaverse,” 2023. 

[EMERGING] Fashion-education practice is beginning to integrate accessibility at the experience-design rather than garment-only level. The Manchester 2025 immersive-interface workshop asked students to consider design for inclusion, XR accessibility, personalisation, privacy and interoperable design while constructing 3D environments and interactive journeys. [HE REPORT] 
StaffNet

[EMERGING] De Montfort's 2025 strategic digital-pedagogy work independently identifies accessibility as an area requiring further development in its digital-transformation framework, confirming that the problem is recognized internally by fashion educators rather than inferred externally. 

[ESTABLISHED] Digital exhibition research also shows that multimodal representation matters: fashion learners benefit from multiple views and detail imagery, while digital access may remain inferior for tactile/material investigation. Smith-Glaviana 2025, DOI 10.1177/0887302X231161641. 
Sage Journals

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: I found no fashion-specific 2024–2026 controlled study evaluating student-created immersive fashion experiences with disabled participants across motor, visual, auditory, cognitive and vestibular access.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: XR accessibility, inclusive immersive design, accessible interaction, multimodal access, participatory/co-design with disabled people and universal design are more durable than “accessible metaverse.”

[ESTABLISHED] Controversy: accessibility claims based solely on “anyone can attend virtually” are methodologically inadequate; immersive systems can remove geographical barriers while introducing device, sensory, motor, cognitive and interface barriers. 

## 9. Case-based teaching of industry digital-fashion campaigns

[ESTABLISHED] Industry cases are unusually important in this field because production practice is evolving faster than peer-reviewed pedagogy, but case exposure and pedagogical validation are not equivalent. Conlon & Gallery's masterclass is important precisely because it converts industry expertise into an explicitly researched educational intervention. DOI 10.1080/17543266.2023.2247425. 
Research Explorer

[EMERGING] Current case material encompasses much more than virtual runway video: AI/physical hybrid catwalks, interactive commerce, avatars, game-world fashion, AR, virtual stores and projection-based environments are all active practice forms. Manchester's 2026 Topshop collaboration is one documented HE/industry example. 
Manchester Fashion Institute

[ESTABLISHED] Earlier cases such as Hanifa's digitally staged runway, virtual fashion weeks and game-platform activations remain historically important because they established the distinction between digital documentation of a show and a show/event conceived natively for a digital medium. [SECONDARY] 
Vogue

[EMERGING] Vogue's Inventing the Runway and related large-scale immersive exhibition formats are relevant current objects for case study because they translate fashion-show history into projection-led shared environments rather than individual VR. Fashion Retail Academy documented student study visits to the exhibition in April 2025, but provides no comparative learning-outcome evaluation. [PRACTITIONER] 
Fashion Retail Academy

[EMERGING] Manchester Fashion Institute's 2025 graduate showcase INTERFACES explicitly framed immersive presentation, exhibition and storytelling as mechanisms for showing process and creative narratives beyond finished runway looks, demonstrating how showcase format itself is being rethought within fashion HE. [PRACTITIONER] 
Manchester Fashion Institute

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: there is no identified standard corpus of academically annotated digital-fashion campaign cases comparable to established business-school case repositories, with production documentation, audience evidence, critical commentary and teaching notes.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: case-based learning, live brief, industry project, situated learning, experiential learning, industry masterclass and practice-based learning are durable educational terms. Brand activation, immersive campaign and phygital activation describe the practice object.

[ESTABLISHED] Methodological caution: brand engagement, purchase intention or novelty scores from consumer research should not be treated as proof that a campaign is pedagogically exemplary. They measure audience/marketing effects rather than student understanding.

## 10. Ethics of labour and visibility in digital-fashion experience production

[ESTABLISHED] Fashion's move toward digital/AI experience production does not erase labour; it redistributes it across designers, 3D artists, developers, content producers, event technicians, data workers, models and platform infrastructures. Research on digital labour broadly documents persistent issues of low pay, opacity and lack of recognition among workers supporting AI systems. Rahman & Sultana, “Digital Labor: Challenges, Ethical Insights, and Implications,” 2025 systematic review/preprint. 

[ESTABLISHED] Fashion's pre-existing labour problems remain relevant rather than being superseded by virtuality. Current fashion research and investigations continue to document exploitation and weak supply-chain visibility; therefore claims that “digital fashion” is intrinsically ethical cannot be generalized from reduced sampling or physical production alone. 
ScienceDirect

[EMERGING] AI introduces an additional visibility/authorship/labour substitution problem in fashion imaging and presentation. Recent disputes involving AI-generated model likenesses illustrate questions about consent, residual rights and displacement of paid modelling work. [SECONDARY] 
Business Insider

[EMERGING] Fashion educators are beginning to position AI as augmentation rather than automatic replacement. In Balasubramanian's 2026 fashion-design module, students explicitly reflected on AI as a supporting/collaborative tool and raised authenticity, efficiency and human-creativity concerns. DOI 10.3389/feduc.2026.1824003. 
Frontiers

[EMERGING] Digital-fashion pedagogy is also beginning to connect technology with sustainability and ethics rather than employability alone. De Montfort's 2025 strategic pedagogy explicitly couples digital transformation with Education for Sustainable Development and identifies long-term impact evaluation as unfinished work. 

[ESTABLISHED] The sustainability claim itself remains contested: reduced physical prototyping can plausibly reduce some material sampling, but the digital-fashion literature does not support treating virtual production as environmentally cost-free. Blazquez et al., “The Metaverse and its potential for digital sustainability in fashion,” 2024, explicitly frames digital sustainability as a question requiring analysis rather than an automatic consequence of digitisation. 
Taylor & Francis Online

[UNVERIFIED] Blank: I found no 2024–2026 fashion-education study that systematically teaches students to document the full labour provenance of a digital fashion experience—3D asset creation, motion capture, AI training/data labour, moderation, software/platform services, event labour and conventional garment labour—and then evaluates learning outcomes.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology: digital labour, invisible labour, platform labour, creative labour, algorithmic labour, human-in-the-loop, provenance, consent, likeness rights and supply-chain transparency are sharper than a generic “AI ethics” label.

[ESTABLISHED] Controversy: a recurring methodological danger is visibility substitution: highly visible virtual garments, avatars and immersive spectacle can make the less-visible material, technical and human labour supporting the experience harder—not easier—to inspect. The wider digital-labour literature and current fashion supply-chain evidence justify treating labour visibility as a distinct analytic problem.
