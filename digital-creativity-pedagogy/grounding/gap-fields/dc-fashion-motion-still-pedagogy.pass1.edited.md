Field map · Fashion animation and digital still-life pedagogy in higher education

Evidence window: emphasis on 2024–16 August 2026, with older sources retained where foundational.
Scope rule: fashion-specific pedagogy is privileged. Animation, photography, accessibility, video-quality, or general design-education literature is identified as adjacent rather than silently treated as fashion evidence.

## 1. Fashion animation and motion-graphics education in higher education

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — moving image is now a normal component of fashion-communication education, but usually under “moving image”, “fashion film”, “fashion media”, or “digital production”, not a distinct scholarly field called fashion animation pedagogy. London College of Fashion's current provision explicitly treats moving image as fashion communication; its 2025 Fashion Moving Image Production unit brought together Fashion Styling and Production, Fashion Photography, Hair and Make-up for Fashion, and Fashion Imaging and Illustration students to produce short works combining storytelling, style, and visual experimentation. Paris College of Art similarly combines still/moving image production, editing and fashion-film history. UAL, “Fashion in Motion” (2025); PCA, MA Fashion Film & Photography curriculum. 

[ESTABLISHED] The dominant pedagogical model is studio/project production rather than animation-theory instruction in isolation. Current programmes foreground concept development, storyboarding, pre-production, shooting, lighting, editing and portfolio production; LCF describes fashion-film education through ideation, production management and organisational skills, while PCA explicitly sequences studio production with video and photo labs. UAL, MA Fashion, Film and Digital Production; PCA, MA Fashion Film & Photography. 

[EMERGING] The principal institutional nodes visible in the recent evidence are programmes and networks rather than dedicated “fashion-animation pedagogy labs”. IFFTI's 2025 proceedings include work on digital-fashion pedagogy and strategic digital transformation, while Fashion in Film remains an identifiable research platform studying fashion across the history of moving images. I found no comparably visible laboratory whose primary remit is empirical research on teaching fashion animation. IFFTI, Proceedings 2025; Fashion in Film, Research. 

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — “fashion film” is a mature enough interdisciplinary object to support pedagogy independently of generic film studies. The peer-reviewed journal Film, Fashion & Consumption explicitly joins film, fashion, design and art-history research; the AHRC-funded Archaeology of Fashion Film project defined fashion film as a hybrid form produced by two industries with distinct practices and histories. 
Intellect Books

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — the shift from still fashion image to moving fashion image predates present AI/video tooling. Nathalie Khan's “Why the Fashion Image Is No Longer Still” (2012) analyses stillness and movement as constitutive of fashion imagery rather than treating movement as merely a technical add-on. 
Taylor & Francis Online

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — I found no 2024–2026 peer-reviewed comparative study establishing which higher-education pedagogy best teaches fashion animation specifically. Current sources document modules, practices and broader digital-fashion transformation, but not controlled comparisons of animation-pedagogy models. The gap is consistent with An & Park's finding that pedagogical integration of digital/AI tools in fashion education remains underexplored relative to their technical applications. Hyosun An & Minjung Park, “AI tools and fashion design education” (2026), Fashion and Textiles. 

[EMERGING] Terminology — fashion moving image and fashion film are more durable specialist terms than fashion animation; motion graphics remains adjacent. Current LCF and other fashion programmes consistently use “moving image” and “fashion film”, whereas animation schools use “motion design”, “2D animation” and “stop motion”. 
CSVPA

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — equating digitalisation with pedagogical progress is contested inside fashion education. Ryan's 2025 work on digital evolution in fashion education explicitly frames curriculum change as a tension between preserving disciplinary skills and integrating new digital competencies; An & Park likewise argue for balancing tactile/hands-on fashion learning with AI-enabled practices. 
ePrints Soton

## 2. 2D animation principles in fashion communication curricula

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — fashion programmes teach the production grammar surrounding animation more consistently than they document formal teaching of animation principles. Current fashion-film courses explicitly include storyboards, camera movement, editing, titles, sound and narrative; publicly documented fashion curricula rarely state that Johnston and Thomas's twelve principles are assessed as such. PCA, Digital Lab 1: Video; CSVPA, Fashion Branding & Creative Communication. 
Paris College of Art

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — Johnston and Thomas's twelve animation principles remain the canonical transferable vocabulary for timing, anticipation, staging, arcs, secondary action, follow-through, slow-in/slow-out and related qualities. Frank Thomas & Ollie Johnston, The Illusion of Life: Disney Animation (1981); the principles were subsequently transferred explicitly to computer animation by John Lasseter. 
Wikipedia

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — the computer-animation transfer is itself canonical. John Lasseter's “Principles of Traditional Animation Applied to 3D Computer Animation”, SIGGRAPH 1987, argued that traditional animation principles remain applicable after the transition from hand-drawn to computer-generated movement. 
Wikipedia

[EMERGING] Adjacent design-education evidence continues to treat animation principles as relevant to contemporary teaching. Singh's review, “Use of Animation in Design Education—A Review” (published online in the 2024–2025 period), specifically examines animation as a component of design education rather than merely animation used to deliver educational content. DOI: 10.1177/10567879241278685. 
Sage Journals

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — no verifiable fashion-education study located tests whether explicitly teaching the twelve principles improves fashion-film or fashion-motion outcomes compared with learning them implicitly through production critique. This should remain a blank rather than being inferred from animation-school practice.

[EMERGING] Terminology — principles of animation is durable/fundamental; motion design principles, kinetic typography, 2D motion graphics, and fashion moving image are more context-specific terms. Current motion-design curricula span 2D/3D animation, graphic design, VFX, stop motion, storytelling and sound rather than defining motion solely through character animation. 
Ringling College of Art and Design

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — the Disney principles are historically situated rather than a complete contemporary evaluation system. They were developed for character animation and physical/emotional believability; their continued relevance does not make them measures of fashion narrative, branding, garment legibility, cultural meaning or ethical representation. Their documented original scope is animation, while fashion-film scholarship establishes a separate fashion/media discourse. 
Wikipedia

## 3. Digital still-life and fashion-product staging pedagogy

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — contemporary still-life instruction remains strongly studio-based and commission-oriented. ECAL's Fall 2024 Applied Photography course centred still life, asking students to interpret objects and transform everyday things into “objects of desire” using commercial/product-photography methods; another ECAL description emphasises responding to commissions and understanding constructed studio images. ECAL FEED, Applied Photography, Charles Negre (2024). 
Ecal

[ESTABLISHED] Lighting, styling, composition and controlled construction remain the durable technical core. Current product/still-life teaching descriptions explicitly foreground professional lighting setups, shadow manipulation, composition and styling rather than treating post-production as a substitute for staging. 
City Lit

[ESTABLISHED] Fashion education increasingly situates still-life practice within a larger still/moving-image continuum. PCA teaches lighting and studio practice for still and moving imagery in the same fashion-film/photography programme, while LCF's MA Fashion Photography explicitly spans analogue, digital, moving-image and emerging platforms. 
Paris College of Art

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — the academic/professional distinction between commercial/editorial fashion photography and still-life/product imagery remains useful. Eldemerdash's study of fashion-photography types explicitly distinguishes commercial, editorial, high-fashion, still-life and related photographic categories. D. Eldemerdash, 2021. 
IDJ

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — I found no 2024–2026 peer-reviewed literature defining a settled higher-education pedagogy called digital still-life, digital bodegón, or bodegón digital within fashion communication. The practice is clearly taught, but the research vocabulary remains photography/still life/product photography/constructed image rather than “digital bodegón”.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology — still-life photography, product photography, constructed image, commercial photography, and object photography are more durable and searchable than digital still-life; CG product visualization is a separate, faster-moving digital-production frontier. ECAL itself uses both “still life photography” and “constructed images”. 
Ecal

[EMERGING] Critique — contemporary fashion image-making increasingly disputes the assumption that a product-only image is merely descriptive. Fashion-image practice uses still life to carry identity, cultural and narrative meanings; for example, PhotoVogue's discussion with Marisol Mendez explicitly describes still-life references being repurposed to reframe traditional shoes and cultural representation. 
Vogue

## 4. Bodegón digital and composed product imagery teaching

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — the stronger pedagogical concept is the constructed image: object selection, surface/background, lighting, styling, framing, colour, compositing and post-production are treated as one authored image-making system. ECAL's 2024 applied-photography teaching explicitly frames studio photography around the “challenges of constructed images”, while current product-photography teaching couples lighting with styling, composition and narrative. 
Ecal

[ESTABLISHED] Digital post-production is normally taught downstream of image construction, not as an isolated effect layer. PCA's current curriculum combines capture/lighting with RAW processing, masks, compositing, selections, layers, colour/B&W processing and explicitly non-destructive workflows. 
Paris College of Art

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — form, balance, proportion, composition and visual communication continue to function as entry-level criteria for fashion image-making. Current Fashion Image & Styling portfolio guidance from UCA explicitly asks applicants to demonstrate balance, proportion, composition, visual communication, experimentation and visual research. 
University for the Creative Arts - UCA

[EMERGING] The contemporary object image is often assessed through narrative or brand communication as well as visual finish. Product-photography training increasingly describes the still life as a means to communicate brand identity, atmosphere or narrative, rather than only rendering the object's material properties accurately. 
Visual Education

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — no fashion-HE research located provides a validated rubric specifically for bodegón digital covering lighting, material rendering, compositional hierarchy, product legibility, styling and narrative coherence. Existing fashion rubrics retrieved concern garment construction, illustration or general fashion criticism rather than this image-making subfield. 
ResearchGate

[EMERGING] Terminology — constructed photography/constructed image and product still life are durable; CGI still life, virtual photography, synthetic product imagery, and AI-generated product imagery belong to the frontier. The recent fashion-AI literature already encompasses image generation and realistic visualisation, but not yet a settled pedagogical taxonomy for digital still life. Balasubramanian (2026), DOI 10.3389/feduc.2026.1824003. 
Frontiers

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — pedagogy and public exhibition must not be collapsed. A curriculum can teach production and critique without thereby establishing norms for consent, cultural representation or public circulation; contemporary fashion-image discourse explicitly raises questions of positionality, representation and respectful collaboration. 
Vogue

## 5. Evaluating fashion-motion quality beyond generic video metrics

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — there is a real evaluation gap between technical video quality and fashion-image quality. VMAF is explicitly a perceptual video encoding/streaming quality metric combining objective image/video-quality features; it was not designed to judge fashion storytelling, styling, garment visibility, editing rhetoric or creative direction. Netflix, “Toward a Practical Perceptual Video Quality Metric” (2016); Bampis, Li & Bovik (2018), “SpatioTemporal Feature Integration and Model Fusion for Full Reference Video Quality Assessment”. 
techblog.netflix.com

[ESTABLISHED] Fashion-film scholarship supplies domain-relevant dimensions absent from VMAF. Recent audience research examines costume and cinematography as meaningful fashion-film variables, while programmes explicitly teach visual narrative, art direction, fashion history and campaign context alongside technical production. Lopes et al., “Screen style: Visual elements and brand identity in fashion…” (2026); PCA curriculum. 
Taylor & Francis Online

[ESTABLISHED] The animation principles supply another adjacent evaluative vocabulary — timing, anticipation, staging, follow-through, arcs, secondary action and related motion properties — but are not fashion-specific. Thomas & Johnston (1981); Lasseter (1987). 
Wikipedia

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — targeted searching found no validated 2024–2026 fashion-specific motion-quality framework combining animation quality, cinematography, garment communication, brand/narrative coherence, sound, styling and ethical representation. Current courses demonstrate these elements in practice, but no peer-reviewed measurement framework emerged. 
Paris College of Art

[EMERGING] Terminology — fashion-film aesthetics, fashion moving image, audiovisual narrative, screen style, visual narrative, and creative direction are sharper domain terms than generic video quality. Burato's “Audiovisual Narrative for Fashion Heritage” (2024) and Lopes et al.'s “Screen style” study exemplify this vocabulary. 
zmj.unibo.it

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — importing VMAF or similar metrics as creative-assessment instruments would be a category error. VMAF research evaluates distortions associated with compression, scaling and perceptual fidelity; even video-quality research discusses limitations in temporal modelling rather than fashion communication. 
ScienceDirect

[EMERGING] An adjacent methodological direction is rubric-informed assessment rather than opaque aggregate scoring. Matsuyama, Kawaguchi & Lim's IRIS work demonstrates, outside fashion, that complex time-based performance can be decomposed into interpretable rubric-linked components; this is adjacent evidence only, not a validated fashion-motion solution. 

## 6. AI-assisted animation for fashion narrative and student authorship

[EMERGING] State of the art — generative image/video systems have made still-to-fashion-motion technically feasible, but the research frontier is currently stronger in computer vision than pedagogy. Karras et al., DreamPose: Fashion Image-to-Video Synthesis via Stable Diffusion (2023), generates pose-conditioned fashion video from still images; Islam et al., FashionFlow (2023), likewise develops diffusion-based fashion video synthesis. 

[EMERGING] Fashion-education research has moved rapidly toward structured GenAI integration in 2025–2026. Balasubramanian's 2026 curriculum study used TPACK with Adobe Firefly, Photoshop and Illustrator and found that structured exposure affected students' confidence and perceptions of creativity, authenticity and efficiency; An & Park's instructor study maps AI tools against stages of the fashion-design process. Balasubramanian, “Implementing generative AI in fashion design curriculum”, Frontiers in Education 11 (2026), DOI 10.3389/feduc.2026.1824003; An & Park (2026), Fashion and Textiles. 
Frontiers

[EMERGING] Recent fashion educators explicitly identify authorship, learner autonomy and human–AI collaboration as pedagogical issues rather than purely tool-training questions. An & Park argue for intentional AI integration that preserves learner autonomy and human–AI collaboration, and explicitly state that traditional assessment criteria require revision. 

[EMERGING] Recent fashion-industry/education reporting corroborates a process shift in assessment. Vogue Business reported in January 2026 that London College of Fashion had adjusted assessment emphasis toward the student's process and critical engagement with AI rather than output quality alone. This is useful institutional evidence, but not peer-reviewed outcome research. 
Vogue

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — I found no empirical study specifically testing AI-assisted fashion animation pedagogy, authorship attribution, or learning outcomes. Existing fashion-AI studies focus principally on design ideation, image generation, 3D visualisation and broader design processes. 
Frontiers

[EMERGING] Terminology — human–AI co-creation, AI-assisted design, AIGC, generative AI literacy, and process-based/authentic assessment are the active frontier terms; authorship and creative agency are durable conceptual problems. Contemporary design research explicitly frames GenAI as forcing reconsideration of originality and authorship. Chung et al., “Measuring Creative Displacement Anxiety…” (IASDR 2025). 

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — “AI increases creativity” is not yet a safe generic claim. Fashion studies report benefits such as ideational support and confidence, but An & Park explicitly describe limited pedagogical evidence and call for classroom-based empirical evaluation; Balasubramanian also records tensions around speed, authenticity and overall efficiency. 

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — assessment scholarship increasingly rejects simple AI-detection responses. Furze, Perkins, Roe & MacVaugh's AI Assessment Scale work proposes explicitly differentiated levels of permissible AI use, while later assessment research criticises escalating surveillance/detection cycles and argues for structural assessment reform. 

## 7. Stop-motion versus timeline animation in fashion schools

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — stop motion remains present in fashion-communication curricula. Manchester Metropolitan University's current BA Fashion Communication explicitly lists static image, stop-motion graphics, short-form video and 3D among its possible communication outputs. 
Manchester Metropolitan University

[ESTABLISHED] Outside fashion, stop-motion pedagogy is well established as a materially grounded production workflow spanning storyboarding, fabrication, animation and exhibition. Avans' higher-education stop-motion programme combines story development, puppet/set fabrication, material experimentation, shooting and premiere; specialised animation degrees similarly combine 2D and stop motion. 
Avans Hogeschool

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — stop motion and timeline/keyframe digital animation are not merely two software choices: they embody different production relations to material, time and iteration. Ternan's HE case study of the historic “shooting blind” stop-motion method explicitly treats process constraints as pedagogically consequential over five years of Level-4 teaching. M. Ternan, “Art and Design Higher Education Curriculum Design: Integrating a Lost Stop Motion Method in Higher Education” (2023). 
makingsjournal.com

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — I found no fashion-specific comparative study testing educational outcomes of stop motion against keyframe/timeline animation, After Effects-style motion graphics, or AI video. Programme pages establish co-existence, not comparative efficacy.

[EMERGING] Terminology — stop-motion graphics, frame-by-frame animation, object animation, 2D motion graphics, keyframe animation, and timeline-based animation describe distinct processes more precisely than one generic “animation” category. Fashion HE currently uses “stop-motion graphics”; animation programmes use “2D animation and stop motion”. 
Manchester Metropolitan University

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — technological novelty should not automatically displace materially constrained methods. Ternan's case is explicitly about recovering a supposedly obsolete stop-motion practice because its constraint changes students' making process; fashion-education research more broadly warns against digital transformation that erases disciplinary skills. 
SHURA

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — there is currently insufficient evidence to claim that stop motion is pedagogically superior for fashion object/material literacy, even though that hypothesis is plausible. No fashion-specific comparative evidence located.

## 8. Still-life lighting and composition transfer to digital canvases

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — lighting and composition remain medium-independent foundations even as fashion image production becomes digitally hybrid. PCA teaches studio lighting for both photography and moving image and then teaches digital compositing, masks, layers and colour workflows; ECAL continues to teach object interpretation and constructed studio photography. 
Paris College of Art

[ESTABLISHED] The transfer is therefore currently visible pedagogically as a workflow continuum: physical staging → capture → digital manipulation/compositing rather than a replacement of lighting knowledge by software. PCA's curriculum explicitly places lighting/capture and advanced Photoshop workflows inside the same programme. 
Paris College of Art

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — composition, balance, proportion, visual hierarchy and light/shadow remain pre-digital competencies used to judge contemporary fashion-image portfolios. UCA's current Fashion Image & Styling requirements explicitly request balance, proportion, composition and visual communication; animation's canonical “solid drawing” principle likewise includes volume, weight, light and shadow. 
University for the Creative Arts - UCA

[EMERGING] GenAI introduces a new pedagogical complication because students can synthesise apparent lighting and staging without constructing the photographed scene. Balasubramanian's fashion curriculum work with Firefly and An & Park's broader AI-framework study both identify the need to align AI functionality with domain learning objectives rather than merely reward plausible outputs. 
Frontiers

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — no longitudinal fashion-HE study located measures transfer from physical still-life lighting instruction to CGI, generative-image, compositing or virtual-camera environments. Current programme evidence demonstrates curricular coexistence, not measured transfer.

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology — lighting design, studio lighting, constructed image, compositing, visual hierarchy, colour management and non-destructive editing are durable; synthetic imagery, virtual photography and generative relighting are frontier terms. PCA documents the durable workflow vocabulary directly. 
Paris College of Art

[EMERGING] Critique — visually convincing synthetic light should not be treated as evidence that a learner understands lighting. Recent fashion-AI research explicitly distinguishes tool-supported visualisation from pedagogical competence and calls for empirical evaluation of specific tools and learner outcomes. 

## 9. Assessing process evidence in animated fashion briefs

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — process evidence is already embedded in contemporary fashion-image education even where it is not named as an AI-era assessment framework. Current Fashion Image & Styling portfolio guidance explicitly asks for work in progress showing exploration, experimentation and development, alongside research and final work. 
University for the Creative Arts - UCA

[ESTABLISHED] Studio fashion-film programmes expose multiple assessable process stages: concept, storyboard, production planning, shooting, editing, critique, sequencing and portfolio contextualisation. PCA's current MA curriculum documents all of these stages, including portfolio work centred on authorship, editing and sequencing. 
Paris College of Art

[EMERGING] AI has increased the explicit importance assigned to process evidence. An & Park conclude that fashion education needs revised assessment criteria under AI integration, and Vogue Business reports LCF shifting attention toward student process and critical evaluation of AI rather than only polished output. 

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational adjacent evidence supports video production as a window onto learning process rather than only a finished artefact. Gama & Barroso's “Student's Video Production as Formative Assessment” (2013) found student-produced video could reveal planning, misconceptions, collaboration and project-management dimensions not visible in conventional tests. This is education-general, not fashion-specific. 

[ESTABLISHED] Rubrics remain a recognised mechanism in fashion education for making otherwise tacit judgements explicit, but fashion-specific rubric research has concentrated more on garment construction than animated communication. Research on Blackboard-embedded garment-construction rubrics explicitly frames rubrics as making evaluation criteria and performance levels visible to learners. 
ResearchGate

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — no validated rubric was located for process evidence in an animated fashion brief covering, for example, research provenance, storyboard evolution, animation tests, editing decisions, AI interventions and final critical reflection.

[EMERGING] Terminology — process evidence, process portfolio, developmental work, work-in-progress, reflective documentation, authentic assessment and process-based assessment are sharper than generic “documentation”. Fashion programmes already use the first cluster implicitly; AI-assessment scholarship supplies the latter cluster. 
University for the Creative Arts - UCA

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — process evidence is not automatically authentic merely because more files are collected. Recent AI-assessment research warns that systems demanding performances of independent authorship can provoke additional surveillance and technological circumvention; Roe et al. therefore argue for structural assessment reform rather than technological solutionism. 

## 10. Accessibility of motion media in fashion-education deliverables

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — the authoritative accessibility framework is WCAG rather than a fashion-specific motion-accessibility model. WCAG 2.2 requires captions for prerecorded synchronized media at Level A and specifies alternatives/audio description requirements for prerecorded media. W3C, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2. 

[ESTABLISHED] Motion itself can be an accessibility hazard. WCAG addresses pausing/stopping moving content and interaction-triggered motion; W3C's reduced-motion techniques explicitly note that animation can cause distraction, dizziness or nausea for users with vestibular conditions. 

[ESTABLISHED] For web-delivered fashion portfolios and motion pieces, prefers-reduced-motion is an operational accessibility mechanism, not merely a stylistic preference. W3C provides both CSS and JavaScript techniques for responding to the user's reduced-motion setting. 

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational — accessible time-based media extends beyond captions. WCAG also covers audio description/media alternatives, flashing thresholds and user control of moving/blinking content. 

[EMERGING] The broader design-education literature now directly challenges ableist assumptions in studio teaching. Ghosh & Coppola, “This Class Isn't Designed For Me: Recognizing Ableist Trends In Design Education, And Redesigning For An Inclusive And Sustainable Future” (2024), document redesign of design-course experiences for blind/low-vision, deaf/hard-of-hearing and other disabled students. 

[EMERGING] European curriculum work likewise treats accessibility as something to teach across disciplines rather than bolt onto finished outputs. The ATHENA project's 2024 transnational work explicitly addresses integrating Accessibility and Design for All into higher-education curricula. 
European Disability Forum

[UNVERIFIED] Blank — I found no 2024–2026 empirical study measuring whether fashion-animation or fashion-film courses actually teach captions, audio description, reduced-motion alternatives, flashing safety and accessible publication as assessed competencies. Existing fashion programme descriptions reviewed here discuss moving-image production extensively but do not establish a research evidence base for accessible-motion pedagogy. 
Paris College of Art

[ESTABLISHED] Terminology — durable terms are time-based media accessibility, captions, audio description, media alternatives, pause/stop/hide, flash thresholds, and reduced motion; accessible motion design is useful but less formally standardised. WCAG 2.2 provides the durable normative vocabulary. 

[ESTABLISHED] Critique — accessibility cannot be reduced to captioning spoken dialogue. WCAG separately addresses auditory information, visual information, harmful motion, flashing and control of time-based content, so an animated fashion submission can remain inaccessible despite having subtitles. 

[EMERGING] Critique — accessibility should remain analytically separate from exhibition consent. Accessibility concerns whether disabled audiences can perceive/control the media; consent and ethical representation concern the rights and relationships of represented participants. Contemporary fashion-image discourse documents the latter as a distinct issue through discussions of positionality, collaboration and representation. 
Vogue
