Research landscape map — Teaching 3D form and volume in fashion digital creativity higher education

Scope: higher-education fashion/design pedagogy concerned with seeing, representing, constructing, evaluating, and communicating three-dimensional form. VTON is excluded. Evidence from generic CAD, product-design, VR, or computer-graphics education is treated as adjacent rather than automatically transferable.

Evidence boundary: the literature is substantially stronger for 2D↔3D spatial cognition, patternmaking, fit, and virtual garment prototyping than for a standalone academic construct called “3D-form pedagogy” in fashion. Recent fashion-specific work is growing, but several frontier areas remain project-, conference-, or practitioner-led rather than supported by a mature body of comparative educational research. 

## 1. 3D form representation pedagogy in fashion and design education

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art — spatial visualization is the strongest pedagogical construct. Fashion/apparel research treats the capacity to mentally transform flat patterns, sketches, and garment components into three-dimensional forms—and back again—as a domain-specific form of spatial visualization. Moritz & Youn’s college-level study operationalised this through an Apparel Spatial Visualization Test adapted to a virtual environment (ASVT-V), finding relationships between apparel-specific spatial ability, general spatial visualization, and performance in apparel courses. Anna Moritz & Song-yi Youn, “Spatial ability of transitioning 2D to 3D designs in virtual environment: understanding spatial ability in apparel design education,” Fashion and Textiles 9, 29 (2022), DOI 10.1186/s40691-022-00293-w. 

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational reference. Park, Kim & Sohn experimentally examined 3D simulation in an introductory apparel-patternmaking setting and reported positive potential for improving spatial-visualization ability; this remains one of the clearest load-bearing empirical precedents for treating virtual garment simulation as an instructional aid rather than merely production software. Juyeon Park, Dong-Eun Kim & MyungHee Sohn, “3D simulation technology as an effective instructional tool for enhancing spatial visualization skills in apparel design,” International Journal of Technology and Design Education 21(4) (2011), 505–517, DOI 10.1007/s10798-010-9127-3. 

[EMERGING] 2024–2026 practice has shifted from isolated software exposure toward semester-scale integration. Papachristou & Zolota-Tatsi describe third-year fashion students using 2D CAD and 3D clothing-design systems across a twelve-week design project, with exploration and experimentation embedded in the project rather than separated into a software-only exercise. Evridiki Papachristou & Nefeli Zolota-Tatsi, “Encouraging 3D Virtual Design in Fashion Education: Best Practices” (2024), DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-48933-4_43. 

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology — durable: spatial ability, spatial visualization, mental rotation, 2D-to-3D transformation, and apparel-specific spatial visualization are more precise research terms than the broad phrase “understanding volume.” Moritz & Youn explicitly distinguish spatial visualization and mental imagery and locate both throughout sketching, draping, sewing, pattern transformation, and fit evaluation. 

[ESTABLISHED] Open problem identified by the field. Moritz & Youn state that very few studies had examined 3D prototyping as an instructional tool in apparel education and call for multi-institutional studies with larger samples; their own sample was restricted to one university. 

[EMERGING] Critique. The evidence does not support equating “students can operate a 3D package” with “students understand form.” The apparel literature increasingly distinguishes observable tool use from domain-specific spatial cognition, with assessment instruments such as ASVT-V attempting to measure the latter directly. 

## 2. Teaching volume and spatial form with digital tools in fashion schools

[EMERGING] State of the art — hybrid analogue/digital sequencing is visible in current curricula. A 2025 IFFTI case study describes a curriculum in which first-year fashion students learn manual pattern-cutting fundamentals before digital pattern cutting with CLO3D is introduced, after which analogue and digital skills continue in parallel through later projects. The published curriculum diagram explicitly integrates manual and digital pattern skills rather than replacing one with the other. Hardaker et al., “Towards digital transformation of fashion and textiles education: a strategic pedagogy,” IFFTI Annual Proceedings, Vol. 4 (2025). 

[EMERGING] Current teaching environments remain studio-like even when digital. The same IFFTI case reports CLO3D instruction in an in-person CAD laboratory with approximately twenty students, explicitly describing the arrangement as analogous to a studio setting. 

[EMERGING] Project-based exploration rather than menu-by-menu training appears in recent fashion-specific literature. Papachristou & Zolota-Tatsi organised 3D virtual-prototyping learning around fashion-design tasks and a semester design project, encouraging students to experiment with available tools and connect them to existing skills. 

[EMERGING] Named active initiative: the Erasmus+-funded ME-YOU-US collaboration between Kolding School of Design, University of Ljubljana and Willem de Kooning Academy ran from 2022–2025 specifically to build digital 3D competencies in fashion education. Its nine teaching modules include virtual prototyping, body/environment scanning, AI, VR, AR and XR and were tested with students and educators. 

[EMERGING] Named research/design labs: at Kolding School of Design, ME-YOU-US is affiliated with the LAB for Sustainability and Design and LAB for Social Design; its explicit themes include digital 3D competencies, sustainability, inclusion and universal design. 

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology — durable: virtual prototyping, 3D apparel simulation, digital pattern cutting, draping, and spatial visualization are better indexed academic terms than “digital volume.” 

[EMERGING] Critique. Recent curriculum cases still often retain conventional pedagogical structures inside new software environments: the 2025 IFFTI case explicitly says its CLO3D pedagogy emulates traditional teaching through digitised 2D pattern blocks and a virtual mannequin. Digitalisation therefore does not by itself demonstrate a distinct pedagogy of three-dimensional form. 

## 3. Digital sculpture, modelling, and fashion product-form literacy

[ESTABLISHED] Fashion-specific 3D scholarship is garment-centric rather than general polygonal-sculpture-centric. The dominant educational objects are pattern pieces, drape, fit, silhouette, avatars/body forms and garment simulations; Moritz & Youn frame spatial learning around translating sketches and patterns into garments rather than generic mesh construction. 

[EMERGING] Recent cross-disciplinary work broadens “form” beyond virtual cloth. El-Mahdy’s practice-based fashion/architecture workshop used modelling, form-finding, assembly, folding and fabrication to teach self-supporting garment geometry, explicitly treating space, mass, structure and geometry as shared concerns between architecture and fashion. Deena El-Mahdy, “Experimental Structural Model: From Manual Paper Garment to Fabrication as an Architectural Practice-Based Approach for Fashion Design Education,” Nexus Network Journal (2023). 

[EMERGING] The frontier includes immersive 3D sketching rather than only desktop modelling. Recent product-design research comparing hand sketching and VR sketching reports different cognitive emphases: hand sketching supported broader ideational variation, whereas VR sketching promoted more intensive exploration of structural aspects. This is design-education evidence, not fashion-specific evidence, so transfer should be treated as provisional. Y. Sim et al., “The impact of immersion on idea generation in product design…” International Journal of Technology and Design Education (2026). 

[UNVERIFIED] Fashion-specific digital-sculpture pedagogy blank. I did not locate a 2024–2026 peer-reviewed HE fashion study that establishes a curriculum for generic polygonal modelling, subdivision modelling, sculpting, topology, or Blender-style hard/soft-surface construction as a foundational fashion-form pedagogy independent of garment simulation.

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology — durable: form-finding, structural modelling, spatial visualization, physical prototyping, and material thinking map better onto established design-education discourse than “digital sculpture literacy” when the intended learning outcome is understanding form rather than software technique. El-Mahdy explicitly frames making and model fabrication as means of reasoning about structure and geometry. 

[EMERGING] Critique. El-Mahdy deliberately avoids relying on digital visualisation in the early stages of the workshop, arguing for material trial-and-error and physical sensing of form. This is a direct counterexample to the assumption that increasingly sophisticated 3D software necessarily produces stronger understanding of volume. 

## 4. CLO3D / virtual garment form education in higher education

[ESTABLISHED] State of the art. CLO3D-class systems are being used to connect 2D pattern construction, virtual sewing, material behaviour, garment visualisation and iteration in higher-education fashion curricula. Papachristou & Zolota-Tatsi situate 3D clothing design alongside 2D CAD; Hardaker’s 2025 case integrates CLO3D across pattern-cutting education. 

[EMERGING] Structured digital learning resources are now appearing. Sholikhah, Widowati & Atika developed a CLO3D e-module for digital fashion patternmaking incorporating images, video, hyperlinks and instructional video access; expert reviews rated the resulting resource highly, although this was a development/evaluation study rather than a controlled learning-outcomes trial. “Development of 3D Virtual Fashion Design E-module Using CLO3D Software to Improve 21st Century Skills in Fashion Design Education” (2025), DOI 10.2991/978-2-38476-360-3_72. 
Atlantis Press

[ESTABLISHED] Canonical antecedent. McQuillan demonstrated CLO-based 3D design as a means of supporting zero-waste fashion practice, linking virtual form construction to pattern logic rather than treating 3D solely as visualisation. Holly McQuillan, “Digital 3D design as a tool for augmenting zero-waste fashion design practice,” International Journal of Fashion Design, Technology and Education 13(1) (2020), DOI 10.1080/17543266.2020.1737248. 
Taylor & Francis Online

[EMERGING] Current institutional projects increasingly connect CLO-class tools to sustainability and inclusion. ME-YOU-US used virtual prototyping as one of three major phases and reports experimentation with silhouette and material before physical manufacture; the initiative deliberately positioned digital tooling within sustainability, inclusion and diversity rather than employability alone. 

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology — durable: 3D virtual prototyping, virtual garment simulation, digital patternmaking, virtual sampling, cloth simulation, and digital garment prototyping. “CLO education” is vendor-specific and therefore a less durable descriptor of the pedagogical domain. 

[EMERGING] Open problem. Recent work still calls for more robust understanding of how 3D tools affect learning rather than merely adoption or perceived usefulness. Moritz & Youn explicitly identify the small educational evidence base, while recent CLO teaching studies remain frequently case-based or developmental. 

[EMERGING] Critique. Vendor-interface mastery can dominate curricular descriptions: UAL’s current introductory CLO3D short course, for example, explicitly uses step-by-step software demonstrations. This is credible practitioner pedagogy but is not evidence that demonstration-led instruction produces transferable three-dimensional reasoning. 

## 5. Transferring drawing literacy to three-dimensional fashion form

[ESTABLISHED] The conceptual bridge is already explicit in apparel education. Moritz & Youn describe fashion students imagining garment drape before sketching, translating sketches into flat patterns or draped forms, and then evaluating a completed 3D garment for fit; 2D↔3D conversion is therefore not a peripheral digital skill but a recurring cognitive operation across the design process. 

[ESTABLISHED] Foundational design-education terminology: visual mental imagery, externalisation, perspective sketching, spatial visualization and mental rotation provide a stronger conceptual vocabulary than “drawing-to-3D transfer.” Design-education literature treats sketching both as visual cognition and as learned expertise. 

[ESTABLISHED] Orthographic/perspective literacy belongs to a broader design-education evidence base. Research in design and technology education has examined perspective-sketching training, spatial conceptualisation, mental rotation and spatial-relation skills as predictors or supports for three-dimensional design performance; this evidence is adjacent, not fashion-specific. 

[EMERGING] Current immersive research adds 3D sketching as a distinct representational mode. The 2026 hand-sketching/VR-sketching comparison suggests that changing representational medium alters the kinds of cognitive transformations designers perform, rather than merely changing the interface through which the same process occurs. Transfer to fashion remains untested. 

[UNVERIFIED] Still-life → fashion-volume pipeline blank. I found no robust 2024–2026 HE fashion study testing a pedagogical sequence from observational still-life drawing, through light-on-form studies, into digital garment or fashion-object modelling.

[UNVERIFIED] Orthographic versus perspective in fashion-specific digital-form pedagogy blank. The concepts are foundational in drawing/design education, but I found no recent fashion-specific controlled study establishing how explicit orthographic/perspectival instruction affects subsequent CLO/Blender/garment-form competence.

[EMERGING] Critique. Recent 3D curricula can skip the representational bridge entirely and introduce students directly to software. The continued importance placed on spatial ability by apparel-specific studies suggests that interface fluency should not be assumed to substitute for internal spatial representation. 

## 6. Assessing understanding of volume without demo-driven tool training

[EMERGING] The clearest recent advance is assessment of transformation competence rather than interface recall. Galada & Baytar developed a simulation-based instrument in which learners interpret a 3D garment-fit problem and select the appropriate modification to the underlying 2D pattern. The instrument is based on an Extended Skill Cycle Model and was evaluated for reliability, validity, item difficulty and discrimination. Aditi Galada & Fatma Baytar, “Developing an instrument with simulations to measure 3D to 2D fit correction skills in fashion design,” Fashion and Textiles (2025), DOI 10.1186/s40691-025-00417-y. 

[ESTABLISHED] ASVT/ASVT-V constitutes an earlier assessment lineage. Instead of asking whether students know a CAD command, these instruments test apparel-specific spatial transformation and its relation to broader spatial ability. Moritz & Youn’s ASVT-V study explicitly positions domain-specific spatial cognition as measurable independently of students’ self-perceived performance. 

[ESTABLISHED] Field-identified curricular problem: Galada & Baytar argue that garment fitting receives insufficient explicit attention and that students are often expected to acquire fit-correction intuition implicitly; their study responds by making 3D-to-2D diagnostic reasoning measurable. 

[EMERGING] Sharper terminology — durable: domain-specific spatial ability, fit diagnosis, pattern–garment relationship, 3D-to-2D fit correction, and performance assessment are more precise than “volume understanding.” 

[UNVERIFIED] Process-evidence assessment blank. I did not find a fashion-specific 2024–2026 validated rubric that assesses construction of volume through a documented sequence of sketches, modelling decisions, revisions and critique while deliberately suppressing software-command performance as a grading criterion.

[EMERGING] Critique. Current assessment research remains narrow: the strongest instruments concern spatial transformation and garment fit, not broader sculptural issues such as mass, negative space, proportion, light on form, topology or surface continuity. That boundary follows directly from what ASVT-V and the Galada–Baytar instrument actually measure. 

## 7. AI-assisted 3D fashion-form generation and human judgment in studio

[EMERGING] State of the art — fashion education is moving from “should AI be used?” toward process-stage integration. An & Park’s 2026 instructor study maps AI tools onto problem definition, ideation/refinement, prototype development and evaluation and explicitly frames useful integration in terms of learner autonomy and human–AI collaboration. Hyosun An & Minjung Park, “AI tools and fashion design education: instructor perspectives on student challenges and design process tool support,” Fashion and Textiles 13, 3 (2026), DOI 10.1186/s40691-025-00452-9. 

[EMERGING] Fashion-specific GenAI/3D research now exists but is still early. Drakou, Papachristou, Tarnanidis & Bilalis explicitly investigate AI-driven 3D design in fashion education and discuss learning, creativity and sustainability. “Transforming Fashion Education: The Impact of AI-Driven 3D Design on Learning, Creativity, and Sustainability,” ACM proceedings (2024), DOI 10.1145/3688671.3688794. 
ACM Digital Library

[EMERGING] 3D generation from sketch input is technically advancing faster than pedagogical validation. Chen et al. presented personalised 3D clothing generation from a single sketch, while Zang et al.’s 2025 From Air to Wear system generates digital garments from free-hand immersive 3D sketches. These are HCI/graphics systems contributions, not validated fashion-HE teaching frameworks. 
ACM Digital Library

[EMERGING] The clearest pedagogical consensus is preservation of human judgment rather than autonomous generation. An & Park explicitly call for a balance between AI and tactile/manual practice and identify learner autonomy, communication, feedback adaptation and evaluation as pedagogically significant. 

[EMERGING] Sharper terminology — frontier: AIED in fashion design, human–AI collaboration, AI-supported design process, generative 3D, sketch-conditioned 3D generation, and co-creative systems. 

[ESTABLISHED] Open problems explicitly stated by current researchers. An & Park call for classroom-based empirical evaluation of specific tools, studies across different learner-expertise levels, investigation from students’ perspectives and validation across more diverse cultural/institutional contexts. 

[EMERGING] Critique. Current fashion-AI pedagogy is vulnerable to technological determinism: An & Park note that prior studies have concentrated more on technical/creative capability than instructional mechanisms, while contemporary arts education is simultaneously contesting questions of authorship, labour, IP and the place of generative systems in studio education. 

## 8. Still-life to volume pipelines in digital fashion curricula

[UNVERIFIED] Direct evidence blank. I found no identifiable 2024–2026 research programme in higher-education fashion that formally studies a sequence of observational still-life → tonal/light-on-form study → simplified primitives → digital modelling → garment/fashion object as a pedagogical pipeline.

[ESTABLISHED] The closest established fashion analogue is sketch → pattern → 3D garment → fit evaluation. Moritz & Youn describe precisely this recurring transformation chain and treat spatial visualization as the cognitive capacity connecting its stages. 

[EMERGING] A second analogue is model/form-finding → assembly → fabrication. El-Mahdy’s architecture–fashion workshop replaces initial two-dimensional drawing with direct structural model-making and uses folding, geometry and physical fabrication to reason about fashion form. 

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology — durable: observational drawing, form study, form-finding, spatial visualization, model-making, draping and prototype development are independently established concepts; the phrase “still-life to volume pipeline” does not appear to be an established fashion-education term in the sources located. 

[UNVERIFIED] Light-on-form pedagogy blank. No verifiable recent fashion-HE source located in this search isolates lighting, cast shadow, terminator, occlusion, or tonal modelling as explicit prerequisite knowledge for subsequent digital 3D-fashion construction.

[UNVERIFIED] Assessment blank. No controlled fashion-education comparison was located testing whether observational drawing or physical still-life construction improves subsequent 3D virtual-prototyping performance.

[EMERGING] Critique. Existing digital-fashion literature tends to operationalise “3D competence” through garment simulation, patterns, fit or digital-production workflows. Consequently, importing a foundational-art still-life pedagogy into fashion may be defensible conceptually, but the direct empirical bridge is presently missing. 

## 9. Accessibility of 3D fashion learning materials

[EMERGING] This is one of the clearest underdeveloped areas. The strongest current fashion-specific initiative I found is ME-YOU-US, whose explicit remit is digital 3D competencies for sustainability, inclusion and diversity; its modules cover virtual prototyping, scanning and immersive/emerging tools and were developed across three European HEIs. 

[EMERGING] Universal Design is entering digital-fashion pedagogy primarily through project work rather than accessibility-evaluation literature. Kolding lists Universal Design as a theme attached to ME-YOU-US, and the project describes body scanning and co-design with diverse users as mechanisms for broadening assumptions about bodies and contexts. 

[EMERGING] A 2025 LearnXDesign contribution explicitly combines Universal Design thinking, 3D digital prototyping and body scanning with design students. This is relevant evidence for inclusive digital-prototyping education, although it is not equivalent to demonstrating accessibility of CLO/Blender interfaces or 3D instructional media for disabled learners. R. Herriott, “The Application Of Universal Design & Digital Tools In …,” LearnXDesign 2025. 

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology — durable: Universal Design for Learning (UDL) concerns access to learning itself, whereas Universal Design, inclusive design, co-design, body diversity and adaptive fashion concern the objects/systems students design. Conflating these is methodologically risky. CAST’s current UDL framework addresses multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression. 

[UNVERIFIED] Critical blank — accessible 3D software instruction. I located no fashion-specific HE study in 2024–2026 evaluating keyboard-only navigation, screen-reader access, nonvisual 3D representation, tactile substitutes, captions/audio description for 3D demonstrations, or accessible assessment inside CLO3D/Marvelous Designer/Blender courses.

[UNVERIFIED] Critical blank — visual impairment. Research on accessible 3D/tactile representation exists outside fashion—for example, work on tactile/3D-printed spatial representations—but direct transfer into fashion-form education has not been demonstrated in the located literature. 

[EMERGING] Critique. “Inclusive digital fashion” currently often means designing for diverse bodies or involving diverse users, not making the learning environment itself accessible. The distinction is visible in ME-YOU-US, where inclusion is strongly represented at the design-content level but evidence for disability-accessible operation of the 3D learning stack is not reported on the project pages. 

## 10. Constructive solid geometry and mesh literacy for fashion students

[UNVERIFIED] Direct fashion-pedagogy blank. I found no 2024–2026 peer-reviewed higher-education fashion study establishing mesh topology, vertices/edges/faces, normals, manifold geometry, subdivision, Boolean/CSG operations, UVs or retopology as a validated foundational curriculum for fashion students.

[ESTABLISHED] The dominant fashion-specific technical model is different: garment systems generally represent form through 2D pattern pieces, sewing relationships, avatars and simulated textile behaviour. Consequently, the conceptual substrate foregrounded by apparel pedagogy is pattern↔surface↔body transformation rather than conventional solid-modelling primitives. 

[EMERGING] A structural-geometry route does exist at the margins. El-Mahdy’s fashion/architecture work introduces form-finding, self-supporting geometry, folding and fabrication as ways to reason structurally about garments; the study itself identifies computational treatment of forces and structure as future work. 

[ESTABLISHED] Sharper terminology — durable in generic 3D/graphics but not yet established as fashion pedagogy: polygonal modelling, mesh topology, surface modelling, solid modelling, constructive solid geometry, parametric modelling, subdivision surfaces, digital sculpting. Their technical definitions are durable, but their curricular status in fashion education is presently weakly evidenced.

[EMERGING] Fashion-specific adjacent terminology is stronger: cloth simulation, digital patternmaking, virtual garment prototyping, digital draping, avatar-based fitting, digital twins of garments and 3D body scanning. These are the terms under which current fashion research is actually clustering. 

[UNVERIFIED] Transfer boundary to Blender/graphics education. No evidence located justifies assuming that a mesh/CSG sequence developed for computer graphics, game art, industrial design or WebGL automatically transfers to fashion-form learning. The fashion literature instead repeatedly emphasises cloth behaviour, pattern relationships, bodies and fit. 

[EMERGING] Critique. Requiring generic mesh expertise may conflate two distinct professional ontologies of 3D form: surface/solid modelling and garment construction/simulation. Current research does not yet establish which mesh concepts fashion students need for durable conceptual literacy versus which merely reflect the requirements of particular content-creation pipelines. The absence of fashion-specific studies here should be retained as a research blank rather than filled from graphics-engineering pedagogy.
