Research landscape map — Fashion student digital portfolio web UX: pedagogy, accessibility, and professional outcomes

Pass 1 · field map · evidence window prioritised: 2024–16 August 2026

Evidence boundary. Fashion education has an active portfolio culture and a rapidly growing digital-fashion/GenAI literature, but the intersection fashion student × independently designed web portfolio × UX/accessibility × measured hiring outcome remains thin. Current fashion-school guidance strongly supports process, narrative, selection and professional presentation; adjacent ePortfolio, art/design education, accessibility and design-employer research supplies stronger empirical foundations. I therefore keep those evidence layers distinct rather than treating adjacent evidence as fashion-specific evidence. 

## 1. Fashion student portfolio web design in higher education
State of the art · 2024–2026

[ESTABLISHED] Contemporary fashion-education portfolio practice still treats the portfolio as a curated narrative of creative development rather than a gallery of polished outcomes alone. UCA's 2025 fashion-portfolio guidance explicitly says tutors seek evidence of how applicants think, create and explore ideas, while UAL's 2025 Fashion Folio material describes the fashion portfolio as simultaneously storytelling, visual résumé and evidence usable for education, employment or personal development. These are institutional/practice sources rather than controlled pedagogical studies, but they are unusually direct evidence of current fashion-school expectations. University for the Creative Arts, How to make a perfect fashion portfolio for university (2025); University of the Arts London, How to create a fashion portfolio for university (2025). 
University for the Creative Arts - UCA

[EMERGING] A particularly relevant adjacent 2025 study treats self-coded websites themselves as authentic portfolio projects: students learned foundational design together with HTML, CSS and JavaScript and produced self-coded digital portfolios rather than relying solely on portfolio builders. The study is not fashion-specific, so it establishes feasibility and pedagogical precedent for creative/professional portfolio education rather than evidence of effectiveness in fashion. Garcia, M. B., Self-Coded Digital Portfolios as an Authentic Project-Based Learning Approach (2025), Education Sciences 15(9), 1150, DOI 10.3390/educsci15091150. 

[ESTABLISHED] Current fashion programmes continue to use institutionally mediated digital portfolios as well as open-web portfolios. UAL, for example, currently asks applicants to some London College of Fashion programmes to submit digital portfolios through PebblePad; Regent's publishes web-accessible graduate portfolios; FIT publishes annual online student-work samples. This means “digital fashion portfolio” should not be equated with “student-coded personal website”: the field includes LMS/ePortfolio systems, institutional showcases, PDF/page-based submissions and independent sites. 
Regent's University London

[EMERGING] A 2026 fashion-specific paper reports communication problems in fashion portfolio presentation and argues for structured communication and focused pedagogical attention. This is closer to the target field than most current research, although the available source does not establish web-interface characteristics as the causal variable. Anuar et al., Communication Challenges in Fashion Portfolio Presentation (2026), Environment-Behaviour Proceedings Journal. 
ebpj.e-iph.co.uk

Foundational / canonical

[ESTABLISHED] The durable ePortfolio distinction is between developmental/learning, assessment, and showcase/professional functions. Barrett's REFLECT work made the tension between “assessment for learning” and “assessment of learning” foundational; Cambridge subsequently situated ePortfolios within lifelong learning, identity and assessment. Helen C. Barrett, Researching Electronic Portfolios and Learner Engagement: The REFLECT Initiative (2007), Journal of Adolescent & Adult Literacy 50(6), 436–449; Darren Cambridge, Eportfolios for Lifelong Learning and Assessment (2010), Jossey-Bass. 

[ESTABLISHED] Farrell's historical review is useful for terminology because it traces portfolios from physical artefact containers into instruments for documenting growth, reflective practice, employability and professional certification, rather than treating “digital portfolio” as a recent web-design genre. O. Farrell, From Portafoglio to Eportfolio: The Evolution of Portfolio in Higher Education (2020), Journal of Interactive Media in Education, DOI 10.5334/jime.574. 
JIME

Open problems / blanks

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No verifiable 2024–2026 comparative study was found testing different web architectures or UX patterns specifically in undergraduate fashion portfolios—for example, custom HTML/CSS versus Webflow/Squarespace/Adobe Portfolio versus institutional ePortfolio systems—with learning or employment outcomes as dependent variables. Current fashion sources describe portfolio content and presentation, while the self-coded-site evidence comes from an adjacent discipline. 

Adjacent / sharper terminology

[ESTABLISHED] “ePortfolio” / “electronic portfolio” is the durable scholarly term when the object serves learning, assessment, reflection or competency evidence; “showcase portfolio”, “professional portfolio” and “career portfolio” sharpen its outward-facing employment function. These are more precise research terms than the generic “portfolio website.” 
JIME

[EMERGING] “Self-coded digital portfolio” is a useful frontier term for work where building the web artefact is itself part of the assessed competency rather than merely the container for other work. Garcia (2025), DOI 10.3390/educsci15091150. 

Critiques / controversies

[ESTABLISHED] Portfolio research repeatedly warns against collapsing its reflective/developmental role into a high-stakes showcase or assessment instrument: what optimises a portfolio for external presentation is not necessarily what best documents uncertainty, iteration and learning. Barrett (2007); Cambridge (2010); Yang, Wang & Lim, E-portfolios as Digital Assessment Tools in Higher Education (2023).

## 2. Digital fashion portfolio UX, accessibility and responsive design
State of the art · 2024–2026

[ESTABLISHED] The normative web-accessibility baseline is WCAG 2.2, which became a W3C Recommendation in October 2023 and remains the relevant current W3C specification; it adds criteria particularly relevant to keyboard/focus interaction, dragging, target size and accessible authentication. W3C, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 (Recommendation; current publication retrieved 2026). 

[ESTABLISHED] For portfolio pedagogy specifically, the AAEEBL Digital Ethics framework treats accessibility as a property of both the platform used by creators and the content experienced by audiences, and explicitly argues that students and educators need accessible-content-creation knowledge. AAEEBL, Digital Ethics Principles in ePortfolios: Accessibility; Cicchino et al., Digital Ethics in ePortfolios: Developing Principles, Strategies, and Scenarios (2021), International Journal of ePortfolio 11(1), 67–82. 
AAEEBL

[ESTABLISHED] The practical accessibility requirements most directly relevant to image-heavy fashion portfolios include meaningful text alternatives, semantic structure, keyboard operability, perceivable focus, sufficient contrast and accessible multimedia; these follow directly from WCAG rather than from fashion-specific portfolio research. W3C, WCAG 2.2. 

[EMERGING] Accessibility remains an implementation problem rather than a solved standards problem: a 2025 European higher-education study found substantial differences between institutions in web accessibility and disability-support provision. This is institutional-site evidence, not fashion-portfolio evidence, but it cautions against assuming that higher-education digital environments automatically instantiate WCAG correctly. Krejtz et al., Higher education accessibility information in practice (2025), Universal Access in the Information Society. 

Foundational / canonical

[ESTABLISHED] The durable conceptual vocabulary is POUR — Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust — implemented through WCAG success criteria rather than aesthetic heuristics alone. W3C, WCAG 2.2. 

[ESTABLISHED] Accessible ePortfolio practice is also an ethical and pedagogical issue, not merely automated conformance checking. AAEEBL's Digital Ethics work explicitly combines accessibility, technology/usability, privacy, data responsibility and learner agency. 
AAEEBL

Open problems / blanks

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No fashion-specific study was found measuring WCAG conformance, assistive-technology usability or disabled-user experience across fashion-student portfolio websites. The strongest evidence located is WCAG itself, general ePortfolio accessibility work and wider HE web-accessibility research. 
AAEEBL

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No verified fashion-portfolio experiment was found comparing responsive/mobile portfolio UX with desktop-first portfolios on reviewer comprehension, assessment marks or recruitment outcomes.

Adjacent / sharper terminology

[ESTABLISHED] “Web accessibility”, “digital accessibility”, “accessible content creation” and “inclusive design” are durable terms. “Responsive portfolio design” is technically valid but describes presentation behaviour rather than the broader accessibility construct codified by WCAG. 

Critiques / controversies

[ESTABLISHED] Automated accessibility evaluation is necessarily incomplete: WCAG contains testable criteria, but accessibility practice also requires human interpretation and user testing; the persistence of widespread inaccessible sites despite mature standards is one reason current accessibility research continues to investigate organisational skill, incentives and implementation barriers. Parthasarathy et al., Skill, Will, or Both? Understanding Digital Inaccessibility from Accessibility Professionals' Viewpoint (2025). 

## 3. Portfolio as process evidence, reflection and authorship
State of the art · 2024–2026

[ESTABLISHED] Recent ePortfolio work continues to regard portfolios as capable of tracking learning processes as well as final artefacts. Slade, Zurhellen & Conefrey explicitly identify this dual function in their 2025 report on AAEEBL's Digital Ethics Task Force. Sustaining ethical ePortfolio practices: Insights from AAEEBL's Task Force Year 4 (2025), Journal of University Teaching and Learning Practice 22(3), DOI 10.53761/41wbmq76. 
Open Access Publishing Association

[ESTABLISHED] Fashion-school guidance independently converges on the same process/product distinction: UCA asks students to show how they think and develop ideas, and UAL describes portfolios through narrative, development and selection rather than finished garment imagery alone. 
University for the Creative Arts - UCA

[EMERGING] Self-coded digital portfolio research adds a second evidentiary layer: the making of the site can itself become process evidence, exposing design and implementation decisions that disappear when students only populate a pre-existing template. Garcia (2025), DOI 10.3390/educsci15091150. The study is not fashion-specific. 

Foundational / canonical

[ESTABLISHED] Portfolio pedagogy's canonical load-bearing idea is that reflection and selection transform an archive of artefacts into evidence of learning. Barrett (2007) explicitly connects electronic portfolios with learner engagement, reflection and the competing logics of formative learning and summative assessment. 

[ESTABLISHED] Creative-arts assessment literature likewise supports keeping creative process and finished product analytically visible together. Watson, Assessing creative process and product in higher education (2014), reports an assessment design deliberately addressing reflective process alongside completed artwork. 
Open Journal Systems

Open problems / blanks

[EMERGING] AAEEBL identifies ongoing research needs around the labour involved in producing and maintaining ePortfolios, including labour visibility and ethical practice. Slade, Zurhellen & Conefrey (2025), DOI 10.53761/41wbmq76. 
Open Access Publishing Association

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No fashion-specific study was located testing which kinds of digital provenance—drafts, sketchbook scans, version histories, source files, process commentary, Git history, AI-use logs—best allow assessors or employers to distinguish authorship from presentation polish.

Adjacent / sharper terminology

[ESTABLISHED] Useful durable terms are “process portfolio”, “developmental portfolio”, “reflective portfolio”, “learning portfolio” and “evidence of learning.” They distinguish the evidentiary role from the employment-facing “showcase portfolio.” 
JIME

[EMERGING] “Digital provenance” and “visibility of labour” are useful adjacent terms for the authorship problem, although neither is yet a settled fashion-portfolio pedagogy construct. AAEEBL's 2025 study explicitly uses “visibility of labour” as a research keyword. 
Open Access Publishing Association

Critiques / controversies

[ESTABLISHED] A portfolio designed principally for summative judgement can encourage selective self-presentation that suppresses failed experiments and ambiguity, creating tension with the portfolio's reflective-learning function. That tension is longstanding in Barrett/Cambridge ePortfolio scholarship rather than a GenAI-era discovery. 

## 4. Hiring outcomes linked to fashion portfolio web characteristics
State of the art · 2024–2026

[ESTABLISHED] Creative-industry careers guidance in 2026 still treats portfolios as important hiring evidence because they expose work, process, style, adaptability and professional presentation beyond what a CV communicates. Leeds Arts University careers specialists quoted by Prospects describe the creative portfolio as effectively a visual job application. Prospects / Leeds Arts University, Putting together a creative portfolio (2026). 
Prospects

[ESTABLISHED] The strongest directly relevant employer study located remains older and adjacent rather than fashion-web-specific: 53 Australian graphic-design employers were surveyed about how graduate portfolios evidence professional capability. The authors frame the ability to construct and communicate through a portfolio as portfolio literacy. Wiley Online Library

This is genuinely fashion/apparel-specific, but it predates the present web-UX environment and therefore cannot establish contemporary site-design effects. Industry professionals' evaluation of apparel design student portfolios (apparel-design employer study). 
ResearchGate

## 5. Teaching HTML/CSS/layout through creative fashion portfolios
State of the art · 2024–2026

[EMERGING] The strongest recent empirical match is Garcia's 2025 self-coded portfolio study, in which HTML, CSS and JavaScript are taught alongside foundational design principles through project-based construction of students' own portfolio sites. It supplies a current pedagogical model for “portfolio as both content and web-development artefact”, but the participants are not fashion students. Garcia (2025), Education Sciences 15(9), 1150, DOI 10.3390/educsci15091150. 
MDPI

[EMERGING] Long & Peng's PortfolioMentor work addresses a neighbouring art/design problem: non-technical design students can struggle to convert portfolio concepts into code and interactive media, so the prototype uses a multimodal generative-AI coding companion to support interactive digital-art portfolio construction. Long & Peng, PortfolioMentor: Multimodal Generative AI Companion for Learning and Crafting Interactive Digital Art Portfolios (2023). 

[ESTABLISHED] Current fashion-school portfolio instructions, by contrast, generally focus on content capture, selection and page preparation rather than HTML/CSS competence. UAL's digital-portfolio guidance, for example, describes photographing/scanning physical work and constructing digital pages for upload. This confirms that a “fashion digital portfolio” is not currently synonymous with front-end-development education. 

Foundational / canonical

[ESTABLISHED] In web-design education, teaching HTML/CSS alongside information architecture, usability and responsive/mobile concerns predates the current portfolio-builder ecosystem. Jen Kramer, How To Teach Web Design To New Students In Higher Education (2013), Smashing Magazine, provides an early practitioner-education formulation of this integrated approach. It is pedagogical practice literature, not fashion research. 
Smashing Magazine

Open problems / blanks

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No peer-reviewed fashion-education study located for 2024–2026 compares learning outcomes from teaching fashion students semantic HTML/CSS versus teaching only portfolio-builder operation.

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No verified study was found testing whether coding one's own fashion portfolio improves fashion students' transferable digital literacy, accessibility literacy or employment outcomes beyond the portfolio artefact itself. Garcia (2025) makes the closest adjacent case but does not establish fashion-specific transfer. 

Adjacent / sharper terminology

[ESTABLISHED] “Self-coded digital portfolio”, “authentic project-based assessment” and “web-development literacy” are more precise than “teach students to make a website” when the coding process itself is curricular evidence. 

Critiques / controversies

[ESTABLISHED] There is a genuine curricular distinction between portfolio-making competence and front-end engineering competence: institutional fashion programmes routinely assess digital portfolios without requiring students to implement the underlying platform. Current UAL practice demonstrates this distinction directly.

## 7. Personal branding versus educational portfolio assessment
State of the art · 2024–2026

[ESTABLISHED] Contemporary ePortfolio scholarship continues to combine professional identity with learning and assessment, but does not treat those purposes as identical. Zainuddin et al.'s longitudinal 2026 study, for example, examines reflective learning, competency development, digital professionalism and professional identity across study and the first post-graduation period. Zainuddin et al., E-portfolios as authentic assessment in pre-service teacher education (2026), Education and Information Technologies, DOI 10.1007/s10639-025-13869-y. 

[ESTABLISHED] In fashion practice, institutional guidance describes the portfolio simultaneously as individual creative voice, evidence of thinking and a professional “visual résumé”. UAL's 2025 fashion portfolio guidance makes this overlap explicit. 

[ESTABLISHED] EPortfolio assessment research nevertheless requires attention to reliability, validity, authenticity and honesty, precisely because the artefact often serves both evaluative and self-presentational purposes. Yang, Wang & Lim, E-portfolios as Digital Assessment Tools in Higher Education (2023). 

Foundational / canonical

[ESTABLISHED] The core tension is Barrett's distinction between portfolio for learning and portfolio of learning: formative reflection and external judgement can coexist, but they place different incentives on selection, disclosure and polish. Barrett (2007). 

Open problems / blanks

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No fashion-specific experimental evidence was found showing how grading a public personal-brand portfolio changes students' willingness to expose unfinished work, failure, uncertainty or experimentation.

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No validated fashion-specific rubric was found that independently scores portfolio craft/content, reflective process, web usability/accessibility and self-branding without conflating them.

Adjacent / sharper terminology

[ESTABLISHED] “Professional identity”, “digital professionalism”, “showcase portfolio”, “assessment portfolio” and “developmental portfolio” are sharper and more durable than a single broad category such as “personal branding.” 

Critiques / controversies

[ESTABLISHED] Treating an assessed portfolio primarily as personal branding risks introducing construct-irrelevant presentation effects: the ePortfolio assessment literature explicitly treats validity, authenticity and reliability as unresolved design issues rather than assuming that polished self-presentation transparently measures learning.

## 9. Inclusive fashion portfolio design for diverse bodies and identities
State of the art · 2024–2026

[ESTABLISHED] At the web-interface level, inclusion has a stable technical foundation: portfolio sites should satisfy accessibility requirements for disabled audiences under WCAG 2.2 rather than assuming a primarily sighted, mouse-using viewer. W3C, WCAG 2.2. 

[ESTABLISHED] AAEEBL extends that principle to portfolio creators as well as viewers, arguing that ePortfolio platforms themselves should be usable by diverse creators and that accessibility knowledge should be taught rather than delegated entirely to the platform. 
AAEEBL

[EMERGING] Current fashion/media initiatives explicitly foreground access to professional visibility for historically underrepresented creative talent. CondéFuture/PhotoVogue, for example, combines mentorship, multimedia creative practice and presentation opportunities for students from underrepresented communities. This is an industry/education initiative, not evidence about web-interface design. 
Vogue

[EMERGING] PhotoVogue's 2025 virtual portfolio-review initiatives explicitly frame digital review access in terms of broader geographic and social inclusion. Again, this demonstrates current professional practice rather than a controlled outcome study. 
Vogue

Foundational / canonical

[ESTABLISHED] The durable distinction is between accessibility—removing disability-related barriers to perceiving and operating the site—and the broader questions of representation, identity and cultural inclusion in the portfolio's imagery and narrative. WCAG directly standardises the former; it does not constitute a theory of representational justice in fashion imagery. 

Open problems / blanks

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No 2024–2026 study was found evaluating fashion-student portfolio websites simultaneously for disability accessibility and representation of diverse body sizes, races, genders or identities.

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No verified evidence was found showing that inclusive representation in a student's fashion portfolio causes improved hiring outcomes; professional and ethical rationales exist, but a causal recruitment claim would currently exceed the evidence retrieved.

Adjacent / sharper terminology

[ESTABLISHED] For the interface layer, use “web accessibility” / “digital accessibility”; for pedagogical participation, “inclusive design” and “universal design” are broader; for bodies and cultural depiction, “representation” is analytically separate from technical accessibility. WCAG and AAEEBL support the first distinction. 

Critiques / controversies

[ESTABLISHED] A technically WCAG-conformant portfolio is not thereby culturally or representationally inclusive: WCAG is explicitly an accessibility standard for web content, so using conformance as a proxy for all forms of inclusion would exceed its stated scope. 

## 10. Evaluating web portfolios without reducing craft to template polish
State of the art · 2024–2026

[ESTABLISHED] Current ePortfolio assessment literature favours constructive alignment, scaffolding, learner ownership and contextualised assessment rather than treating surface polish as the sole quality criterion. Yang, Wang & Lim's review identifies learning needs/context, constructive alignment, task scaffolding and capacity-building as key implementation conditions. 

[EMERGING] Garcia's 2025 self-coded-portfolio model makes the underlying construction activity assessable: because students implement the portfolio using HTML/CSS/JavaScript, the educational object contains evidence of technical design and development, not only content placed into a predefined visual shell. Garcia (2025), DOI 10.3390/educsci15091150. 

[ESTABLISHED] Creative-assessment literature provides a durable precedent for splitting evaluation across process and product rather than allowing the final artefact to absorb the full mark. Watson (2014) examines exactly this problem in HE creative work; an SQA expressive-art portfolio model similarly separates process, skills and evaluation, demonstrating how institutional assessment can make process explicit. 
Open Journal Systems

[EMERGING] The arrival of GenAI makes this construct-separation more urgent because studio-design researchers report that AI can make the production process opaque to educators, weakening the evidentiary value of finished output alone. Park (2025), IASDR. 
Design Research Society Digital Library

Foundational / canonical

[ESTABLISHED] Portfolio assessment has always had to reconcile validity, reliability, authenticity and learner ownership. Those criteria remain more durable than any specific website-builder or visual trend. Yang, Wang & Lim (2023); Barrett (2007). 

[ESTABLISHED] The distinction between developmental, assessment and showcase portfolios is therefore directly relevant to rubric design: a showcase optimised for visual selection is not automatically sufficient evidence for assessing the learning process that produced it. Farrell (2020); Barrett (2007). 
JIME

Open problems / blanks

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] I found no validated fashion-specific web-portfolio rubric that separately measures (a) fashion/design craft, (b) research and process evidence, (c) authorship/attribution, (d) information architecture and usability, (e) accessibility/responsiveness, and (f) professional communication.

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No retrieved study establishes how much evaluative weight should be assigned to portfolio-site implementation when the student's disciplinary degree is fashion rather than interaction/web design.

[UNVERIFIED] [UNVERIFIED-GAP] No evidence was found that template polish should itself be treated as a proxy for professional competence; current research instead supports treating presentation as one evidentiary dimension among learning, reflection, authenticity and professional identity. 

Adjacent / sharper terminology

[ESTABLISHED] “Authentic assessment”, “constructive alignment”, “portfolio assessment”, “process evidence”, “assessment validity” and “portfolio literacy” are the durable academic terms around this problem. 

[EMERGING] Under GenAI, “process transparency”, “creative agency” and “authorship evidence” are increasingly useful frontier concepts because they specify what final-render assessment may fail to reveal. 
Design Research Society Digital Library

Critiques / controversies

[ESTABLISHED] A portfolio can become “polish theatre” if interface refinement is allowed to substitute for evidence of disciplinary thinking, but “polish theatre” itself is not a verified scholarly construct in the retrieved literature.
