# AI Usage Declaration

**Digital Creativity I–II · authorship and transparency guidelines**

- Table of Contents
  {:toc}

## Purpose

This declaration supports transparency about the use of artificial intelligence tools in digital fashion creation work. It does not prohibit AI; it requires clear documentation of what tools were used, how they were used, and what human decisions shaped the final work.

## Required declaration elements

Every submitted piece (portfolio entry, exercise, project, or exam response) must include:

- **Tools used:** name specific AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Midjourney, Claude, GitHub Copilot, Adobe Firefly, etc.) or state "No AI tools used"
- **Purpose of use:** describe what the AI was asked to do (e.g., "generate initial concepts," "assist code structure," "create reference textures," "suggest UX patterns")
- **Human contribution:** explain what you decided, modified, rejected, or added after AI interaction
- **Source material:** if the AI was trained on or prompted with specific references, name them

## Example declarations

### Example 1: No AI used

> **AI tools used:** None  
> **Human contribution:** All modelling, texturing, lighting, and rendering were done manually in the assigned software.

### Example 2: AI for concept ideation

> **AI tools used:** Midjourney v6  
> **Purpose of use:** Generated 15 moodboard images to explore colour palettes and silhouette variations for the avatar project.  
> **Human contribution:** I used the AI images as visual reference only, did not copy them directly, and modelled the final avatar in CLO 3D from scratch. The AI helped me consider colour combinations I had not initially explored.

### Example 3: AI for code assistance

> **AI tools used:** GitHub Copilot  
> **Purpose of use:** Suggested HTML structure and CSS grid layout for the portfolio website.  
> **Human contribution:** I reviewed every suggestion, kept only what matched my intended design, rewrote unclear parts, and customised the styling to match my visual language. The AI accelerated typing but did not design the user experience.

### Example 4: AI for texture generation

> **AI tools used:** Adobe Firefly  
> **Purpose of use:** Created base texture patterns for fabric simulation.  
> **Human contribution:** I used the AI-generated textures as starting points, manually adjusted colour and scale in Photoshop, and applied them within the 3D software with custom material settings. The final fabric appearance is a result of both AI generation and human material editing.

## Principles

1. **Transparency over prohibition:** Digital Creativity does not ban AI tools; it requires that their use be documented.
2. **Human authorship remains central:** AI can assist, but the creative decisions, technical choices, critical judgement, and final responsibility are yours.
3. **Context matters:** Using AI to generate an entire submission without human contribution is not acceptable practice. Using AI as one tool among many (like a reference library, a tutorial, or a technical forum) is acceptable when documented.
4. **Honesty about uncertainty:** If you are unsure whether a tool counts as AI, declare it. The emphasis is on clarity, not on technical classification.

## Digital-specific considerations

In Digital Creativity, AI tools may be used for:

- **Concept development:** moodboards, style exploration, reference generation
- **Technical assistance:** code suggestions, workflow optimisation, troubleshooting
- **Media enhancement:** texture generation, colour grading suggestions, retouching assistance
- **Language support:** translation, grammar checking, writing assistance (for Spanish/English content)

AI should not replace:

- **Core creative decisions:** what to make, why it matters, how it communicates
- **Technical understanding:** you must be able to explain and defend your technical choices
- **Critical judgement:** evaluating whether AI outputs are appropriate, ethical, and fit for purpose
- **Final responsibility:** you are accountable for the work you submit

## What this declaration does not replace

This AI declaration does not replace:

- **Academic integrity rules:** plagiarism, misrepresentation, or dishonesty remain violations regardless of tool use
- **Source citations:** traditional references (books, articles, websites, tutorials) must still be cited properly
- **Collaboration credit:** if you worked with others, name them and describe their contributions
- **Material permissions:** ensure you have rights to use any source material, whether human- or AI-generated
- **Accessibility standards:** AI-assisted code must still meet web accessibility requirements

## Questions

If you are uncertain about how to declare AI use in a specific situation, ask during class or office hours. The goal is to develop habits of transparent authorship that will serve you in professional digital fashion practice.

## Scholarly grounding

Research on AI in fashion design education supports transparency as essential for maintaining academic integrity and student authorship. Bartlett and Camba (2024) emphasize that AI should be a tool, not the creator, and recommend documenting AI use and decisions for legal and educational reasons. Embedding principles of transparency in AI use helps preserve fairness while fostering ethical and responsible use (Anwar et al. 2025). Ethical evaluation of AI-assisted work for cultural appropriateness, attribution, and data transparency aligns with UNESCO's call for responsible, human-centered AI use in education (Anwar et al. 2025). Maintaining student agency is crucial as AI shifts designers from sole authors to co-creators (Anwar et al. 2025).

The EU AI Act (2024) provides a regulatory framework that aligns with these educational principles, requiring transparency in AI models to ensure copyright compliance and mandating that deployers disclose when AI has been used in content creation (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2024). This legal framework recognizes both the development opportunities offered by generative models and the need for ethical integration of AI in creative domains.
