## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

- **Produce one short edited fashion video**, storyboard through export, at a professional craft level.
- **Apply consent-aware defaults** — no autoplay-with-sound, captions present, before publishing.
- **Write a process note** — storyboard decisions, cuts made, and why.
- **Name the genre anchor correctly** — what Díaz Soloaga and García Guerrero establish about fashion film, and what they do not establish about editing pedagogy.
- **Connect editing choices to invisible platform labour** — whose negotiation, circulation, and communication work sits behind the apparent immediacy of a fashion video online.

## Why this unit exists — a fashion edit is consent-aware before it is cut

**Claim:** a fashion edit is a sequence of *consent-aware* choices — what plays automatically, what needs a click, what has captions — before it is a sequence of cuts. This unit teaches that discipline alongside cutting and pacing craft, deliberately, rather than treating accessibility as an afterthought pass at the end.

Díaz Soloaga and García Guerrero describe fashion film as a communication format shaped by audiovisual advertising, cinema, music video, video art, and branded content. That supports the brief's purpose-and-audience question; it does **not** validate this unit's editing sequence, storyboard workflow, or accessibility defaults (Díaz Soloaga and García Guerrero 2016, 5).

**What this supports, and what it does not.** Díaz Soloaga and García Guerrero (2016) support naming fashion film as a branded communication genre descended from advertising, cinema, clips, and video art. **No reviewed source validates a fashion-video editing teaching sequence** for HE; tool docs ground operations only — label `[PLATFORM]`, never research.

**Practice anchor (field lens):** storyboard, shot purpose, continuity, rhythm, audio bridge, caption track, and consent-aware export as durable operation classes — separate from any one non-linear editor's UI or effect menu.

**Frontier signal (field lens):** AI auto-cut, generative B-roll, and upscaling may accelerate polish faster than pedagogical validation; treat any generated cut or caption as input to judgement, not proof that consent-aware defaults were applied.

## Critical perspective

A substantial share of the media work performed by independent designers remains invisible even though it is indispensable for circulation and recognition on Instagram and other digital environments — negotiation, garment circulation, networking, and communication labour sit behind the apparent immediacy of platform fashion imagery (Skjulstad 2025).

Fashion video packages desire, brand power, and selection through rhythm and omission. Who negotiates access to garments, who captions for whom, and whose labour disappears so the edit feels effortless are not technical afterthoughts — they are editorial and platform choices with distributive consequences.

**Deliverable:** one short edited fashion video (storyboard → footage selection → edit → caption/consent-aware export) with a one-paragraph **process note** — storyboard decisions, cuts made, and why.
