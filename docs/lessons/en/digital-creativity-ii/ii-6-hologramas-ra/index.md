## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

- **Write a medium-choice rationale** for an AR/hologram concept — why this medium, for this piece, versus a flatter alternative.
- **Name the cost** of the medium — hardware, access, novelty-fatigue — alongside its potential benefit.
- **Correctly bound the evidence** — what the reused II.3 finding does and does not establish for AR specifically.
- **Produce a staged concept** (storyboard or annotated mockup), not a full AR build, at this unit's hour allocation.

---

## Why this unit exists: a medium choice, deliberately made

**Claim:** hologram/AR presentation is a medium choice with its own cost — hardware, access, novelty-fatigue — not a default upgrade to reach for because it is available. This unit teaches AR/hologram as a **declared pilot**, the same register as this course's other frontier units.

### Declared gap — and an explicit citation-reuse boundary

The grounding matrix's row for this CONTENIDOS anchor is **NONE for AR pedagogy**. This unit reuses the same node cited in [II.3]({{ '/lessons/en/digital-creativity-ii/ii-3-experiencias-digitales/' | relative_url }}) — Kim (2023), on stimulus-type response comparison (screened vs. 360°/immersive) — **strictly as a stimulus-type parallel**: immersive presentation changes audience response *in general*, which is a reason to *consider* AR/hologram deliberately. **It is not independent AR/hologram evidence** — Kim's study did not test a hologram or AR device, and citing it as if it validated AR specifically would misrepresent the finding. State this boundary to students explicitly; do not let the citation imply more than it supports.

A prior harvest candidate (Hafliger, p.15) was found to be **bibliography only** — a reference list entry, not a supporting finding — and is **discarded**, not cited.

### The debate prompt

When is AR the honest choice for a fashion piece, versus a novelty add-on nobody asked for?
