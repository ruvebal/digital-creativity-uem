## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

- **Write a medium-choice rationale** for an AR/hologram concept — why this medium, for this piece, versus a flatter alternative.
- **Name the cost** of the medium — hardware, access, novelty-fatigue — alongside its potential benefit.
- **Correctly bound the evidence** — what the reused II.3 finding does and does not establish for AR specifically.
- **Produce a staged concept** (storyboard or annotated mockup), not a full AR build, at this unit's hour allocation.

## Why this unit exists — a medium choice, deliberately made

**Claim:** hologram/AR presentation is a medium choice with its own cost — hardware, access, novelty-fatigue — not a default upgrade to reach for because it is available. This unit teaches AR/hologram as a **declared pilot**, the same register as this course's other frontier units.

Kim's comparison of flat and 360° fashion-show presentations is used here only as a stimulus-type parallel: immersive staging can change audience response, but the study tested neither AR nor holograms and does not validate an AR teaching method (Kim 2023, 6).

A quantitative study of 855 Middle Eastern consumers reports positive associations between fashion AR, purchase decisions, customer engagement, and brand loyalty. It is useful here as a brief criterion: a proposal must say what interaction it offers, for which audience, and what response it expects; it is not evidence of AR pedagogy, holograms, or a generalisable classroom sequence (Sarkis et al. 2026, 2, 8).

**What this supports, and what it does not.** Kim (2023) and Sarkis et al. (2026) support audience/stimulus reasoning and brief criteria for fashion AR experiences. **No reviewed source validates AR/hologram teaching or presentation pedagogy** for HE; tool docs ground operations only — label `[PLATFORM]`, never research. A separate metaverse-focused reference in the field map does not meet this unit's student-facing evidence gate and is **not used here**.

**Practice anchor (field lens):** trigger, tracking, overlay, spatial relation, viewer action, and non-AR fallback as durable interaction-design operations — separate from any one AR SDK or hologram-display vendor demo.

**Frontier signal (field lens):** generative 3D assets and one-click AR filters may accelerate spectacle faster than pedagogical validation; treat any generated layer as input to judgement, not proof that access or privacy were considered.

## Critical perspective

Virtual fashion formats may expand certain forms of access but also generate friction and generational differences — a useful corrective to automatically equating virtualization with accessibility or improved experience (Farah, Ramadan, and Nassereddine 2025).

Immersive novelty can increase attention while shifting privacy, access, energy, and interpretive work to audiences. Metaverse and "always-on immersion" claims in adjacent industry discourse often outrun what any classroom coat can prove — equating an added layer with participation without naming device cost, data capture, or excluded viewers is a category error this unit refuses.

## B2 · Taller — talleres 1 h + investigaciones y proyectos 3 h (workplace-like, contextualised)

**Deliverable:** a staged AR/hologram **concept** for one piece — a storyboard or an annotated mockup is acceptable; a full AR build is **not** required at this hour allocation — with a one-paragraph rationale citing the medium-choice reasoning above.

**Definition of done:** concept artefact exists (storyboard/mockup); rationale present and correctly bounds the Kim citation; piece ID assigned; process folder kept.

**If you want this piece exhibited publicly** (vShowroom), attach the **unsigned** consent checklist below. Filling it in is not signing it. **VTON/try-on is explicitly out of scope** — this concept is a presentation medium, not a garment fit-check simulation.

### S1 — Exhibit this piece in vShowroom (unsigned draft — do not sign)

**Pieza / piece ID:** _________________
**Tipo:** still / avatar / look / video / other: _______

- [ ] I licence Universidad Europea / the vShowroom operators to **display** this piece on the showroom (web). I keep copyright.
- [ ] The licence is **revocable**. After withdrawal the public page comes down within a DPO/ops-defined delay (suggested 14 days). Cached copies may linger briefly.
- [ ] This is **not** a sale, a VTON/try-on right, or a right to train models on my work.

Nombre · Fecha · Firma — *(unsigned template; not a live consent instrument)*

### S2 — Attribution vs. anonymity (unsigned draft — do not sign)

Default: **attributed** (name as the student writes it).

- [ ] Show my name / chosen designer name: _________________
- [ ] Exhibit **without** my civil name (piece ID only)

S2 without S1 does nothing. S1 without S2 defaults to attributed.

## B3 · Resolución de problemas — 1 h · individual, decontextualised

1. **Diagnostic.** Given a piece description defaulting to AR "because it's impressive," identify what is missing from a medium-choice rationale and name it.
2. Write the one-paragraph medium-choice rationale, by hand, for a described piece (no AI assistance).
3. **No-AI, declared:** in two sentences, explain precisely why Kim is not independent AR evidence and why Sarkis does not prove AR pedagogy.
