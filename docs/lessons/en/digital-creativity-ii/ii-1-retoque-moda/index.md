## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

- **Perform advanced retouching** at a professional craft level — skin, drape, background, proportion edits, done deliberately.
- **Keep a before/after pair** for every piece, as a matter of practice, not an afterthought.
- **Write an honest disclosure line** naming what changed, every time — including when nothing changed.
- **Distinguish craft from harm** — where advanced retouching crosses into appearance distortion, and why that line matters for a fashion communicator.
- **Connect retouching choices to research on altered images** — digitally altered images can influence body satisfaction and beauty aspirations (MacCallum and Widdows 2018); self-photo editing is associated with body dissatisfaction in adjacent literature (McLean et al. 2015).

---

## Why this unit exists — retouching is a representation-ethics skill first

**Claim:** advanced retouching is a *representation-ethics* skill before it is a tool skill — every edit changes what a viewer believes about a body or a garment, whether or not the editor intended that effect. This unit teaches the tool craft and the disclosure discipline together, not the tool craft alone.

Digitally altered images can influence individuals' body satisfaction and beauty aspirations (MacCallum and Widdows 2018, 1). That finding supports teaching consequence analysis and visual literacy alongside formal disclosure — it does **not** validate which retouching exercises, sequence, or classroom workflow best teach advanced fashion retouching.

## Critical perspective

The dressed body is not a pre-social support: fashion theory understands it as a situated bodily practice, traversed by social norms of gender, appearance, and presentation (Entwistle 2015).

Digital fashion archives can reproduce white and Eurocentric historiographies, but they can also be designed explicitly to decentralise those narratives and make racialised and indigenous histories recoverable (Rodriguez Schon and Valle-Noronha 2025). Retouching defaults — skin smoothing, body proportion, reference libraries — are not neutral: they condition which bodies and garments read as "correct" before any disclosure line is written.

### The debate prompt

Put this to the group before touching a single image: **where is the line between "advanced retouching" (craft) and "appearance distortion" (harm)?** Should the studio require a disclosure line on every retouched fashion image submitted for this course, the same way an AI-use declaration is required elsewhere?

## B1 · Conceptual — magistral 2 h + seminarios 4 h + debates 2 h

Covered above: MacCallum/McLean framing, critical lens, debate, and placeholders. Cover the retouching techniques the studio uses (skin, drape/fit correction, background, proportion) as craft, then pair each technique with the disclosure question it raises: *if I do this, what does the viewer now believe that may not be true?* Run the debate prompt above before any hands-on work.

## B2 · Taller — talleres 3 h + investigaciones y proyectos 4 h (workplace-like, contextualised)

**Deliverable:** one retouched fashion image, with the **before/after pair kept** (both files, not just the final), plus a one-line disclosure of what was changed — skin, garment drape, background, proportions, or "no retouching applied."

**Definition of done:**
- Before/after pair saved together, same piece ID.
- Disclosure line present and specific (not a generic "edited in [tool]").
- Piece ID assigned; process folder kept even if not published.

**No S1/S2 in this unit.** Retouching (CONTENIDOS anchor 1) is not on the framework's vShowroom-eligible anchor list (`oficial-guia-framework.mdc` §2 names anchors 2·3·5·6 only) — this deliverable is not framed as an exhibitable showroom piece.

## B3 · Resolución de problemas — 2 h · individual, decontextualised

Not the retouched-image deliverable. Short problems isolating one judgement each:

1. **Diagnostic.** Given a retouched image with a visibly altered body proportion and no disclosure line, name exactly what is missing from an honest disclosure and write it.
2. Given a piece description — *"the editor smoothed skin texture, narrowed the waist by an unstated amount, and added no credit or disclosure"* — identify which specific change makes this cross from craft into undisclosed distortion.
3. **No-AI, declared:** without AI assistance, write a two-sentence disclosure for a described edit (background replacement + minor skin retouching, garment untouched).

Professor answer sketches are not published on this page — kept in the instructor's private notes.
