## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

- **Build volume by iterating between physical and digital passes**, not by committing to one representation and discarding the other.
- **Name what each representation revealed** that the other one didn't — the core evidence trail this unit asks for.
- **Recognise the limits of a single documented case** — this unit's own grounding is one module, one cohort, one tool, and says so.
- **Engage the sustainability caveat named by the unit's own source** — hybrid tools reduce sampling waste but do not, by themselves, disrupt fashion's overproduction logic.

## Why this unit exists: volume is built by iterating, not by picking a medium

**Claim:** volume is built by moving back and forth between physical and digital representations of the same piece, each pass correcting or extending the other — not by choosing analogue *or* digital and treating the choice as final.

This is not an abstract preference — it is a documented, theorised teaching module. "The Denim Project," a published 2026 case study, describes a hybrid physical/digital studio in which students worked a denim garment across both material and CLO3D form. The module is explicitly framed within a constructivist model of experiential learning (citing Biggs & Tang 2011; Kolb 1984): students iterated across analogue and digital workflows, which fostered technical proficiency *and* critical reflection — not efficiency alone (Coats 2026, 8 — Ahmes coat `6d1a7b81`, node `b3ded30f-381e-5425-80b1-cf125b52449f`).

### Declared gap — read this before generalising the method

**What is grounded above:** *that* a real, theorised hybrid analogue/digital teaching module for volume exists and how it was framed pedagogically. **What is NOT grounded:** that this approach generalises beyond one module, one cohort, and one specific tool (CLO3D). This is a single published case study — a strong existence proof, not a validated method across contexts. Do not extend it to other volume-building tools without new evidence, and do not present it as settled practice across fashion education.

### The tool's own caveat — carried forward honestly, not smoothed over

The same paper is explicit that its own tool has a limit: while CLO3D helped reduce muslin (sampling) waste through virtual prototyping, students in the study expressed scepticism about its *wider* sustainability impact — echoing Fletcher & Tham (2023) and Wetherell, Nicholson & James (2024), who argue digital tools alone cannot overcome fashion's systemic drivers of overproduction and rapid trend turnover (Coats 2026, 8, same node). This is a caveat about the *tool's broader industry effect*, not a critique of the *pedagogy* — the two must not be blurred. It grounds a classroom discussion prompt (see B1), not a claim about teaching effectiveness.

## B1 · Conceptual — magistral 1 h + seminarios 2 h + debates 1 h

### The debate prompt

Use Coats's own caveat directly, unaltered: **if a hybrid tool like CLO3D reduces physical sampling waste in the studio, does that make fashion practice more sustainable overall, or does it risk normalising faster, more disposable design cycles by making iteration cheaper?** The paper reports the study's own students were sceptical — do not resolve the debate for them; let the group reach its own position in writing, same as the II.5 unit's authorship debate.

### What "iterating between representations" means, concretely

1. **The physical pass** — a maquette, muslin study, or hand-built volume study, however small.
2. **The digital pass** — a digital 3D representation of the same volume idea.
3. **The correction** — at least one documented instance where one representation revealed something the other missed, and what changed as a result.
4. **The declaration** — which tool(s) were used, dated, labelled as platform notes, not research citations.

## B2 · Studio — talleres 2 h + investigaciones y proyectos 2 h

**Deliverable:** one small garment-adjacent volume study built by iterating between a physical maquette/muslin pass and a digital 3D representation — mirroring Coats's hybrid workflow structurally, at CD I's introductory scale. This is not a finished garment and does not require CLO3D specifically; any available 3D representation tool may stand in for the digital pass, labelled as such.

**Definition of done:**
- A piece ID and a process folder documenting at least one physical→digital (or digital→physical) iteration.
- A one-paragraph reflection on what each representation revealed that the other didn't — the same evidence structure named in B1.

**Evidence this deliverable maps to:** **Investigaciones y proyectos (20%)** directly; **Cuaderno de prácticas de laboratorio/taller (10%)** if the process folder is submitted with the reflection.

Tool docs referenced (any 3D garment/volume tool) are dated platform notes, not research citations.

## B3 · Resolución de problemas — 1 h · individual, decontextualised

1. **Diagnostic.** Given a described hybrid workflow (which step used a physical pass, which used a digital pass), identify which step was probably analogue and which digital, and justify the likely reason for that choice.
2. Identify what a flat digital render of a garment cannot show about volume that a physical or rotating 3D view can — name the specific limitation, not a general "digital is different."
3. **No-tool, declared:** without any digital or physical tool, describe in writing how draping changes as the amount of volume in a piece increases — name at least one structural consequence (e.g. how ease or grain must adjust).
