## 🎯 Learning Objectives

- **Describe vector drawing as a relationship** (anchor points, curves, fills) rather than a grid of pixels.

- **Produce a vector illustration of a simple fashion silhouette** with **three visibly different alternatives** and a short selection rationale.
- **Distinguish a tool feature from a transferable drawing concept** and name what remains unvalidated for fashion-specific 2D tool sequences.

## Why this unit exists — drawing as externalised thinking

**Claim:** the clearest contemporary pedagogical model is studio-based, assignment-driven learning in which software technique is embedded in visual problems rather than taught only as isolated commands. Curcic's higher-education Digital Drawing study explicitly combines raster freehand drawing with line, volume, value, lighting, texture, colour, perspective and composition, alongside vector techniques; redesigned assignments matched to students' starting drawing ability produced improved outcomes (Curcic 2024).

Vector drawing in this course is therefore taught as **externalised visual thinking** — anchor points, curves, and revision visible before polish — not as button fluency. Fashion drawing conventions (proportion, croquis, flats) supply a transferable visual grammar whether the substrate is hand, raster, or vector (Abling 2023).

**What this supports, and what it does not.** Curcic (2024) supports assignment redesign, differentiated difficulty, and the separation of software proficiency from art fundamentals. It does **not** establish a validated *fashion-design-specific* 2D-tool teaching sequence; fashion pedagogy literature itself remains thin on named "digital drawing pedagogy" (Yu 2025). Tool documentation grounds *how* software works — label `[PLATFORM]`, never research.

**Practice anchor (field lens):** studio assignment pedagogy and visual fundamentals inside raster/vector workflows (Curcic 2024).

**Frontier signal (field lens):** fashion ideation is moving toward human–AI collaborative sketching and controllable generation. Treat any AI variant as input to judgement, not a substitute for drawing decisions — classroom evidence for GenAI sketching remains an open research gap.

## Critical perspective

The fashion manuals with which fashion is taught can themselves constitute an infrastructure of exclusion: systematic analyses find simultaneous race, gender, and body biases before the student arrives to produce their own work — audit who counts as the "normal" body, legitimate designer, and fashion subject before fixing briefs (Reddy-Best et al. 2018).

Tool fluency can reward prior access to hardware, software, and studio conventions rather than drawing judgement alone. Name one low-tech alternative or access barrier your workflow assumes, and which line qualities your rubric treats as "professional" by default.

### The debate prompt

**Does starting from vector-first (rather than freehand-raster-first) change what a beginner notices about garment silhouette?** Curcic frames heterogeneous prior freehand ability as an unresolved calibration problem; your studio evidence today is the only fashion-specific data in the room.

## B2 · Studio — talleres 1 h + investigaciones y proyectos 2 h

**Deliverable:** one vector illustration of a simple fashion silhouette (flat sketch or croqui), built from primitive shapes and paths, with **three visibly different alternatives**, a selection rationale, and a process note on which anchor-point decisions mattered most.

- Piece ID; process folder; the process note present and specific (not "I drew a dress").

## B3 · Resolución de problemas — 1 h · individual, decontextualised

1. **Diagnostic.** Given a vector path with a visibly wrong curve handle, identify what's wrong and what a correct handle would do.
2. Describe, in writing and with no tool open, how to construct a simple vector shape from primitives. **No AI — declared as such.**
3. Name why "I drew it in a raster app" would not satisfy this unit's own CONTENIDOS anchor.
