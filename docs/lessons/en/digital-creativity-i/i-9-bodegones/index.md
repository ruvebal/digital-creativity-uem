## Why this unit exists — and what it honestly does not have

**Claim:** a digital bodegón is where the field-of-practices frame (I.1), drawing/arrangement literacy (I.2), colour decisions (I.3), and effects discipline (I.4) converge into one deliberately balanced image — a synthesis exercise, not a new technique. This is craft discipline: **the evidence matrix's row for I.9 is `NONE`**, no source validates a teaching method for still-life composition, digital or otherwise. Tool documentation grounds *how* any given compositing tool works, labelled `[PLATFORM]`, never a citation.

### The debate prompt

**Is a digital bodegón judged by the same compositional rules as a painted or photographed one, or does the "digital" part change what balance means?** No source settles this; the group's own judgement, exercised on the studio piece, is the evidence in the room.
