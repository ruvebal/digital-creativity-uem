## Why this unit exists — a real citation, a narrow one

**Claim:** sourcing a compositional reference is not a neutral act of collection — *what a search surfaces* already carries a representational politics, before a student has chosen which image to reference at all.

This session's live re-check resolved a genuine evidence upgrade. Campinho, Cardim da Silva, Dantas Soares & Amparo-Santos (2025) studied Google-Images searches for "fat body" and "obese body," and found that "the images of the fat body show an expression of empowerment," while "fragmented bodies are linked to a cultural production that associates the fat, so-called obese, body with disease" — a pattern the authors argue perpetuates "influential discourses in the collective perception of bodies" (Campinho 2025, 2 — Ahmes coat `31a9a359`, node `29e0b3cf-bd30-5373-bfbe-d208de8c3a3b`).

**What this grounds, precisely, and what it does not.** This finding grounds the *ethics of the images students will encounter while sourcing* — a real demonstration that a search's own results carry a representational pattern before a student has made any creative choice. It does **not** ground a validated *teaching method for compositional-reference-sourcing* itself — no source in this vault studies how to teach mood-boarding, visual research, or reference-attribution practice as a classroom sequence. That gap stays declared, undiminished by the citation's own resolution: the paper's bibliographic metadata was fixed this session (it now resolves as `evaluator_safe=yes` where the matrix previously flagged it `[BIBLIO-GAP]`), but a metadata fix changes *whether a citation is usable*, not *what topic it actually studies*. Hold the two apart.

### The debate prompt

**If a mood-board search reproduces a biased pattern before you've chosen anything, whose responsibility is the bias — the search engine's, the archive's, or the student's for not noticing?** No source in this unit settles it; the point of naming Campinho's finding is that the question becomes unavoidable once you know to look for it.
