**A shared guide for Digital Creativity I–II and New Media.**
[Versión en español]({{ '/dignity/es/' | relative_url }}).

How can fashion communication invite desire while recognising the people
who appear in it, make it, and encounter it? Use this guide to investigate a
<strong class="dignity-emphasis">communication decision</strong>, defend your reading and make a revision that another person can
question. The subject of critique is the communication decision; nobody's
<strong class="dignity-emphasis">body or identity receives a score</strong>.

### 1. Equal worth, different lives

{% include dignity/scene.html number=1 %}

A person's <strong class="dignity-emphasis">worth does not rise with a campaign's production budget</strong>. The Universal Declaration begins from equal dignity and rights and names differences that must not cancel that equality [(United Nations 1948)](#ref-united-nations-1948). What does your image invite a viewer to value: the person's choices, the garment, or a hierarchy of bodies? Treat this as a starting principle for inquiry; a declaration alone does not settle whether a particular advertisement broke the law.

### 2. An image distributes agency

{% include dignity/scene.html number=2 %}

An <strong class="dignity-emphasis">empowering caption can sit on top of a familiar stereotype</strong>. A visual discourse study of 15 Nordic fast-fashion advertisements found complex portrayals that did not necessarily dislodge stereotypes [(Srivastava et al. 2022)](#ref-srivastava-2022). Who acts, looks, speaks, or is reduced to a role in the image you are analysing? These cues support a reading of representation; they do not establish a model's intentions, health, consent, or an effect on every audience.

### 3. Shared dignity does not erase history

{% include dignity/scene.html number=3 %}

An image can make a difference visible while <strong class="dignity-emphasis">stripping it of history</strong>. Browne's discussion of commodity spectacle shows why histories of racialisation belong in a visual analysis [(Browne 2015)](#ref-browne-2015). What history would you need to understand before using a person, garment or cultural practice as the object of a visual joke? The historical lens opens that question; it does not prove that every contemporary image repeats the same mechanism or that everyone reads it alike.

### 4. The person outside the frame

{% include dignity/scene.html number=4 %}

A respectful-looking poster cannot tell you whether the garment was made under <strong class="dignity-emphasis">fair conditions</strong>. The supplied practice bank's textile-company exercise connects production location, working conditions, materials and communication decisions [(Global Dignity España and Fundación AYO 2026)](#ref-global-dignity-2026). Which promise in your campaign would require evidence from outside the picture? This is a documented teaching practice, not proof that a better poster improves working conditions or that the exercise changes students' behaviour.

### 5. A viewer can also be a citizen

{% include dignity/scene.html number=5 %}

A call to action can offer something besides <strong class="dignity-emphasis">another purchase</strong>. The Sustainable Fashion Communication Playbook recommends reducing messages of overconsumption and recognising people's capacity to demand wider change as citizens [(United Nations Environment Programme 2023)](#ref-unep-2023). Could your image or media plan make room for repair, reuse, reflection or collective action? This is practitioner guidance; whether your proposed message changes consumption or production remains a separate question requiring evidence.

### 6. A critique must be answerable

{% include dignity/scene.html number=6 %}

A classroom critique loses its point if the teacher's verdict <strong class="dignity-emphasis">cannot be questioned</strong>. Freire describes problem-posing education through a shared object of inquiry rather than the transfer of ready-made answers [(Freire 2000)](#ref-freire-2000). What evidence would make you revise your judgement of the campaign, including a judgement you share with the teacher? This guide adapts that relation to fashion communication; it does not claim that the proposed activities have measured learning benefits.

## A method for reading a campaign

Start with one frame, caption, voiceover or placement decision. Record its
source and version before judging it. For video, write a timecode only after
viewing that version; a campaign title does not authenticate every repost.

| Question | Look for | Do not infer from appearance alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who can act and speak? | Gaze, action, framing, narration, relation to the garment | Intention, identity or a person's feelings |
| Who can refuse? | Documented participation, permission, approval or withdrawal arrangements | Meaningful consent merely because a person appears |
| Which differences matter? | Historical context, roles, omissions, distinct audience readings | That visible variety proves inclusion, or that equal worth means identical lives |
| Who supplies work and value? | Production, modelling, editing, posting, audience participation and credit | Fair pay or fair production from a respectful-looking image |
| What can a viewer check? | Evidence for a claim, qualifications and clear commercial purpose | That an attractive promise is substantiated |
| What follows publication? | Placement, distribution, responses, correction and opportunities to reply | That views, purchases or complaints measure dignity |

These are inquiry questions, not a validated dignity scale. Write **unknown**
when consent, pay, audience effects or a source version cannot be established.
Abidin's analysis of Instagram fashion advertorials is useful for asking who
contributes visibility labour; it does not describe every present platform
or every follower's motives. [(Abidin 2016)](#ref-abidin-2016)

Use three separate sentences in your analysis:

1. **Documented finding:** what the source or decision actually establishes.
2. **My interpretation:** the mechanism I think the evidence supports,
   including the strongest alternative reading.
3. **My proposal:** the decision I would change, and what that change cannot
   establish by itself.

For Spain, Article 3(a) of the General Advertising Law addresses advertising
that attacks personal dignity and specifies degrading forms of
representation. Read its exact wording and scope. The BOE consolidation was
checked on 20 October 2026 and shows a latest update of 1 March 2023;
consolidated text is informational. A classroom interpretation is not a
court decision, and the foreign cases below do not automatically determine
Spanish legal outcomes. [(España 1988, art. 3(a))](#ref-espana-1988)

## Six cases: different decisions, different evidence

These are supplemental communication cases, without a quality ranking. Some
involve adverse findings; one includes a revised finding. Investigate the
specific execution rather than label an entire brand or its audience.
The sources may describe sexualised imagery, racial stereotypes or body
presentation. You can work from the text-only record and a fictional brief.
The campaign visuals below are optional to view; the written records are
enough for each activity.

<section class="dignity-campaign-card" markdown="1">

### Boohoo: what is the image selling?

**Record:** a T-shirt product listing seen in November 2021; ASA ruling of
16 February 2022. The UK authority upheld an objection about objectification
and sexualisation in the particular images. **Image question:** how did pose
and framing direct attention in relation to the garment? **Media question:**
how does a product-search context affect the reading? **Limit:** this is a
finding about the execution, not a judgement about the model or all swimwear
imagery. [(Advertising Standards Authority 2022)](#ref-asa-boohoo-2022)

### Image lab — make a Dignity campaign image

**First action · 45–60 minutes.** Work through these steps to create an original campaign image. You may use photography, AI image generation, graphic design, drawing or collage.

1. **Make the image.** Show a fashion-related environment and a human situation in which dignity is celebrated. Start with your own idea, informed by the learning templates in <a href="https://globaldignity.org/teaching-dignity/teaching-tools/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Global Dignity’s Teaching Tools</a> and the campaigns analysed in this guide; do not reproduce a campaign asset.
2. **Write the campaign copy.** The Global Dignity templates include a claim. Pair your image with a short piece of copy that uses that claim, or rephrases it in your own words while preserving its meaning. Keep the claim accurate: do not add promises the image or your sources cannot support.
3. **Shape the message.** Centre people’s agency, relationships or care for the environment. Avoid body comparison and identity stereotypes. Ask what a viewer can learn about dignity from the image and what the image leaves out.
4. **Add the campaign tags.** Put <strong>#GlobalDignity</strong>, <strong>#GlobalDignityDay</strong> and <strong>#ThisIsDignity</strong> in a campaign caption or mock post. These are the hashtags listed in the <a href="https://globaldignity.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Toolkit_Global-Dignity-Day.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Global Dignity Day toolkit</a>. Public sharing is optional; if you share, get permission from everyone identifiable and follow the platform and course rules.
5. **Revise one visual choice.** Change the crop, gaze, pose, scale, context or retouching. Keep the original and explain how the revision changes the image’s reading. Credit sources and collaborators, obtain image permissions, and declare material AI assistance in line with the course guidance.
6. **Prepare your portfolio record.** Include the image and its copy, a before/after pair or two shot plans, a source and permissions record, an edit log, the strongest counter-reading, and a 120-word explanation of your revision and its limits. More clothing, a smiling face or greater visible variety is not automatically the better solution; explain your decision.

**If you publish the image:** credit Creative Campus at Universidad Europea, your professor, this course context, Global Dignity, and every partner or collaborator who helped make it. Name people and contributions accurately; ask permission before identifying or tagging anyone. Say that it is student work and does not imply endorsement by the named organisations. **Naming everyone who contributed is fair and recognises each person’s dignity.**

### Media lab — change the circulation

**Suggested duration: 45–60 minutes.** Use one guide case as a documentary
reference and a fictional brand brief for your production. Map advertiser,
publisher/platform, represented person, workers and audience. Change one
mechanism: narration, unsupported promise, pressure to buy, uncredited
participation request or placement. Produce a six-frame storyboard or a
three-post plan with captions and an accessible text alternative.

**Portfolio evidence:** original/revised flow; source record; two alternatives;
chosen version; one success criterion beyond engagement; and what remains
unknown. A more appealing slogan is not evidence of fair production.
No public posting is required.

## What makes a defensible submission?

| Dimension | Show this in your work |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence | A sceptical reader can find the source, version and documented finding |
| Agency and difference | You identify a specific mechanism without ranking people or assuming their experiences |
| Editorial craft | The formal or media change addresses that mechanism and you considered another option |
| Accountability | You preserve the process, state a serious counter-reading and identify what remains unknown |

You can disagree with the teacher's reading. Assessment of this work concerns
the quality of your evidence, reasoning and decisions, rather than agreement
with a prescribed moral verdict. Personal testimony, body comparison and
disclosure of identity are not required. You may pass on sensitive imagery
and use the documentary text or a fictional brief instead.

For a submitted portfolio entry, credit sources and collaborators and record
any material AI assistance with research, text, translation, imagery or
editing. “No AI tools used” is a valid declaration. Follow the
