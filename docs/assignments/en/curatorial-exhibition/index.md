# D3 · Curatorial exhibition proposal

**Digital Creativity I · case / problem · 15% of the course grade**

> **Submit through Campus Virtual.** Campus Virtual is the binding source for the exact submission time and file channel.

## The assignment in one sentence

Act as a curator and propose a coherent exhibition about **“What is fashion for you?”**: define the exhibition’s theme, research its context, select works from fashion and related creative practices, and communicate how visitors would experience the exhibition through a professional pitch.

You are not simply presenting your own work. You are constructing a point of view, making a selection, establishing relationships between works, and designing the conditions in which an audience will encounter them.

## Your curatorial problem

Develop an exhibition proposal that brings together pieces from at least **three different areas** of fashion or creative practice. Possible areas include fashion design, accessories, textile or material experimentation, photography, illustration, audiovisual work, animation, digital image-making, 3D or avatar work, web or interactive experiences, performance, sound, or spatial design.

Your exhibition must have:

- a specific **theme and curatorial question**, not only a broad topic such as “fashion” or “beauty”;
- a clear **selection policy** explaining why some works belong and others do not;
- a researched context and reliable credits for every selected work;
- a readable relationship between the works, the space, the visitor, and the chosen technologies;
- a visual proposal showing how the exhibition could be built, installed, or experienced.

The exhibition may be physical, digital, or hybrid. It may be realistic or speculative, but the proposal must make its practical and ethical conditions visible.

## Work through the creative process

Use the [Creativity Techniques repository’s introduction to the creative process](https://github.com/ruvebal/creativity-techniques-uem/tree/main/docs/lessons/en/creativity-techniques/u-1-introduction-creativity) as a working guide. The five stages are not a rigid recipe: in real creative work, **incubation, insight, and evaluation interrupt one another**. Your process package should show that movement rather than inventing a perfectly straight story after the fact.

1. **Preparation.** Gather references, identify the curatorial problem, map possible themes, and investigate the relevant fashion and creative fields.
2. **Incubation.** Leave space for associations and alternative readings. Record notes, images, questions, walks, conversations, or other activities that allowed the theme to develop without forcing an immediate answer.
3. **Insight / Eureka.** Identify the moment or combination that gave the exhibition its direction. A Eureka moment may be gradual; explain what suddenly became clear and what it made possible.
4. **Evaluation.** Test the theme, selection, sequence, and visitor experience. Ask peers or an intended audience what they understand first. Remove works that do not strengthen the argument.
5. **Elaboration and iteration.** Develop the final identity, spatial logic, labels, mock-ups, renders, and pitch. Show at least one substantial iteration and explain the decision behind it.

Opening and closing are both required: generate alternatives before selecting, then refine the chosen proposal through evidence and critique.

## What the pitch must communicate

Your presentation should allow an audience to understand the exhibition without needing your private explanation. Include:

- **Title and one-sentence premise.**
- **Curatorial statement** of approximately 250–400 words: what question does the exhibition ask, why does it matter, and how does the selection answer it?
- **Research file:** historical, cultural, social, technological, or material context; relevant references; and a bibliography or webography.
- **Selection of works:** approximately 8–15 pieces, with image, title, maker, date, discipline or medium, source/credit, and a short explanation of its curatorial role.
- **Exhibition structure:** sections, sequence, visitor journey, spatial relationships, and any interpretive text or labels.
- **Visual identity:** title treatment, typography, colour, graphic system, and examples of signage or communication.
- **Spatial proposal:** floor plan, diagram, storyboard, mock-ups, renders, or a combination that makes the installation legible. These may be made with 2D, 3D, collage, video, or other appropriate tools.
- **Access, care, and circulation:** audience, accessibility decisions, permissions or consent where relevant, material limits, and how digital or borrowed work would be credited and shown responsibly.
- **Process evidence:** preparation, incubation, Eureka/insight, rejected routes, feedback, and iterations.

The final pitch can be a slideshow, a designed PDF, a navigable website, or another format agreed with the professor. It must be visually coherent, technically reliable, and suitable for a live defence.

## What to submit

- Final curatorial pitch and exhibition proposal.
- Editable working file or accessible working link, when the chosen tool permits it.
- Curatorial statement and research bibliography/webography.
- Selection catalogue or work list with complete credits.
- At least one plan, mock-up, render, storyboard, or spatial visualisation.
- Process trail showing preparation, incubation, insight, evaluation, and iteration.
- Authorship, material, image-rights, and AI declaration. Follow the [AI usage declaration]({{ '/ai-declaration/' | relative_url }}); “no AI tools used” is a valid declaration.

## Submission checklist

- [ ] The exhibition has a specific title, theme, and curatorial question.
- [ ] At least three fashion or creative areas are represented.
- [ ] Every selected work has a maker, title, date where known, medium, and usable credit.
- [ ] Research supports the selection and is listed in a bibliography/webography.
- [ ] The visitor journey and spatial proposal are visible through plans, mock-ups, renders, or storyboards.
- [ ] The process trail includes preparation, incubation, insight/Eureka, evaluation, and iteration.
- [ ] Access, care, permissions, authorship, materials, and AI use are declared.
- [ ] The pitch opens correctly and is ready for live defence.
