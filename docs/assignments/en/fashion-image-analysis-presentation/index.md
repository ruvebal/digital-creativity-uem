## The assignment in one sentence

Analyse **one fashion image** with the eight-step Master Lecture method, then **present that analysis live** so classmates can follow your evidence, feel your critical stake, and ask hard questions.

The deliverable is not a written essay handed in alone. The deliverable is a **presentation file** you can run in class: PowerPoint / Keynote / Google Slides, Acrobat PDF as slides, online Figma (presentation mode), or another slideshow format approved by the professor. You speak with the slides — the slides do not speak for you.

## Why we are doing this

Fashion images organise desire, labour, and brand power. A private note in a notebook is useful; a **public analysis** trains the skill the profession actually needs: to transmit ideas clearly, defend them under time pressure, and keep **enthusiasm** without dropping critique.

Two presentations run per Analysis session. Each slot is **maximum 15 minutes including questions and feedback**. That limit is pedagogical, not bureaucratic: short time forces selection, hierarchy, and voice.

## What to analyse

Choose **one** fashion image you can show legally in class (campaign still, editorial page, lookbook frame, or feed post you are allowed to screen). Credit the source. Prefer an image you care about enough to argue for — enthusiasm without evidence fails; evidence without energy fails the communication criterion.

Follow the Master Lecture card:

1. Describe → 2. Inventory → 3. Forms → 4. Origin → 5. Context of production → 6. Meaning (hypothesis + evidence) → 7. Critical → 8. Circulation (language / medium / support).

**Without step 7 the defence fails.** Description alone is not analysis.

## What to submit (the presentation)

Submit **one** navigable slideshow package through Campus Virtual (format and date announced there), containing:

- **Title + image credit** on the opening slide (author/brand, year if known, where you found it).
- **Eight-step trail** — not eight walls of text: one claim per beat, image detail visible when you cite it.
- **Lens A** named explicitly at least once (language ≠ medium ≠ support).
- **Lens B** named explicitly at least once (production → reception → what could circulate next).
- **One critical sentence** you are willing to defend aloud.
- **Authorship / AI declaration** on a final or penultimate slide (tools used; “no AI” is valid).
- **Speaker-ready file**: fonts embedded or standard, media linked, opens offline if required.

Optional: a one-page PDF handout for peers — it does **not** replace the live slideshow.

## Live defence rules

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Format** | Two presentations per Analysis session |
| **Time** | **≤ 15 minutes total** — your talk + questions + feedback |
| **Suggested split** | ~8–10 min present · ~5–7 min Q&A / feedback |
| **Who speaks** | You. Reading dense slides aloud is a fail of public communication |
| **Audience duty** | Ask for evidence, not taste alone; give one usable feedback line |

Public communication matters here as much as analytic correctness. You are training to **transmit ideas and enthusiasm** — clarity, pacing, eye contact with the room (or camera), audible energy, and respect for the clock.

## Rubric

The rubric uses a 0–10 score for this defence. Institutional weighting follows the official guide categories announced on Campus Virtual (typically knowledge / oral or project evidence). This rubric does not rewrite those percentages; it states how *this* presentation is judged.

| Criterion | Weight | 9–10 · Excellent | 7–8.9 · Competent | 5–6.9 · Minimum pass | 0–4.9 · Insufficient |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Analytic completeness (8 steps + lenses)** | 25% | Full trail; Lens A and B explicit; critique is earned from earlier steps. | Almost full trail; lenses named; critique present. | Missing steps or lenses collapsed; critique thin. | Description-only, no critique, or method abandoned. |
| **Critical judgement & evidence** | 25% | Tests desire, labour, omission; quotes image detail as evidence; defends refusals. | Clear criteria; evidence usually tied to claims. | Claims without enough visual evidence. | Taste talk, slogan critique, or uncritical brand praise. |
| **Public communication & enthusiasm** | 25% | Ideas land; pacing fits 15′; audible commitment to the argument without hype replacing critique. | Clear, audible, mostly on time; some presence. | Understandable but flat, rushed, or over-read. | Inaudible, hostile to time, or slides replace speaking. |
| **Slideshow craft & authorship** | 15% | Hierarchy, credit, contrast, and file reliability serve the talk; declaration complete. | Adequate slides; minor craft issues; declaration present. | Cluttered, illegible, or fragile file. | Broken deck, missing credits/declaration, or image theft without source. |
| **Exchange quality (Q&A)** | 10% | Answers with evidence; uses feedback; stays inside the remaining time. | Answers relevantly; accepts one critique. | Defensive or vague under questions. | No engagement with questions or feedback. |
{: .portfolio-rubric}

Polish cannot rescue a missing critical step. Enthusiasm cannot replace evidence. Evidence whispered to the laptop fails public communication.
