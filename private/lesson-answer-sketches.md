# B3 answer sketches — Creación Digital I–II

Instructor-only working notes. This file is deliberately outside `docs/` and is not linked from student pages. It gives expected reasoning, not a single wording or a grading rubric.

## CD I

### I.1 · Digital images

- A file format is not a field of practice. The student should identify the image's role/context (editorial, advertising, documentary, etc.) and the boundary sentence that follows from it.
- A defensible no-AI answer names the image's artistic/commercial boundary and keeps the practice label separate from the file extension.

### I.2 · 2D drawing

- A wrong curve handle creates a kink, bulge, or unintended tangent; the handle should be adjusted to produce the intended continuous contour.
- A no-AI construction names primitives, anchor points, handles, fills/strokes, and a deliberate export; “I used an app” is not a drawing method.
- A raster-only result does not demonstrate the official vector-drawing anchor, even if the silhouette looks good.

### I.3 · Colour and bitmaps

- The student should connect low bit depth to banding/posterisation and a limited palette to loss of colour nuance; the answer must name the visible artefact, not just “lower quality.”
- A no-AI response compares palette range, tonal transitions, and the trade-off between file/control constraints and visual intent.
- Correction restores or compensates for capture/display conditions; change intentionally alters the depicted colour and needs disclosure when the context makes that material.

### I.4 · Effects

- The missing item is the preserved original/before state plus a record of the transformation.
- A disclosure names the effect and its scope, e.g. “contrast and grain applied to the background only”; “edited” is too vague.
- An effect is disclosed stylisation; manipulation becomes the ethical problem when a material change is hidden or misrepresented.

### I.5 · Three-dimensional form

- The answer should reason from projection/pattern to implied volume, then identify a mismatch between the 2D read and the render.
- A strong no-AI response describes a flat piece becoming a rounded volume through seams, darts, folds, or controlled bending before naming software.
- The expected distinction is spatial understanding versus successful package operation.

### I.6 · Volume

- The process must show the analogue maquette/muslin and digital pass as two representations of the same volume, with a named divergence or confirmation.
- A valid sustainability answer separates reduced physical sampling from the broader environmental effects of making iteration cheaper.
- The key diagnosis is whether the student can explain volume decisions without treating CLO3D as the explanation.

### I.7 · Fashion references

- A reference board without attribution and reflection lacks source traceability and a sentence about the representational pattern it repeats.
- “Informs” means the student transforms a formal idea into a new decision; copying means the source's distinctive expression is reproduced without a meaningful transformation or credit.
- The no-AI answer should still name search/browsing choices and the boundary between reference, appropriation, and plagiarism.

### I.8 · Animation

- No separate B3 is allocated (0 h resolution); the process note is the individual evidence channel by design.
- The note should identify selected stills/keyframes, timing, loop logic, and one reason each retained frame matters. It must not claim a hidden problem set exists.

### I.9 · Digital still life

- “Too heavy” should be diagnosed through hierarchy, scale, contrast, density, or figure/ground; the correction must name what is removed, lightened, resized, or repositioned.
- A no-AI balance note explains the decision sequence, not only the final arrangement.
- The student should point to real decisions inherited from I.1–I.4, not merely list those unit names.

## CD II

### II.1 · Fashion retouching

- The missing requirement is a before/after pair and a precise disclosure of the material change, especially where body proportions or garment appearance changed.
- A no-AI declaration states the tool/process and human verification, or explicitly says none was used.
- “Better engagement” is an unsupported outcome claim unless evidence is supplied; advanced retouching craft and audience response must remain separate.

### II.2 · Avatars

- A narrow default body type is a representational decision/problem, not a neutral technical default; the correction is to state intent and revise or justify the choices.
- The identity-intent note names body, skin, styling, and purpose, plus the human decision that controlled the output.
- Avatar creation represents an identity; VTON simulates garment fit on a body. The latter is outside this unit.

### II.3 · Digital fashion experiences

- Kim’s comparison supports a prediction about stimulus type, not a guarantee that 360° is universally better. The student must name the audience goal and access trade-off.
- A good staging rationale specifies where observation, reflection, multiple views, or close-ups occur and why.
- The C1/C2 coat is not cited because its citation gate is unsafe; a discovery hit is not evidence.

### II.4 · Video editing

- The order is storyboard/brief → capture or footage selection → edit → captions/audio/accessibility check → export. Autoplay with sound and missing captions fail the last pass.
- A no-AI storyboard can be written from the three-shot description with shot purpose, duration, transition, and sound/caption decision.
- No research citation is not an omission here: the matrix is NONE for fashion-video-editing pedagogy, so the page labels its guidance as craft.

### II.5 · Web and portfolio

- The broken image needs meaningful `alt` text; the diagnosis should also notice whether the surrounding content gives the piece context and credit.
- An honest AI declaration names the generator, contribution, step, changes, and verification; self-credit alone does not erase the generator's contribution.
- A no-AI answer can truthfully say the sketch was human-made, then disclose the generator's colour-variation role and the manual selection/refinement.

### II.6 · Holograms and AR

- “Because it is impressive” is not a medium-choice rationale. The answer needs audience, intended response, access/hardware cost, and a flatter alternative.
- A valid rationale can be written without AI and must explain why the medium serves the piece rather than adding novelty.
- Kim’s node is reused only as a screened-versus-immersive stimulus parallel; it is not independent evidence for AR or hologram pedagogy.
